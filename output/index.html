Russia threatens NATO with nuclear arsenal over Baltic enclave Kaliningrad

Moscow warned NATO it would deploy nuclear weapons if the alliance attempts to cut off Kaliningrad, its Baltic exclave bordered by Poland and Lithuania, prompting NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte to reject the threats as unhelpful.

Russia sent a diplomatic document to NATO member states claiming the alliance is preparing an air and naval blockade of Kaliningrad in the Baltic Sea, warning it would respond with its entire nuclear arsenal if the territory comes under attack. The Russian enclave, bordered by Poland and Lithuania, serves as a major military outpost housing Russia's Baltic fleet and nuclear-ready Iskander missile systems.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte rejected the threats at Euronews' Defence and Space Summit on Wednesday, calling them "absolutely not called for and not helpful." Rutte stated that NATO is a defensive alliance and dismissed immediate danger to member states. "We will defend every inch of NATO territory," he said, adding that "if anything happens, we are ready to counter it." Rutte also noted that "Putin knows that he can never win" in direct escalation with NATO.

Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev, speaking at the same summit, called for European dialogue with Russia to avert nuclear conflict, warning that Russia might resort to nuclear weapons if weakened or cornered. "We cannot close our eyes to the fact that there are nuclear weapons on our continent," Radev said. The exchange occurs more than four and a half years into Russia's full-scale war in Ukraine, as tensions increasingly spread to neighbouring states. Poland has heightened its air defence posture following alleged airspace incursions in recent weeks, while Danish authorities reported a Russian warship fired two flares near a Danish military helicopter over the Baltic Sea two weeks ago.
