Yankees activate Giancarlo Stanton for Wild Card Series after months sidelined

The New York Yankees activated Giancarlo Stanton from the injured list for their Wild Card Series against the Boston Red Sox, marking his return after calf injuries kept him sidelined since late April.

Giancarlo Stanton has not played since April 24, when a calf strain began what became a two-injury ordeal. He suffered his initial calf injury in April, then injured his other calf while attempting to return. After weeks of speculation about whether his season had ended, the Yankees activated him Tuesday, according to MLB Network's Jon Morosi.

Stanton's return matters for October baseball. Home runs carry extra weight in postseason play, and the Yankees get one of baseball's best home run hitters back, even if limited. Manager Aaron Boone said Stanton would likely be used primarily as a pinch hitter off the bench rather than in a starting role.

On Monday, the day before the roster decision, Stanton took five at-bats in live batting practice at an empty Yankee Stadium against pitchers Ryan Weathers, Elmer Rodríguez, and prospect Kyle Carr. He walked once, struck out twice, and hit two ground balls. Boone watched the entire session and said beforehand it would be important for determining the final roster call. The slugger showed no visible discomfort during the workout, running curves in the outfield and taking leads off first base, though he was not going full-speed, a purposeful approach to avoid further injury.

Stanton does not typically use rehab assignments after injuries, preferring instead to find his timing in the cage. This approach may help him contribute immediately for the Yankees, as his bat could be ready without a lengthy minor league stint.

Judge ruled out for Wild Card Series against Red Sox with calf strain

Yankees slugger Aaron Judge will miss the entire Wild Card Series against Boston after being sidelined since September 16 with a calf injury, though designated hitter Giancarlo Stanton is available.

Aaron Judge, the back-to-back American League MVP, will not play in the Yankees' Wild Card Series against the Red Sox despite showing signs of improvement in recent days. The 34-year-old has been on the injured list since September 16 with a calf strain and will miss at least the first round of the playoffs entirely.

Judge's absence significantly weakens the Yankees' lineup heading into the postseason. The team ranked 11th in MLB in OPS during the regular season and 9th in runs scored, numbers that improved considerably when Judge was healthy. The Yankees were 41-25 when Judge started and 52-43 without him. Beyond Ben Rice, the Yankees lack depth in the batting order to compensate for losing their slugger.

On Monday, Yankees manager Aaron Boone told reporters that Judge had been "making progress" after taking batting practice on Sunday, and Judge himself said he felt great running on an anti-gravity treadmill. Despite these optimistic signals and plans to potentially run outside, the team decided to leave him off the roster. One positive development for the Yankees: designated hitter Giancarlo Stanton will be available after missing time with his own calf strain. Stanton has been the active career home run leader with 456 across 17 seasons and has hit .254 with 18 home runs in 48 career playoff games.

The Wild Card Series begins Tuesday night with Game 1 in the Bronx, a best-of-three matchup where the Yankees will start Cam Schlittler against Red Sox starter Payton Tolle.

Kevin Durant expresses happiness with Rockets, hints at long-term future

Houston Rockets GM Rafael Stone says Kevin Durant told the team at the end of the 2025-26 season that he is happy and wants to remain with the franchise before his 2027-28 player option.

The 37-year-old's commitment matters because Durant has a player option for 2027-28, meaning his long-term future has been uncertain. Stone's optimism signals the Rockets may be able to retain their star forward beyond the upcoming season and build a sustained contender in the Western Conference.

Stone emphasized Durant's fit with Houston's culture, saying "His fit's been really good. It feels like he's really happy to be here, he's been a good teammate. He just wants to hoop, which fits great with our culture." With Fred VanVleet returning alongside Alperen Şengün, Amen Thompson and Jabari Smith Jr., the Rockets are positioned to contend again if Durant remains healthy.

MLB Wild Card Series kicks off with four games on Tuesday

The MLB playoffs begin Tuesday, September 29, with all four Wild Card Series matchups getting underway, including Phillies vs. Braves at 2:00 PM ET, White Sox vs. Astros at 5:00 PM ET, Red Sox vs. Yankees at 8:00 PM ET, and Cubs vs. Padres at 10:00 PM ET.

Four Wild Card Series games tip off Tuesday as October baseball arrives early in the season. The slate begins with Phillies vs. Braves at 2:00 PM ET and concludes with Cubs vs. Padres at 10:00 PM ET, with White Sox vs. Astros and Red Sox vs. Yankees filling the afternoon and evening windows.

The postseason debut carries high stakes for teams seeking to advance. The White Sox face particular pressure: the vast majority of their hitters are getting their first taste of postseason play after several key contributors slumped down the stretch. Munetaka Murakami and Colson Montgomery posted two of the lowest batting averages in the Majors since August 1, despite their strong early-season performances. For the Astros, Yordan Alvarez enters as the American League MVP frontrunner, hitting .400 with four home runs and a 1.300 OPS across his last 11 games before the playoffs.

The Phillies will turn to Jesus Luzardo as their opener despite his limited recent work—he has thrown just one inning since September 7 due to injury. Luzardo owns a career-best 2.85 ERA and posted a complete-game shutout with nine strikeouts against the Braves in his last full outing. The Braves counter with Chris Sale, who at 36 years old posted a 2.16 ERA this season while averaging 96.2 mph on his fastball, his hardest velocity since his rookie year.

All four Wild Card Series will air on Peacock and NBCSN, with select games on NBC. Game 2 matchups follow on Wednesday, with Game 3s, if necessary, scheduled for Thursday.

Madagascar and Tanzania seek first points in AFCON 2027 qualifying after opening defeats

Madagascar and Tanzania face each other on Tuesday, September 29, in Group L of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers, both searching for their first points after losing their opening matches.

Both teams enter Matchday 2 without a single point, making the encounter in Morocco one of the most consequential of their campaigns so far. Madagascar lost 2-1 to Nigeria despite taking an early lead through Ehsan Kari in the 16th minute, while Tanzania suffered a 2-0 home defeat to Guinea-Bissau in Zanzibar. Tanzania's coach Miguel Ángel Gamondi acknowledged his team dominated possession but failed to convert chances into goals, challenging his players to improve their efficiency in the final third.

The stakes differ significantly between the two teams. Tanzania has already qualified for AFCON 2027 as one of three co-hosts alongside Kenya and Uganda, so the qualifiers serve primarily as competitive preparation. For Madagascar, however, the situation is urgent. Only the highest-ranked non-host nation in Group L will earn the group's additional qualifying spot, meaning Nigeria, Guinea-Bissau, and Madagascar must compete for one berth. After Matchday 1, Guinea-Bissau and Nigeria both held three points, widening the gap.

A second consecutive defeat would significantly damage Madagascar's qualification prospects in a six-match campaign. Tanzania's automatic qualification removes the pressure of results, allowing them to experiment and build form. The match kicks off at 4:00pm East Africa Time on Tuesday in Meknès, Morocco.
