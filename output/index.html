Magnitude 3.3 earthquake rattles Southern California coast near El Segundo

A magnitude 3.3 earthquake struck off the coast of L.A. County's South Bay on Sunday at 9:17 p.m.

Residents reported feeling a short but significant single jolt from Venice to Thousand Oaks as light fixtures rattled in Sawtelle. This tremor marks the fourth earthquake to hit the region in just four days, according to the U.S. Geological Survey. The event occurred at a depth of 6.2 miles and was rated a 3 on the Modified Mercalli Intensity Scale, indicating it was weak but noticeably felt by people indoors. No property damage or injuries have been reported following the incident.

For those in the region, these tremors serve as a reminder of California's active seismic landscape. The U.S. Geological Survey stated that the quake's epicenter was roughly two miles from El Segundo and three miles from Manhattan Beach. The activity follows other recent tremors, including a magnitude 3.9 quake in Commerce on Friday and a magnitude 3.3 quake near San Pedro on Saturday. A separate magnitude 3.2 earthquake also occurred early Monday morning off the coast of Newport Beach.

Geological data from the U.S. Geological Survey and UC Berkeley Seismological Laboratory indicates that the state remains under intense scrutiny regarding fault stress. In Northern California, a magnitude 3.0 earthquake was also recorded Sunday morning near the Mendocino Triple Junction, approximately 34 miles west of Petrolia. Researchers note that this complex area where tectonic plates converge frequently experiences seismic activity. Furthermore, a June study identified that tectonic stress along major sections of the San Andreas and San Jacinto faults has reached its highest levels in at least 1,000 years, keeping the region in a state of heightened seismic monitoring.
