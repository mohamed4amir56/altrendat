Broadcaster Miriam O’Callaghan Investigates Ireland’s Dramatic 30 Per Cent Birth Rate Plunge

Veteran presenter Miriam O’Callaghan leads a new two-part documentary examining the causes and future impact of Ireland’s sharp population decline.

In South Korea, where the fertility rate has hit 0.8 births per woman, Miriam O’Callaghan observed that more nappies are now sold for adults than for babies, with baby buggies increasingly used for dogs. This stark reality serves as a warning in her new two-part documentary, Ireland’s Population Crash, which investigates the shifting demographics and collapsing birth rates now transforming Ireland. The series highlights a 30 per cent drop in the national birth rate over the last decade, a trend sparking significant concern among economists and demographers regarding the future workforce and the sustainability of the State pension system.

For many young people, the choice to remain childless or limit family size is driven by harsh economic realities. O’Callaghan notes that the lack of affordable housing prevents people from feeling they can bring children into the world, with many unwilling to raise a family in a parent’s spare bedroom. Beyond financial pressures, the broadcaster argues that negative discourse on social media, which often frames parenthood primarily as a burden, is further influencing these decisions. Despite the challenges, she remains a staunch advocate for the joy of raising children, citing her own experience as a mother of eight as her greatest personal success.

Beyond her new documentary work, O’Callaghan remains a constant presence on RTÉ with her roles on Prime Time and her weekend radio show. She continues to reject suggestions of retirement, stating that she prefers to live life to the full and remains as busy as ever. Her latest project serves as both an analytical look at the changing Irish landscape and a broader inquiry into how a society functions when fewer children are born.
