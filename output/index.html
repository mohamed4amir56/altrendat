James Maddison strikes as Tottenham clinches first Premier League win of season

Tottenham Hotspur moved off the bottom of the Premier League table after beating Coventry City 3-1 at home.

James Maddison marked his first start in 18 months with a brilliant 20-yard right-foot finish in the 14th minute, setting the tone for a crucial victory. The 29-year-old, who missed much of last season due to an anterior cruciate ligament injury and a subsequent shoulder fracture, celebrated his opening goal against his former club with his trademark imaginary dart throwing motion. This victory is a significant turning point for Tottenham, who had averaged less than a point per game since his last top-flight goal in February 2025. According to the BBC, the result provides a much-needed lift for manager Roberto de Zerbi, who had employed various strategies from team bonding dinners to a sunshine break in Marbella to spark his struggling side. Before this win, Spurs had won just four league matches throughout 2026. Despite the result, the team remains cautious as they have secured only six wins in their last 37 home league games. Coventry manager expressed pride in his side's personality and competitive spirit throughout the match, noting that his team pushed for an equalizer even when trailing 2-1. He maintained that the squad would not be defined by this away defeat as they prepare for a challenging match against Fulham. The match marked the first time Tottenham scored three or more goals in a home league fixture since the opening day of the previous season. The team now looks to maintain this newfound consistency as they aim to build on the performance delivered against Coventry.

Kings of Leon Storm Australia and New Zealand for 2027 Tour Dates

Kings of Leon return to Australia and New Zealand in February and March 2027 to support their upcoming 10th studio album.

Fans seeking tickets for the 2027 Australian run must prepare for specific windows, as the band limits purchases to 10 tickets per show during both presale and general release. The tour kicks off on February 28 at Sydney’s Centennial Park, followed by Brisbane Showgrounds on March 3, and concludes at Flemington in Melbourne on March 5, with Ocean Alley joining as special guests on all dates.

Following the November 6 release of their 10th album, O My Beloved, this tour serves as the band's latest live outing since their 2024 record, Can We Please Have Fun. For concertgoers, securing access requires careful attention to time zones, as all presale and general sale timings are set to the local time of the venue rather than the buyer's home location. Queensland’s lack of daylight saving time, for instance, results in staggered sale windows compared to Melbourne and Sydney.

According to Ticketmaster and Beat Magazine, multiple presale opportunities are available to eligible fans. Those who pre-order the new album through the official store by 4:00 pm AEDT on October 21 gain access to the artist presale. Additional windows are reserved for Mastercard holders and Vodafone customers, both opening at 12:00 pm local time on October 22. A Live Nation presale follows on October 23, while the general public sale commences at 1:00 pm local time on October 27.

All tickets for these performances are mobile-only, meaning attendees will use their phones as entry credentials via the Ticketmaster app. Accessible booking requests can be managed through the Ticketmaster website or by calling the dedicated help line at 1300 446 925 between 9:00 am and 5:00 pm AEDT.

Record-breaking El Niño intensifies with wildfire destruction and multiple active hurricanes

The strongest El Niño on record continues to intensify, causing catastrophic wildfires in Indonesia and shifting jet stream patterns for the upcoming U.S. winter.

Since January, wildfires across Indonesia have scorched 895,657 hectares of land, an area nearly six times the size of Greater London. This destruction is more than double the land area burned during the previous year, as the country faces its worst wildfire season since 2015 due to record-breaking heat and drought conditions attributed to El Niño.

These extreme weather events carry significant consequences for human health and infrastructure. In Indonesia, toxic haze from the fires has triggered school closures and sent 175,000 people to hospitals with respiratory illnesses, including more than 40,000 toddlers suffering from conditions ranging from coughs to pneumonia. Meanwhile, in the United States, the upcoming winter season brings a different set of risks, as the weather phenomenon is expected to shift the jet stream, creating an increased potential for stormier conditions, flash floods, and mudslides across the southern regions.

According to the BBC, El Niño has fueled rapid storm intensification in the eastern Pacific, where 22 named storms and 12 hurricanes have already developed, exceeding the typical seasonal average with two months remaining. The phenomenon has produced wind gusts reaching 180 mph. In contrast, the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) reports that the phenomenon will influence the U.S. winter from December 2026 to February 2027, bringing warmer-than-average temperatures to the North and wetter conditions to the South, Florida, and the Eastern Seaboard.

Looking ahead, the World Meteorological Agency projects that this El Niño will continue to strengthen and persist until at least 2027. NOAA plans to release the next update to its three-month seasonal outlook on November 19 to help communities monitor changing drought conditions and potential winter weather risks.
