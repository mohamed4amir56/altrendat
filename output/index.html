Dancing With the Stars Pro Blasts Fans for Manipulating Show Voting Results

Pro dancer Sharna Burgess claims fans are weaponizing the show's 20-vote system to eliminate top-scoring contestants rather than supporting their favorites.

The current voting structure allows viewers to cast up to 20 votes per couple—10 online and 10 via SMS—a system Sharna Burgess argues is being used to target and purge contestants with professional dance backgrounds. Burgess, who returned this season after a five-year hiatus, publicly criticized this shift in viewer behavior on November 20, stating it is disappointing to see the system used to vote people off instead of highlighting personal favorites.

This trend has created a volatile environment for competitors, where earning perfect scores from judges is no longer a guarantee of safety. The eliminations of leaderboard-topping pairs Harry Shum Jr. with Jenna Johnson and Jenna Dewan with Val Chmerkovskiy in back-to-back weeks have sparked significant controversy among the cast. Shum, who was eliminated on November 17 despite receiving perfect marks for his performances, noted that he feels the social media discourse surrounding the show has become increasingly negative, even describing the atmosphere among the cast as trauma-bonded.

Burgess proposes that producers limit fans to a total of 10 votes per person to force more selective, positive engagement. She warns that the current trend of negative voting is antithetical to the show's foundations. Meanwhile, some eliminated stars have begun creating their own content in response to the snubs, including a viral TikTok video featuring Shum, Johnson, Chmerkovskiy, Dewan, Ezra Sosa, and Julia Stiles. This follows the precedent set last season by Mark Ballas and Whitney Leavitt, who produced a post-elimination freestyle dance that went on to earn a 2026 World Choreography Award.
