15cm Snow Warning Triggers Travel Disruptions Across Northern England And Southern Scotland

The Met Office has issued a yellow warning for snow in effect until 11:00 GMT on Tuesday as temperatures across the UK plummet.

The temperature dipped to a freezing -7.7C at Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands, marking the coldest night of the autumn so far. As this cold air settles over the region, a weather front moving north is encountering these low temperatures, triggering a transition from rain to snow across hills in northern England and southern Scotland. The Met Office projects that between 5cm and 15cm of snow could accumulate on high ground above 300-400m elevation. Even at lower elevations near 100m, residents may see up to 5cm of snowfall, though meteorologists expect this to be short-lived and melt quickly by mid-morning. The impact of this weather front extends beyond accumulation, as thick, wet snow flakes risk sticking to infrastructure, creating potential hazards. Coupled with wind gusts reaching 40-50mph, this combination threatens power lines and tree branches, heightening the risk of localized power outages and disruptions to telecommunications. Commuters should prepare for potential delays, as the Met Office warns that major transit arteries, including the A74(M), A66, and M62, may face significant travel disruption, alongside longer journey times for rail services. While the highest mountains and hills in Scotland and northern England are expected to contend with wintry conditions through the afternoon, the rest of the UK will experience a shift in weather. By Wednesday, high pressure is forecast to move in, bringing clearer and sunnier skies to replace the current rainy, cold spell that has impacted Northern Ireland, England, and Wales. For now, however, a cold health alert issued by the UK Health Security Agency remains active for northern England to assist those managing the sudden drop in temperatures.

Luke Littler hits triple-bull checkout as Gerwyn Price benefits from referee error

Luke Littler and Gerwyn Price advanced to the Grand Slam of Darts knockout stage in Wolverhampton following a rare scoring blunder and a historic finish.

Luke Littler electrified the Aldersley venue by landing three consecutive bullseyes to produce a 150 checkout while his mother and girlfriend watched from the crowd. Although the 19-year-old subsequently lost his match to Niels Zonneveld 5-4, the three legs he secured were sufficient to seal his progression to the last 16. Remarkably, Littler was unaware he had already clinched his spot in the next round when he opted for the high-risk triple-bull finish during the eighth leg.

Controversy struck elsewhere in the tournament when fourth seed Gerwyn Price defeated Sebastian Bialecki 5-2. During the fourth leg, Price needed 135 but was incorrectly told by referee Charlie Corstorphine that 88 remained. After hitting treble 16 and double 20, the Welshman was declared the winner despite having actually scored 145, which would have bust his turn. The PDC confirmed the result stands under Darts Regulation Authority rules, as the error was not identified before the following leg commenced.

While Price and Littler secured their places in the next round, the tournament saw the elimination of ninth seed Stephen Bunting and 12th seed Wessel Nijman. Six of the 16 original seeds have been eliminated as the group stage moves toward its conclusion. For the knockout stages, Littler is set to face Jermaine Wattimena, who advanced from Group B following a competitive match against Alan Soutar. Organisers confirmed that despite the unusual nature of the scoring mistake, the regulation allowing the scoreboard to stand remains in effect for all matches held at the Grand Slam of Darts.
