Divers Finally Locate German U-672 Submarine Lost for 82 Years Off Devon

The U-672 submarine, which was scuttled by its crew in 1944, has been identified 20 miles off the coast of Salcombe.

A missing German World War Two submarine has been discovered sitting on the seabed 230ft below the surface, 82 years after it vanished. Divers from the Gasperados team successfully identified the vessel, known as U-672, after finding it roughly 20 miles off the coast of Salcombe, Devon. The discovery concludes a decades-long mystery caused by a clerical error in British navy logs, which originally recorded the wreck's position nearly 46 miles away from its actual resting place.

The identification serves as a poignant reminder of the human history behind sunken wartime wreckage. After being damaged by HMS Balfour in July 1944, the U-boat's commander made the critical decision to scuttle the vessel to save his 52 crew members. The sailors managed to surface the sub, board life rafts, and were rescued by British forces the following day, eventually living long lives after the war. For the families of those involved, the discovery of the physical wreck provides a final piece of closure to their relatives' wartime experiences.

The identification process relied on specific engineering features identified by the dive team, including the snorkel system and German Aphrodite radar decoy equipment. Dominic Robinson, a diver with the Gasperados, noted that identifying such vessels is increasingly rare as most U-boats in the English Channel have already been accounted for. The team was assisted by the family of the submarine’s commander, Ulf Lawaetz, who provided personal records that helped confirm the identity of the ship.

While the wreck was initially mistaken for a First World War sailing vessel, the deep-sea discovery in August confirmed its true origin. The successful mission has allowed researchers to clear one of the last remaining underwater mysteries in the region.
