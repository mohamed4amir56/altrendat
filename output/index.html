OpenAI Revenue Forecast Plummets By $20 Billion Prompting Massive Tech Stock Slide

OpenAI projected $50 billion in annual revenue instead of the previously anticipated $70 billion, triggering a 1.4% decline in the Nasdaq.

The massive $20 billion gap between OpenAI's previous $70 billion revenue projection and its current $50 billion forecast has sent shockwaves through the technology sector. This stark revision, based on sales data collected up to the end of September, effectively cooled market enthusiasm regarding the break-neck growth rate of artificial intelligence demand. Market analysts are now re-evaluating the long-term scaling potential of industry giants as the once-relentless optimism around AI revenue models faces its first major reality check.

For investors and market observers, this sudden downward revision is a critical signal that the AI investment bubble may be encountering significant headwinds. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite index tumbled 1.4% on Thursday following the announcement, marking its most severe one-day decline since mid-August. The market reaction signals deep investor concern regarding whether AI firms can sustain their previous growth forecasts as scrutiny over business practices and technological risks intensifies.

According to The Guardian, the revenue discrepancy resulted from investors adjusting OpenAI's metrics to better compare against rival firm Anthropic, which reported $65 billion in forecast revenue as of July. While Anthropic includes sales generated through major cloud partners like Amazon’s AWS and Google Cloud, OpenAI does not currently account for those specific channels in its figures. Meanwhile, CNBC reports that broader tech sentiment remained fragile as other major players also faced market corrections, with Nvidia falling 2.9%, Oracle dropping 5.5%, and Micron declining 4.8% on the same day.

Despite these financial hurdles, OpenAI remains in active discussions to secure $30 billion in new funding, which would push the firm's total valuation to approximately $1.4 trillion. CEO Sam Altman has confirmed the company will delay any potential stock market floatation until after this year, citing ongoing safety concerns related to the development of autonomous AI systems.

Singapore central region air quality hits highest PSI level of 2026

The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index reached 189 in the central region on Oct 9 as hazy conditions persist.

The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) soared to 189 in Singapore's central region at 3pm on Oct 9, marking the highest reading observed during the current haze season. This peak followed a steady climb throughout the day, having already surpassed the previous 2026 record of 167 set on Sept 29. By 2pm, the central area had already logged a 186 reading, contributing to a widespread deterioration of air quality across all five regions of the island.

For residents, the persistent haze means navigating air quality levels that have largely remained in the unhealthy range. Health risks associated with these pollutants require specific behavioral adjustments to limit exposure. The National Environment Agency (NEA) advises that healthy individuals should reduce prolonged or strenuous outdoor physical activities when the 24-hour PSI is between 101 and 200. Meanwhile, vulnerable groups, including children, pregnant women, the elderly, and those with chronic heart or lung conditions, are urged to minimize or completely avoid outdoor exertion.

According to The Straits Times, the hourly PM2.5 readings also reflect elevated pollution, with scores reaching 131 in the central region at 2pm. Other areas reported significant levels as well, including 144 in the west, 120 in the south, 159 in the east, and 130 in the north. The highest hourly PM2.5 reading for 2026 was recorded in the west at 2am on Oct 9, hitting 171. These PM2.5 metrics are used to gauge immediate air quality, with scores between 151 and 250 indicating high pollution levels that necessitate the avoidance of strenuous outdoor activity.
