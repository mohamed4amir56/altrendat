The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 3.4% on Thursday as tech and chip stocks plummeted, erasing post-election market rallies.

This broad sell-off carries significant implications for investors who had bet on a sustained post-election rally. By shedding the year's top artificial intelligence performers, the market signaled a major repricing of large-cap tech stocks, which recorded their worst day of losses since the pandemic-era sell-off. Within the tech sector, the PHLX Semiconductor index plummeted 12%, driven by steep declines in companies such as Nvidia and Micron. While investors navigated the equity sell-off, commodity markets reacted to geopolitical developments; Brent crude oil prices reached $98 per barrel amid uncertainty regarding future foreign policy engagements in the Middle East.

Looking ahead, market participants are turning their focus toward upcoming labor data. A report from Challenger, Gray & Christmas indicated that October layoff plans hit their lowest level since 2023, providing a critical baseline for the government employment report due this Friday. Additionally, corporate earnings remain a focus, with Duke Energy, Airbnb, and Lumentum scheduled to release their latest results.
