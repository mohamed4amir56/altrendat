120km/h Gales Batter Wellington As 20 Flights Are Cancelled Or Diverted

Severe gale winds across New Zealand have triggered orange weather warnings, causing flight disruptions and road closures through Tuesday morning.

Wind gusts reaching 120km/h have forced Wellington Airport to divert or cancel 20 arriving flights, with authorities warning that these numbers are likely to rise as severe conditions persist. The extreme gusts have already disrupted local travel, leading police to close a stretch of Oriental Parade after reports of cyclists falling and wing mirrors being ripped from parked vehicles. In the South Island, wind speeds in Canterbury’s high country and plains have surged to 130km/h, creating dangerous conditions for holidaymakers returning home from the long weekend.

The volatile weather system is putting significant strain on infrastructure and emergency services nationwide. Beyond aviation delays, firefighters are battling multiple scrub fires in regions including Central Otago, Southland, and the Central Plateau, where gale-force winds are hampering containment efforts. Emergency management offices warn that the strong winds may cause further power outages and advise residents to secure loose outdoor items like trampolines and charge essential electronic devices.

According to the NZ Herald and RNZ, orange strong wind warnings remain in effect for Wellington and the Wairarapa until 10am and 11am on Tuesday, respectively. Fire and Emergency crews have already responded to multiple incidents in the capital involving downed power lines and building debris, including falling panelling and glass. MetService meteorologists note that while the front moves toward the North Island, the West Coast ranges could see as much as 350mm of rainfall, complicating travel through the lower South Island.

Roading authorities are monitoring the situation closely, specifically noting that SH2 over the Remutaka Hill may face closure if wind speeds hit the 140km/h threshold. Residents in the affected regions are urged to avoid unnecessary travel and check current conditions before departing.

Chancellor Healey to Shield Nearly One Million Pensioners From Income Tax Hikes

Chancellor John Healey is set to introduce a new tax-free allowance to prevent pensioners from paying income tax after the state pension rises in April.

A projected 3.9% increase in the full, flat-rate state pension, which will rise by £489 to £13,036.40, would have pushed payments above the current £12,570 personal tax allowance. Chancellor John Healey intends to counteract this by creating a new tax-free threshold specifically for low-income pensioners, ensuring they do not face tax liability for relying solely on state income. Reports suggest this intervention will cost taxpayers approximately £200m annually by the end of the decade.

This measure serves to remove an administrative burden from retirees who otherwise would have been forced to file for small amounts of tax. While this policy protects those on lower incomes, pensioners with additional private income streams will remain subject to income tax. The allowance is expected to track future state pension increases throughout the current parliament.

Beyond this specific relief, the Chancellor has reportedly opted against reducing the maximum tax-free lump sum withdrawal available from pension pots. Speculation had suggested the government might lower the threshold from £268,275 to £100,000. Data from the Financial Conduct Authority highlights that total withdrawals from retirement pots surged to £91bn over the last two years, marking a 75% increase as individuals moved to access funds ahead of potential tax shifts.

Looking further ahead, the state pension triple lock is scheduled to conclude in 2030, according to Andy Burnham. Future increases will then shift to a new calculation method, though the Department for Work and Pensions indicates that the benefit will maintain at least 30% of its value relative to average earnings.

المصدر: أسعار الصرف الرسمية — وسعر السوق قد يختلف

كسر سعر الدولار الأمريكي حاجز الـ56 جنيهاً خلال منتصف تعاملات اليوم الإثنين 26 أكتوبر 2026، مسجلاً مستويات جديدة في البنوك المصرية، حيث وصل سعر البيع في البنك الأهلي المصري إلى 56.04 جنيه، بينما سجل في البنك التجاري الدولي 56.03 جنيه.

تعد هذه التغيرات في أسعار الصرف مهمة للمتعاملين والمهتمين بالقطاع المصرفي، حيث تعكس حركة الارتفاع في قيمة العملة الأجنبية مقابل الجنيه، مما يؤثر على المعاملات المالية اليومية والالتزامات المرتبطة بالعملة الصعبة في السوق المصرفي المصري.

ووفقاً لبيانات "اليوم السابع" و"معلومات مباشر"، فقد سجل الدولار في بنك مصر 55.84 جنيه للشراء و55.94 جنيه للبيع، بزيادة قدرها 7 قروش. وفي البنك الأهلي المصري، ارتفع السعر بنحو 17 قرشاً للشراء والبيع ليصل إلى 55.94 جنيه للشراء و56.04 جنيه للبيع. أما البنك التجاري الدولي (CIB)، فقد سجل 55.93 جنيه للشراء و56.03 جنيه للبيع، بزيادة بلغت 16 قرشاً.

كما سجل بنك البركة 55.95 جنيه للشراء و56.05 جنيه للبيع بزيادة 18 قرشاً، فيما وصل سعر الدولار في بنك قناة السويس إلى 55.90 جنيه للشراء و56.00 جنيه للبيع، بارتفاع 13 قرشاً. وسجل البنك المركزي المصري 55.75 جنيه للشراء و55.89 جنيه للبيع، في حين سجل بنك الإسكندرية 55.82 جنيه للشراء و55.92 جنيه للبيع. وتأتي هذه التحركات وسط متابعة مستمرة من المتعاملين لأسعار الصرف التي تشهد تبايناً طفيفاً بين البنوك خلال التعاملات اليومية، مع تأكيد المصادر أن سعر السوق قد يختلف عن الأسعار الرسمية المعلنة في بعض البنوك.
