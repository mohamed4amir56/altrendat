    DSWD Distributes P189 Million to Farmers Devastated by El Niño Drought Conditions

    The Department of Social Welfare and Development has released over P189 million in emergency cash and food packs to support drought-stricken farmers as of November 7.

    The scale of the crisis facing the agricultural sector is evident in the numbers: over 191,000 families across six regions have been directly impacted by the drought. While the national farm output dropped by 5 percent in the third quarter, the rice harvest alone suffered a 17.8 percent decline, leaving farmers in a precarious financial position. According to DSWD Assistant Secretary Irene Dumlao, some agricultural workers have halted planting entirely, fearing that another failed harvest would erase all investments in labor and seeds.

    This humanitarian assistance is a critical lifeline for families whose crops have been decimated. By prioritizing those identified by local governments as most severely affected, the agency aims to mitigate the immediate impact of the weather phenomenon. The support package combines emergency cash transfers with essential family food packs, providing basic sustenance to communities from the Cagayan Valley to the Bicol region.

    Data from the DSWD reveals that the agency has provided more than P141 million specifically to corn and rice farmers in Isabela and Cagayan. In Camarines Norte, more than P31 million was recently disbursed to 1,967 beneficiaries in Capalonga and 1,002 in Paracale. These figures are expected to climb as additional field reports are processed, particularly as national rice stocks have thinned by 8.3 percent and government buffers have plummeted by 38 percent.

    Further cash transfers are slated for additional towns in Camarines Norte throughout this week and next. Recipients in these upcoming rounds are scheduled to receive P10,000 each. As the weather bureau warns that as many as 68 provinces could face drought conditions by February, the DSWD continues to monitor the situation across the six affected regions.
