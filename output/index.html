Suspended Rassie Erasmus Risks Breach After Hosting Unauthorized Online Matchday Watchalong

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus held a livestream during the Ireland vs Springboks match while serving a six-match ban.

Thumping his table in frustration while watching Ireland secure a 20-17 victory, South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus spent the match in a Dublin hotel room rather than the Aviva Stadium. The 54-year-old, currently serving a six-match suspension handed down by World Rugby, hosted a public watchalong session that saw him appear on camera during both halves of the Nations Championship clash. While the suspension already prohibits him from matchday coaching or contact with team management, the event has drawn scrutiny from rugby's governing body regarding the terms of his ban. Sky Sports reports that officials are evaluating whether his actions constitute a breach of his restricted duties.

The potential fallout extends beyond World Rugby's disciplinary findings. According to The Guardian, Six Nations and Sanzaar executives have monitored the stream for copyright violations. While Erasmus is permitted to discuss the game, any broadcast of official match footage could trigger legal action from tournament organizers and rights holders like SuperSport, ITV, and Virgin Media. These organizations maintain strict anti-piracy measures and assigned staff to track the broadcast.

Erasmus received his suspension following public claims of match-fixing and manipulation after a previous defeat to France. The disciplinary panel ruled that he must refrain from stadium access, media duties, and all communication with players or staff on matchdays. Although he remained muted for most of the broadcast, Erasmus spoke during half-time to critique his team's performance, suggesting they needed to step up to avoid a narrow result. He justified the event on social media by stating he was not keen to watch the game alone. The governing body and tournament organizers continue to review the situation to determine if further sanctions or legal steps are warranted.

France Clashes With South Africa In Inaugural Nations Championship Twickenham Final

France and South Africa face off in the inaugural Nations Championship final at Allianz Stadium on November 29 following the conclusion of the pool stage.

South Africa secured a spot in the final despite suffering consecutive defeats, including a 17-20 loss to Ireland in Dublin. The Springboks finished at the top of the southern hemisphere table after New Zealand failed to earn bonus points during a loss to England at Twickenham. France claimed their place in the final by topping the northern hemisphere table following a 45-17 victory over Argentina in Paris.

The Nations Championship serves as a new joint venture between Six Nations and SANZAAR, aiming to provide more structure and consistent international matchups between northern and southern hemisphere teams. This alignment ensures high-profile fixtures outside of the World Cup window, with the inaugural tournament featuring twelve teams including Fiji and Japan.

According to Sky Sports and Rugby365, the finals weekend at Allianz Stadium will determine final tournament standings based on corresponding finishes from both hemispheres. Following the headline event between France and South Africa, England is scheduled to face New Zealand in a rematch, while Ireland takes on Australia. Other fixtures include Scotland versus Argentina, Wales against Japan, and Italy facing Fiji.

The tournament conclusion takes place across three days at the end of November. Following these matchups, international teams will conclude their involvement in this first edition of the competition.

Columbus Crew routs Inter Miami 3-0 in opening MLS playoff series game

Referee Allen Chapman defended the decision, claiming the force used was negligible and did not constitute violent conduct. Miami struggled throughout the evening, finishing the match with 10 men and failing to contain a clinical Columbus attack.

The decisive loss leaves Inter Miami on the brink of an early postseason exit. The club must now secure a victory in the next match to force a tie-breaking game, as their current deficit puts them in a precarious position within the best-of-three series. Miami coach Kily González acknowledged the performance was subpar, describing it as a test of character for his squad.

Gonzalo Tapia opened the scoring with an early penalty before adding his second goal later in the match at Nu Stadium. Taha Habroune also contributed to the tally, doubling the lead shortly after half-time. According to The Guardian, nothing went right for Miami as the team effectively gifted chances to their opponents.

Inter Miami faces a must-win scenario in the second game of the series. The teams are scheduled to meet again in Ohio next Friday to determine if the series continues to a final deciding match.
