          Denver Braces For Dramatic Weather Shift: Warm Sunday Gives Way To Rain And Snow

          Denver's warm Sunday respite in the 80s will shatter this week as a powerful system brings rain to the plains and snow above 12,000 feet elevation.

          Denver's final Sunday of September delivers one last blast of warmth before a major weather pattern reverses course, according to KDVR and Denver7 meteorologists. Temperatures will climb into the 80s across the metro area with increasing clouds as the system approaches from the west.

          The pleasant conditions won't last long. Beginning early this week, the Front Range faces a significant cooldown with "good chances for rain," Denver7 reported. The incoming system could deliver multiple rounds of precipitation, transforming the weather pattern that has dominated late September.

          The most dramatic element of the approaching system involves elevation-dependent precipitation. While lower elevations from Denver through the urban corridor should receive rain, snow becomes increasingly likely above 12,000 feet in the Rocky Mountains and elevated terrain, according to Denver7's forecast. This elevation threshold—typical for early autumn storms in Colorado—means ski country could see accumulation while city dwellers experience wet weather.

          KDVR emphasized that "changes next week may bring more rainfall," suggesting the system could produce multiple precipitation chances beyond the initial event. The timing remains crucial for water-starved Colorado, where autumn moisture typically helps replenish snowpack for spring runoff.

          Residents should prepare for sudden conditions shifts from today's comfortable mid-80s to significantly cooler, wetter weather by midweek. The contrast represents a classic Colorado autumn transition pattern where monsoon moisture and cooler jet streams collide.
