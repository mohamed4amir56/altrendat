Dublin Pizza Max shuttered after discovery of extensive active rodent infestation

Health officials ordered the immediate closure of Pizza Max on Talbot Street after finding rodent droppings in food preparation areas.

Rodent droppings were discovered directly on top of a mayonnaise container at Pizza Max, just one sign of an extensive active infestation that forced the restaurant to close its doors. The severity of the contamination, which included droppings found in the dough preparation area while food was being processed, prompted an immediate shutdown by the Food Safety Authority of Ireland. Inspectors noted that the infestation was not isolated to one corner but was present throughout the entire premise, compounded by gnawed packaging and multiple structural gaps in the building that allowed pests to enter.

This closure serves as a warning to patrons of the critical importance of hygiene standards, as the environment presented a clear danger to public health. The presence of pests in areas where dough and food contact materials are stored creates high risks for contamination that can have serious implications for customer safety.

The enforcement order against the 64 Talbot Street outlet, served on October 29th, follows a broader crackdown by the Food Safety Authority of Ireland and Health Service Executive officers across the region. According to official reports, investigators observed droppings on shelving units, floors, and inside food delivery bags. The restaurant remained under a closure order at the time of the latest update, preventing any further operations until officials confirm the premises are safe and the pest issue is resolved.

While this specific business remains closed, environmental health officers continue to monitor food premises for similar breaches of safety legislation. Owners are required to prove that all hazardous conditions have been eliminated before they are permitted to resume trading.
