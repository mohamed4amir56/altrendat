15cm Snow Warning Triggers Travel Disruptions Across Northern England And Southern Scotland

The Met Office has issued a yellow warning for snow in effect until 11:00 GMT on Tuesday as temperatures across the UK plummet.

The temperature dipped to a freezing -7.7C at Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands, marking the coldest night of the autumn so far. As this cold air settles over the region, a weather front moving north is encountering these low temperatures, triggering a transition from rain to snow across hills in northern England and southern Scotland. The Met Office projects that between 5cm and 15cm of snow could accumulate on high ground above 300-400m elevation. Even at lower elevations near 100m, residents may see up to 5cm of snowfall, though meteorologists expect this to be short-lived and melt quickly by mid-morning. The impact of this weather front extends beyond accumulation, as thick, wet snow flakes risk sticking to infrastructure, creating potential hazards. Coupled with wind gusts reaching 40-50mph, this combination threatens power lines and tree branches, heightening the risk of localized power outages and disruptions to telecommunications. Commuters should prepare for potential delays, as the Met Office warns that major transit arteries, including the A74(M), A66, and M62, may face significant travel disruption, alongside longer journey times for rail services. While the highest mountains and hills in Scotland and northern England are expected to contend with wintry conditions through the afternoon, the rest of the UK will experience a shift in weather. By Wednesday, high pressure is forecast to move in, bringing clearer and sunnier skies to replace the current rainy, cold spell that has impacted Northern Ireland, England, and Wales. For now, however, a cold health alert issued by the UK Health Security Agency remains active for northern England to assist those managing the sudden drop in temperatures.

United Airlines resolves technical meltdown after 100-minute system outage delays flights

A software issue affecting weight and balance calculations forced United Airlines to delay departing flights for one hour and 40 minutes on Monday.

Pilots reported the system failure to air traffic controllers by requesting permission to remain at airport gates while software issues prevented them from finalizing critical pre-flight weight and balance data. A recording from the ATC app captured one pilot in Houston choosing to wait until the connectivity was restored rather than attempting a departure without the required performance software calculations. The outage lasted for one hour and 40 minutes before technical teams resolved the error, according to statements from the airline. Flights already in the air and those operating under the United Express regional network remained unaffected throughout the event.

For passengers, the incident meant widespread travel disruptions, with FlightAware data reporting approximately 600 delayed United flights by Monday evening. The ability for pilots to calculate a plane's final weight—accounting for passengers, cargo, baggage, and fuel against runway and weather conditions—is a mandatory safety requirement before every departure. Because these calculations are tied to internal performance software, the inability to access that data effectively grounded departures until the system was back online.

United confirmed that while the issue caused significant delays, there is no evidence that the event was related to cybersecurity threats. The airline has not specified if the glitch was linked to the Unimatic system, which caused a similar outage in August 2025 that impacted more than 1,000 flights. While the primary issue was resolved on Monday, the airline has not provided further details regarding the specific software failure. The disruption followed a separate nationwide ground stop caused by a Federal Aviation Administration network malfunction just days earlier, as well as unrelated technical issues at several major U.S. banks reported the same day.

      وافق مجلس النواب المصري على تعديلات قانون الكهرباء لفرض غرامات تصل إلى مليون جنيه على المصنعين والمستوردين المخالفين لاشتراطات كفاءة الطاقة.

      تصل قيمة الغرامات الجديدة على المصنعين والمستوردين الذين يخالفون قواعد بطاقات كفاءة الطاقة إلى مليون جنيه كحد أقصى، مع إقرار مبدأ المصادرة الإلزامية للأجهزة والمعدات محل المخالفة في جميع الحالات. هذا التوجه التشريعي الذي أقره مجلس النواب يأتي في إطار تعديل المادة 76 من قانون الكهرباء رقم 87 لسنة 2015، بهدف إحكام الرقابة على سوق الأجهزة الكهربائية.

      تعد هذه الخطوة بالغة الأهمية للمستهلك المصري، حيث تستهدف ضمان حصوله على أجهزة موفرة للطاقة، مما سينعكس مستقبلاً على خفض معدلات الاستهلاك الشخصي داخل المنازل وتقليل تكلفة فاتورة الكهرباء الشهرية. وأكدت النائبة إيرين سعيد أن الهدف من هذه التعديلات هو دفع المصانع نحو الالتزام بمعايير كفاءة الاستخدام، مشيرة إلى أن هذه الإجراءات لا تستهدف المواطن بل تضع معايير صارمة على الإنتاج والاستيراد.

      شهدت جلسة مجلس النواب نقاشات حول العقوبات المقررة، حيث اقترح نواب مثل إيهاب منصور وإيرين سعيد جعل المصادرة جوازية للمحكمة بدلاً من كونها إلزامية، وإعادة النظر في حجم الغرامات لضمان عدم تأثيرها سلباً على الاستثمار. في المقابل، أوضحت رشا رمضان، مقررة اللجنة، أن مبدأ المصادرة ليس جديداً بل هو منصوص عليه في القانون الحالي منذ عام 2015، وأن التعديل الحالي ركز على رفع قيم الغرامات لتناسب الوضع الاقتصادي الراهن، وهو ما أيده ممثل وزارة الكهرباء مشدداً على ضرورة هذا التشديد لحماية الموارد.

      من المنتظر أن تؤدي هذه التعديلات إلى جعل شهادة كفاءة الأجهزة -التي كانت اختيارية في السابق- شرطاً إلزامياً لضمان دخول أي جهاز كهربائي إلى السوق المصري، وذلك في سياق جهود الدولة لترشيد استهلاك الكهرباء.
