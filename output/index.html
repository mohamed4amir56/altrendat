England mandates total phone ban as pupils face strict September 2027 rules

The government is strengthening guidance to ensure truly phone-free school days by prohibiting students from keeping mobile devices in their bags or pockets.

Starting in September 2027, pupils in England will no longer be permitted to keep mobile phones in their bags or pockets throughout the school day. While current policies often rely on 'not seen, not heard' rules, the updated government guidance aims to eliminate digital distractions entirely. Education Secretary Lucy Powell stated the objective is to ensure that schools remain environments for learning and friendship rather than scrolling, noting that phone access risks pulling children away from their studies and social interactions.

This shift to 'truly phone-free' schools is intended to foster a whole-school culture change. Proponents argue that restricting access improves focus in lessons and encourages face-to-face socialization. Karl Harrison, head teacher at Burnage Academy for Boys, reported that investing in systems like metal detectors and secure pouches has already resulted in higher grades and increased student interaction at his school.

Schools will retain flexibility in how they enforce these requirements, with potential methods including the use of locked storage, collection at the start of the day, or outright prohibitions on devices being brought to school sites. The Department for Education acknowledges that some institutions have already successfully implemented such measures.

However, leadership unions have raised concerns regarding the implementation of these more robust rules. Both the NAHT and the Association of School and College Leaders have warned that schools will struggle to manage these mandates without additional government funding. While the updated guidance offers clearer expectations for head teachers, union representatives emphasized that schools cannot simply make the logistical challenge of mobile phones disappear without the necessary financial support.

DSWD Distributes P189 Million to Farmers Devastated by El Niño Drought Conditions

The Department of Social Welfare and Development has released over P189 million in emergency cash and food packs to support drought-stricken farmers as of November 7.

The scale of the crisis facing the agricultural sector is evident in the numbers: over 191,000 families across six regions have been directly impacted by the drought. While the national farm output dropped by 5 percent in the third quarter, the rice harvest alone suffered a 17.8 percent decline, leaving farmers in a precarious financial position. According to DSWD Assistant Secretary Irene Dumlao, some agricultural workers have halted planting entirely, fearing that another failed harvest would erase all investments in labor and seeds.

This humanitarian assistance is a critical lifeline for families whose crops have been decimated. By prioritizing those identified by local governments as most severely affected, the agency aims to mitigate the immediate impact of the weather phenomenon. The support package combines emergency cash transfers with essential family food packs, providing basic sustenance to communities from the Cagayan Valley to the Bicol region.

Data from the DSWD reveals that the agency has provided more than P141 million specifically to corn and rice farmers in Isabela and Cagayan. In Camarines Norte, more than P31 million was recently disbursed to 1,967 beneficiaries in Capalonga and 1,002 in Paracale. These figures are expected to climb as additional field reports are processed, particularly as national rice stocks have thinned by 8.3 percent and government buffers have plummeted by 38 percent.

Further cash transfers are slated for additional towns in Camarines Norte throughout this week and next. Recipients in these upcoming rounds are scheduled to receive P10,000 each. As the weather bureau warns that as many as 68 provinces could face drought conditions by February, the DSWD continues to monitor the situation across the six affected regions.
