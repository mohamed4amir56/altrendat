Irish unions suspend nationwide public sector strike after securing major pay deal

Trade unions have called off a nationwide strike planned for November 12 after reaching a new 10.75% pay agreement with the government.

The breakthrough came at 3.30am at the Workplace Relations Commission, preventing a 24-hour stoppage that would have crippled schools, health services, and government offices during the upcoming European Political Community summit. With the strike suspended, over 300,000 workers who participated in earlier industrial action will now wait to review the full details of the proposal before casting their votes in a national ballot.

This deal carries a total cost of €5.2 billion to be spread across five national budgets. The agreement is designed to specifically protect those on the lowest incomes, who could see their total pay increase by as much as 18.3% over the lifetime of the contract. Public Expenditure Minister Jack Chambers described the settlement as a balanced and sustainable measure that prioritizes lower-paid staff while remaining within the government's fiscal parameters.

Under the terms reached, pay will rise by 10.75% over a period of two years and nine months, running from July 2026 to March 2029. The deal includes several phased adjustments, with the first significant pay increase scheduled for September 2026. The agreement also raises the threshold for lower-pay provisions from €50,000 to €60,000, ensuring a broader base of workers benefits from the new minimum payment protections.

National executives of the affiliated unions are scheduled to meet in the coming days to formally analyze the document. Following this assessment, they will provide a recommendation to their membership, setting the stage for a national ballot to confirm the final acceptance of the new terms.
