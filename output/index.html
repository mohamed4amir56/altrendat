Hackers crank European supermarket fridges to 50C in mass food supply attack

Hackers targeted supermarket refrigeration units across seven European countries and the UK on Monday by remotely increasing temperatures, causing potential food spoilage.

Thermostats in supermarket freezers across seven European countries were remotely surged to 50C (122F) during a widespread cyber-attack on Monday. While the full extent of the damage is still being assessed, the incident follows an early October attack in France that destroyed fresh and frozen food estimated at millions of euros in value.

The disruption of food supply chains poses a significant threat to consumer confidence and infrastructure security. Experts warn that the simplicity of these hacks—where attackers manipulate internet-connected refrigeration controls without needing complex malware—leaves supermarkets highly vulnerable. The National Cyber Security Centre in the UK confirmed it is aware of instances where vulnerable technology was exploited in the country and has been working to alert affected organizations.

Investigations remain ongoing, with officials currently unable to confirm the identity of the perpetrators. According to The Guardian, security experts suggest the culprits could range from state-based actors, such as those linked to Russia or China, to lone-wolf hackers testing their capabilities. The attacks exploited remote management systems that cyber-security researchers had previously identified as vulnerable due to outdated or unsecure configurations.

Dr. Richard Horne, chief executive of the NCSC, stated it is too early to attribute the attacks to a specific group. He emphasized that the events serve as a critical reminder for businesses to upgrade legacy technology that has not been kept secure or disconnected from the internet. As intelligence agencies monitor a broader campaign of potential sabotage in Europe, the vulnerability of everyday connected devices, including ovens and heating systems, remains a significant concern for the coming months.
