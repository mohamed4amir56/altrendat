Daniil Medvedev disqualified for hitting spectator during China Open semi-final against Djokovic

Daniil Medvedev was defaulted from his China Open semi-final match against Novak Djokovic on Monday after striking a spectator with a ball.

The ball struck a spectator directly in the face, leading to the disqualification of the Russian player just a few minutes later. Under tennis regulations, players are strictly barred from hitting a ball in a dangerous or reckless manner, or with negligent disregard for the consequences regarding the safety of officials, ball kids, and spectators. While Medvedev maintains that he is a calm individual off the court and that his tennis-related aggression fades quickly once he departs the venue, this disqualification marks the end of his run at the China Open. Despite his tendency for explosive behavior during matches, Medvedev remains a popular figure with the media for his humorous and reflective interviews after play concludes. British Airways jet plunges 28,000 feet before emergency landing at Heathrow

A Chicago-bound flight returned to London Heathrow on Monday after a technical issue forced a rapid descent over Ireland.

Oxygen masks dangled from the cabin ceiling as a Boeing 777-200ER descended from its cruising altitude to below 10,000 feet in a matter of minutes. The sudden drop occurred roughly an hour into the transatlantic journey, prompting pilots to issue a 7700 emergency squawk and abort the flight to Chicago’s O’Hare airport.

This incident highlights the standard emergency protocols required when an aircraft encounters unexpected technical difficulties at high altitude. The immediate descent ensures that cabin pressure is stabilized, protecting the safety of those on board, while the subsequent return to a major hub like Heathrow allows for a controlled resolution of the mechanical alert.

According to The Guardian and the Daily Star, the flight, identified as BA295, departed London Heathrow on Monday. While passengers reported uncertainty as the masks deployed, they noted that the descent itself did not feel overly rapid and praised the crew for their handling of the situation. After declaring the emergency over Ireland, the pilots spent several hours flying in a holding pattern over the Bristol Channel. This maneuver was necessary to burn off sufficient fuel to meet weight requirements for a safe landing. The aircraft eventually landed back at Heathrow just after 5pm.

British Airways confirmed that the aircraft landed safely and that customers disembarked normally. The airline is now coordinating efforts to assist passengers in rescheduling their travel plans to their intended destinations.
