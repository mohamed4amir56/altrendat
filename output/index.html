Prime Minister Mark Carney Targets Viable Canadian Auto Sector in USMCA Negotiations

Prime Minister Mark Carney reports progress in U.S. trade talks but refuses to sign any agreement that jeopardizes the future of Canada's automotive sector.

Prime Minister Mark Carney is standing firm in high-stakes negotiations with Washington, declaring that Canada will not accept a trade deal that fails to secure the long-term viability of the Canadian auto sector. Speaking from Manila, Carney emphasized that while progress is being made on the USMCA terms, the preservation of the industry remains non-negotiable for his government.

The future of the auto industry is critical for both nations, as the competitiveness of the American market is deeply integrated with Canadian manufacturing. Carney noted that the North American sector faces significant challenges due to the rapid shift toward electric vehicles and the need for major investments. By pushing for stability in the trading relationship, the Prime Minister argues he is protecting the interests of workers on both sides of the border.

According to The Globe and Mail and CityNews Ottawa, Carney recently held a long, substantive conversation with U.S. President Donald Trump, which included White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller and U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer. Carney described the auto sector discussions as one of the most challenging issues in the entire negotiation process. Despite the difficulty, he confirmed that Canada is currently making gains on terms that could leave a clear path to profitability for domestic production.

Beyond international trade, Carney also addressed domestic industry concerns, specifically criticizing the automaker Stellantis for its recent actions regarding the Brampton, Ontario, assembly plant. He stated that the government is dissatisfied with the company's approach, insisting that Stellantis must fully honor its obligations to Canadian workers and the state. Ottawa intends to closely monitor investments and trade conditions to ensure all industrial commitments are met as the regional transition toward electrification continues.
