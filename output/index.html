Marco Jansen hammers unbeaten 72 to rescue South Africa against Australia

Marco Jansen hit three sixes to reach lunch at 72 not out, propelling South Africa to 6-216 on day three of the second Test.

Marco Jansen entered the fray with South Africa reeling at 6-110, but he quickly shifted the momentum by striking three massive sixes to reach lunch unbeaten on 72 from just 71 balls. His counter-attacking display alongside Temba Bavuma, who holds steady at 76 not out, blunted the Australian attack and pushed the Proteas to 6-216 at St George's Park. The pair forged a vital 106-run partnership, transforming the team's prospects after a difficult start to the Test.

This resurgence is critical for South Africa as they attempt to recover from their previous 214-run defeat in Durban and fight for a result in this series. The match holds significant pressure, with the Proteas looking to stabilize their innings after Mitchell Starc claimed his 450th Test wicket earlier in the day to rattle the batting lineup.

According to The Canberra Times, Jansen’s approach was aggressive from the start, evidenced by his boundary-heavy scoring that saw him drive through the covers and pull Pat Cummins for six. The Australian bowlers struggled to maintain their initial dominance, particularly with seamer Josh Hazlewood unavailable for the first session and spinner Nathan Lyon left out of the rotation. The Australian effort was supported by Scott Boland, who maintained a figure of 4-57, and Mitchell Starc, who finished the morning with 2-62.

Play continues through the afternoon sessions as South Africa aims to build further on their current total to challenge Australia's commanding position.

Luxon and Hipkins Clash Over Health System Crisis in Final Leaders Debate

National leader Christopher Luxon and Labour leader Chris Hipkins traded blame for emergency department and staffing pressures in their third debate ahead of the November 7 election.

Christopher Luxon described the current state of New Zealand’s healthcare as incredibly challenging, while Chris Hipkins accused his opponent of being in denial about a system he characterized as being in a full-blown crisis. As the two leaders faced off in their final televised debate, the exchange centered on who bears the responsibility for the severe pressure currently facing the nation's hospitals and medical staff.

For voters, the confrontation highlights fundamentally different approaches to managing public health infrastructure. The debate serves as a critical final assessment for the electorate, forcing both party leaders to defend their records and proposed solutions for emergency care and nationwide staffing shortages just days before citizens head to the polls.

According to the New Zealand Herald, the discussion moved quickly from policy to direct confrontation after moderator Paddy Gower pressed Luxon on whether the system was in a crisis. Luxon asserted that his party inherited a difficult situation but focused his response on plans to invest in emergency department infrastructure. Hipkins countered by emphasizing the challenges of navigating a global pandemic and argued that the current government has failed to support doctors and nurses, claiming Luxon has spent his time as Prime Minister attacking them instead.

This encounter marks the conclusion of three debates held over the last fortnight. Previous exchanges saw similar friction, particularly concerning potential coalition partners and law enforcement policies. As the November 7 election approaches, both candidates are using these platforms to solidify their positions on the most urgent issues identified by the public.
