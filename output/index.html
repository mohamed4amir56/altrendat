Clocks spring forward in Australia as daylight saving starts this Sunday

Residents in five Australian states and territories must turn their clocks forward one hour at 2am this Sunday as daylight saving begins.

At 2am on Sunday, clocks across New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, Tasmania, and the Australian Capital Territory will skip forward to 3am. This shift marks the official start of daylight saving time across much of the eastern seaboard and South Australia, while Queensland, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory continue to observe standard time.

For workers, the transition presents specific scheduling challenges. The Fair Work Ombudsman advises that most employees are paid "by the clock," meaning an eight-hour shift during the night of the change may result in working only seven hours while receiving eight hours of pay. In border regions like Tweed Heads and Coolangatta, local businesses are bracing for potential confusion among clientele who may struggle with the time difference between neighbouring states, with some owners adjusting operating hours to accommodate the shift.

While the change offers an additional hour of afternoon sunlight, it effectively requires people to wake up an hour earlier. To help manage the adjustment, the New South Wales government recommends gradually shifting bedtimes by 15-minute increments in the days leading up to the change to help the body clock adapt. Research suggests that while some individuals may experience effects comparable to minor jet lag, there is no evidence of significant negative impacts on mental health.

The debate over the practice remains active, with Gold Coast Mayor Tom Tate recently calling for the adoption of daylight saving in southeast Queensland. Despite such regional discourse, the seasonal schedule change remains a fixture for millions of Australians until the clocks revert in April.

Government Selects Horoirangi Peak Site for Critical New South Island Radar

A new radar station will be installed near Nelson by mid-2027 to eliminate a significant weather forecasting blind spot in the South Island.

The site for the new weather radar has been confirmed near Horoirangi Peak, situated 630 meters above sea level and approximately 15 kilometers northeast of Nelson City. This installation addresses a long-standing coverage gap that forced the Nelson Tasman region to rely on distant equipment in Wellington that could not effectively monitor the northwest Tasman area.

This project carries a total funding commitment of up to $5 million, a measure fast-tracked by the government following severe storms and flooding that occurred last July. The area has faced persistent challenges, with South Island Minister James Meager noting that the region has declared five states of emergency since 2011. Improved forecasting capability is intended to provide emergency managers, councils, and local first responders with the necessary data to prepare for future extreme weather events.

MetService will manage the installation and ongoing operation of the facility, which will feature modern Doppler dual-polarisation technology. This system is designed to provide more accurate rainfall estimates and enhance overall weather monitoring capabilities. The selection of the Horoirangi Peak location followed a rigorous evaluation of approximately 10 potential sites across the eastern hills near Nelson, the central hills around Moutere, and areas reaching toward Motueka and Kahurangi National Park. The chosen site was prioritized for its optimal altitude and the support of local landowners willing to host the infrastructure.

Operations at the new site are expected to be fully functional by mid-2027. Once active, the radar will require an annual operating budget of approximately $260,000 to maintain its service to the community.
