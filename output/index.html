Magnitude 5.0 earthquake rattles California central coast near Pinnacles National Park

A 5.0 magnitude earthquake struck near Pinnacles National Park late Thursday, marking California's second-strongest seismic event of the year.

The 5.0 magnitude quake was part of a broader swarm of 81 smaller tremors recorded in the immediate vicinity of the national park. The epicenter was located north of the park, approximately 12.5 miles from Soledad and 13.5 miles from Gonzales, according to reports from Monterey County Weekly and The Mercury News. Ground movement from the event was felt as far west as the Monterey Peninsula. The earthquake is categorized as the second-strongest tremor to hit California so far this year.

For residents and local officials, the incident serves as a reminder of the region's active seismic nature. Despite the intensity felt in San Benito and South Monterey County, CalFire San Benito-Monterey spokesperson Josh Silvera confirmed that no calls for service were received. There have been no reported injuries or damage following the tremor, which occurred just before 11:00 p.m. on November 19.

Geological data from the U.S. Geological Survey indicates that while the main shock has passed, the area remains under watch for continued seismic activity. Experts currently estimate a 64-percent probability of aftershocks reaching a magnitude above 3.0 within the next seven days. Conversely, the likelihood of a subsequent earthquake exceeding a 5.0 magnitude remains very low, calculated at less than 1 percent. While the park is noted for its volcanic spires and canyons, operations at the national park remained largely unconfirmed by officials following the event.

As of the latest updates, there are no further alerts regarding the structural integrity of the area near the epicenter. Residents in the affected regions are encouraged to monitor USGS forecast updates throughout the upcoming week.
