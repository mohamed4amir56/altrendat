South Africa petrol prices set for major cuts in December 2026

Motorists can expect petrol prices to drop by up to R2.90 per litre starting in December 2026 due to international market shifts.

Projections from the Central Energy Fund indicate a significant downward shift for South African motorists, with 93 ULP/LRP and 95 ULP/LRP petrol prices slated to decrease by R2.65 and R2.90 per litre respectively. While 50ppm diesel is also expected to fall by 12 cents per litre, the market remains mixed as 500ppm diesel faces a projected increase of 28 cents per litre. These figures are driven by a decline in international petroleum prices and fluctuations in the Rand, which currently trades at R17.27 against the US Dollar with crude oil priced at $83 per barrel.

This relief at the pump arrives during a period of intense budgetary strain for lower-income households. Research from Field IQ indicates that nearly 40% of a minimum-wage income is already consumed by daily transport costs, with commuters often spending R2,016 monthly on taxi fares. Because transport costs represent such a large portion of individual earnings, experts warn that temporary fuel price reductions are unlikely to translate into increased retail spending. Consumers remain under pressure as they balance daily necessities against the high cost of transportation.

Additional adjustments to the fuel price structure continue to influence these outcomes, including a government-led hike in the slate levy implemented in early November 2026. Furthermore, existing fuel levy cuts of R3 for diesel and R1.50 for petrol remain active until 1 December 2026. Following this date, the levy relief will adjust to R1.50 for diesel and R1 for petrol, a change that will persist until 5 January 2027.

The Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources is expected to confirm the final official fuel prices as the December 2026 implementation date approaches.
