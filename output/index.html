Pacific Hurricanes Surge While Atlantic Stays Eerily Calm Thanks to El Niño

An exceptionally strong El Niño is fueling an unusual spike in Pacific hurricanes while suppressing Atlantic storm activity to historic lows this season.

The Pacific Ocean is experiencing a dramatic uptick in hurricane activity this year while the Atlantic basin remains unusually quiet, a stark reversal driven by one dominant climate pattern. According to NPR, the Pacific has churned with an exceptional number of storms during the 2026 season, while the Atlantic has failed to produce a single full-blown hurricane—a striking anomaly that has captured meteorologists' attention worldwide.

Scientists have pinpointed the culprit: an exceptionally strong El Niño event. This periodic warming of Pacific Ocean waters alters wind patterns and atmospheric conditions across ocean basins, creating ideal breeding grounds for tropical cyclones in the eastern Pacific while simultaneously suppressing hurricane formation in the Atlantic. The Weather Channel's hurricane season tracker is currently monitoring multiple active systems including Hurricanes Polo and Nolo, underscoring the Pacific's relentless storm production.

El Niño typically weakens Atlantic hurricane activity by increasing wind shear—the change in wind speed and direction with altitude—making it harder for storms to organize and intensify. Conversely, the same atmospheric shifts can reduce wind shear in the Pacific, allowing tropical depressions to escalate into major hurricanes more readily. This seasonal dynamic means Pacific coastal communities from Mexico to Hawaii face heightened risks, while Atlantic regions from Florida to the Caribbean enjoy reduced hurricane threats this cycle. Meteorologists stress that while El Niño provides a predictable framework, individual storm behavior remains difficult to forecast, and residents in all hurricane-prone areas should maintain preparedness.

According to TechPowerUp, developer BlackBearReloaded released ProsperoEden, an unofficial port based on the Eden emulator, which itself is forked from the now-defunct Yuzu project that Nintendo successfully shut down last year.

The technical achievement is significant: ProsperoEden adapts existing Switch emulation code to run as homebrew directly on PS5 hardware, eliminating the need for intermediate operating systems. TweakTown reports that some Switch titles are already hitting 60 FPS performance on the platform, demonstrating functional optimization despite the experimental status.

This development highlights the ongoing cat-and-mouse game between console manufacturers and the emulation community. Nintendo's legal action against Yuzu in 2024 forced the original project offline, but the open-source nature of emulation technology allows developers to adapt and repurpose the code across different hardware platforms. The ProsperoEden project specifically targets users who have jailbroken their PS5 consoles, sidestepping Sony's security measures to enable unauthorized software execution.

While the emulator represents a technical marvel for enthusiasts, it operates in legally ambiguous territory. Game preservation advocates argue emulation serves historical purposes, but console manufacturers view it as a direct threat to their ecosystem control and revenue streams. The emergence of ProsperoEden on PlayStation 5 demonstrates that emulation innovation continues despite industry crackdowns, pushing the boundaries of what modified consumer hardware can achieve.
