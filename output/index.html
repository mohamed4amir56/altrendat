Canada indefinitely blocks MAID access for patients with sole mental illness

The federal government will introduce legislation this fall to permanently exclude individuals suffering exclusively from mental disorders from medical assistance in dying.

The federal government's shift arrives after a special joint parliamentary committee reviewed testimony from more than three dozen witnesses regarding the controversial expansion. Justice Minister Sean Fraser noted that the decision reflects concerns about inadequate access to mental-health services in various communities and the lack of clinical consensus on whether mental illness can be deemed irremediable in every case.

This policy change significantly alters the landscape of Canada's medical assistance in dying regime, which has permitted access for other conditions since 2016. By declaring this position before an upcoming vote on a Conservative private member's bill, the government aims to finalize its stance on an issue that has been previously delayed twice due to concerns regarding the readiness of the national health care system.

According to CTV News, the government recently formalized its position following months of study. While closing the door on mental illness as a sole basis, Justice Minister Sean Fraser confirmed that upcoming legislation will also introduce measures to legally allow advance requests for patients facing future cognitive decline. This addition ensures those who anticipate losing their ability to consent will have a clear framework to request the procedure.

The government plans to table the formal legislation in Parliament this fall, effectively pre-empting the March 17 expiration of the existing law that currently bars access to the program for those with mental disorders.
