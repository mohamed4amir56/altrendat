Brooks Koepka and Justin Thomas headline $4 million Farmers Insurance Skins Game

Four PGA Tour stars will compete for a $4 million prize at Atlantic Fields Golf Club on Black Friday.

Four major champions and tour winners—Brooks Koepka, Wyndham Clark, Justin Thomas, and Jackson Koivun—are set to compete for a $4 million purse this Friday. The Farmers Insurance Skins Game returns for its second consecutive year, moving the action to the Atlantic Fields Golf Club in Hobe Sound, Florida. The event utilizes a classic format where each hole, or skin, carries a set dollar value, with tied holes resulting in a carry-over of the prize money. While traditional 72-hole tournaments demand intense focus, this made-for-TV competition allows the players to showcase their personalities in a more relaxed, conversational environment.

Fans gain unique access to the players through this format, as competitors are mic'd up throughout the day. This provides viewers with an opportunity to hear everything from lighthearted banter to the specific tactical discussions between golfers and their caddies. Wyndham Clark noted that he would welcome this increased level of transparency in standard tour events, comparing the potential experience to the style of production seen in NFL Films.

According to the PGA Tour, the event was originally meant to feature a press conference early in the week, but scheduling was shifted due to the extended conclusion of The RSM Classic. The delay provided a target for playful trash talk between the stars, with Koepka and Clark trading jokes about their absent competitor, Justin Thomas, and the inclusion of the rookie Koivun.

The competition will be broadcast on Prime Video throughout the day on Friday. Amazon will host the golf coverage as part of a larger sports slate, which includes an NFL game between the Broncos and the Steelers, followed by NBA matchups in the evening. Access to the stream will be available without requiring a subscription or Prime membership.

Detroit Lions owner Martha Firestone Ford dies at 101 years old

The longtime owner and chair emeritus of the Detroit Lions has passed away at her home surrounded by family.

Martha Firestone Ford lived to the age of 101, closing a chapter on a life that included six decades of ownership of the Detroit Lions. She first became involved with the franchise in 1963 alongside her husband, William Clay Ford, before assuming sole control of the team following his passing in 2014.

Her leadership provided stability for the franchise and helped the team secure two playoff appearances in 2014 and 2016. Beyond the field, her tenure included significant financial investments in Ford Field, which the organization credits as the foundation for the current stadium experience. She was also a prominent figure in the Detroit community, notably launching the Detroit Lions Inspire Change initiative in 2019 and serving as a longtime trustee of Henry Ford Health.

Following her transition to the role of chair emeritus in 2020, Mrs. Ford supported her four children as they took on leadership responsibilities within the family franchise. The Green Bay Packers, longtime rivals of the Lions, released a statement expressing their sadness at the news and extended their condolences to the Ford and Firestone families.

Mrs. Ford’s legacy is carried forward by her children, including Sheila Ford Hamp, who currently serves as principal owner and chair. The Ford family continues to oversee the franchise, maintaining the traditions and values established during the couple's long tenure.
