London Firms Charge £100,000 for Fraudulent UK Global Talent Visa Packages

An undercover BBC investigation revealed consultancy firms are creating fake art portfolios and fabricated credentials to help wealthy migrants secure UK visas.

Wealthy migrants are paying over £100,000 for a "fully inclusive" package that guarantees a Global Talent visa, with consultants offering to supply artwork produced in China, arrange fake media coverage, and even hire individuals to pose as the applicant at gallery events. One consultant in London claimed no client utilizing this high-cost service had ever been refused entry under the fast-track scheme, which allows applicants to bring their families and achieve permanent residency in as little as three years. The investigation identified that agencies are planting fake articles on news websites using the bylines of reputable journalists to deceive immigration officials.

This exploitation of the Global Talent visa system poses significant security and integrity risks for the Home Office, which designed the program in 2020 to attract the world's most promising scientists, researchers, and artists. By bypassing rigorous interviews, the system relies on documentation that investigators found is now being systematically fabricated, including forged mentoring awards and evidence of non-existent speaking engagements for tech-track applicants.

Following the BBC's undercover findings, the Home Office has begun revoking the visas of two Nigerian men who successfully posed as fine art photographers using deceptive materials. Officials have labeled the revelations as "extremely serious" and confirmed that iCareer Consultancy UK, the firm identified as offering the £100,000 package, is currently under active investigation.

While iCareer has denied selling visa services and claimed the consultant involved had left its employment, the investigation highlights a sophisticated network targeting wealthy nationals with promises of residency that require no English proficiency. The Home Office maintains it will not tolerate attempts to cheat the immigration system and continues to review the applications processed through these fraudulent channels.
