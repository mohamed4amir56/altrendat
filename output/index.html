Level 4 Weather Warning Triggers Emergency Rescue Alert Across Gauteng This Weekend

South African Weather Service issued a level 4 warning for severe storms, heavy rain, and strong winds affecting Gauteng and Mpumalanga.

Technical rescue teams under the Ekurhuleni Disaster and Emergency Management Services are on high alert to respond to flooding and infrastructure damage. The South African Weather Service forecast warns that the combination of heavy rains, hailstorms, and strong winds poses a significant risk to homes and communication lines throughout the region.

Residents must remain cautious as the storms threaten to disrupt power supplies and submerge low-lying roads and bridges. Spokesperson William Tladi explicitly warned the public not to attempt driving through floodwaters, as localized disruptions and potential damage to settlements are highly likely as the weather system moves over Gauteng and the south-western Mpumalanga Highveld.

Beyond Gauteng, the weather impact extends to KwaZulu-Natal, where a separate level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms is in effect for Friday. According to the South African Weather Service, areas such as uMlalazi, uMngeni, and KwaDukuza face specific risks including lightning, damaging winds, and localized flash flooding. Motorists in these municipalities are advised to exercise extreme caution when navigating roads prone to water accumulation.

Authorities expect these severe conditions to persist throughout the weekend. Residents are encouraged to monitor local updates and take necessary precautions to safeguard property and personal safety as the storms track across the affected provinces.

SASSA issues critical review warning ahead of December 2026 grant payments

The South African Social Security Agency confirms December 2026 payments begin on 2 December, while mandating that beneficiaries under review respond to notices to prevent payment suspension.

Beneficiaries must act immediately on official review notifications to avoid the suspension of their monthly support. SASSA mandates these reviews to verify that recipients continue to meet strict eligibility requirements and to curb the irregular or fraudulent payments that have historically diverted billions of rand. Those who undergo a review and successfully clear their names are eligible for a deferred payment date, which the agency has set for 7 December 2026. For anyone failing to respond, the consequence is the loss of access to their scheduled funds.

This administrative alert arrives as recipients prepare for a significantly longer 33-day payment cycle. Following the adjustment of November dates to accommodate local government elections, the agency has returned to its standard calendar, meaning recipients will need to stretch their funds over four additional days compared to a typical month. While older persons receive their grants on 2 December, disability grants follow on 3 December, and children’s grants are scheduled for 4 December.

The payment amounts remain consistent with the levels introduced in April 2026, as no further increases were announced in the October Budget Speech. The agency emphasizes that these December dates are final and apply through March 2027. If beneficiaries encounter difficulties or delays in accessing their accounts, the agency instructs them to visit their nearest local office for direct assistance. These measures are designed to align with strict eLife certification policies and cross-departmental data checks intended to ensure the integrity of the national social assistance program.
