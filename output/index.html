IRS Raises Income Thresholds for 2027 Tax Brackets to Combat Bracket Creep

The IRS has increased federal income tax brackets and standard deductions for 2027 to account for inflation, helping taxpayers avoid higher tax rates.

The standard deduction for married couples filing jointly will rise to $33,200 in 2027, up from $32,200 in 2026. Single filers will see their deduction increase to $16,600 from $16,100, according to data provided by the IRS. These adjustments represent a shift in the income ranges that determine how much of an individual's earnings are taxed at each level.

These updates are critical for taxpayers navigating the rising costs of daily living. By adjusting income thresholds annually based on the consumer price index, the IRS aims to prevent "bracket creep." This phenomenon occurs when cost-of-living pay raises push earners into higher tax tiers, even if their actual purchasing power has not increased. Higher thresholds ensure that individuals can earn more in 2027 without being automatically subjected to higher marginal rates.

While the income ranges for the seven federal tax brackets have shifted, the actual tax rates remain constant at 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. The highest rate of 37% applies to individual taxable income exceeding $661,375 and $793,650 for married couples filing jointly. Because the U.S. system is progressive, these rates apply only to the specific portion of income falling within each designated tier rather than the entirety of an earner's income.

In addition to brackets and standard deductions, the IRS has increased thresholds for several other provisions for 2027, including long-term capital gains, the estate and gift tax exemption, and eligibility for the earned income tax credit. Taxpayers will utilize these new figures when filing their federal income tax returns in 2028.
