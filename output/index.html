Packers safety Xavier McKinney slams NFL officiating after controversial Detroit Lions defeat

Safety Xavier McKinney faces potential fines for publicly criticizing officiating following multiple disputed calls during the Green Bay Packers' loss to the Detroit Lions.

Safety Xavier McKinney publicly labeled NFL officiating as "bulls--t" during a postgame session after several controversial calls influenced the outcome of Sunday's game against the Detroit Lions. McKinney explicitly pointed to a third-quarter sequence where Lions receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown collided with umpire Terry Killens Jr. before an unnecessary roughness penalty was assessed against Packers defensive lineman Devonte Wyatt, which helped Detroit secure a 17-14 lead.

The NFL prohibits players and club employees from publicly criticizing game officials, leaving McKinney likely to face league-issued fines for his comments. This policy underscores the strict league stance on maintaining the professional image of its officiating crews despite recurring player frustration with specific in-game decisions.

Beyond McKinney’s remarks, officiating quality drew scrutiny from FOX broadcaster and Raiders minority owner Tom Brady, who openly questioned several calls during the broadcast. Brady criticized a roughing the passer penalty against Packers linebacker Brenton Cox Jr., noting a lack of force on the play, and later expressed disbelief over an illegal hit penalty on Lions linebacker Derrick Barnes. FOX rules analyst Dean Blandino agreed with Brady's assessment, stating he did not view the hit by Cox as roughing the passer. Brady also challenged a late roughing the passer call against Packers defensive tackle Karl Brooks, calling the decision an error on what he described as a "bang-bang play."

As the league evaluates these incidents, the focus remains on whether such public critiques, particularly from individuals with ownership stakes, cross the threshold of permitted commentary under league guidelines. No further disciplinary steps beyond expected fines have been announced.
