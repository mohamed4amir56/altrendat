Eight Couples Storm Blackpool Tower Ballroom for Strictly Come Dancing Annual Special

The remaining eight celebrity couples perform in the historic Blackpool Tower Ballroom this weekend as the competition moves north for its annual tradition.

The venue, which dates back to 1894, is constructed from 30,600 pieces of oak, mahogany, and walnut wood and includes a sprung floor designed to provide a slight bounce for competitors. This location has hosted the series since 2004, interrupted only by the Covid pandemic since 2013, making it a critical stage for the remaining dancers.

For the participants, dancing in Blackpool is a highlight of the season due to the venue's reputation and its long-standing connection to professional dance competitions. Dancers like Lauren Oakley have noted that many performers are already familiar with the space from their earlier careers, as it regularly hosts World and all-England dance championships. The show provides a change of pace from the standard studio environment, allowing the production to utilize the venue’s expansive size for bigger staging and complex routines.

According to the BBC, Saturday’s live broadcast features a performance by Basement Jaxx who will deliver a medley of their hits alongside the show's cast. The Sunday results show will include two group performances by the professional dancers, alongside a musical set from S Club. While the couples have been filming ahead of the main event, the song and dance lists have been finalized and shared across the programme's social media platforms and the spin-off show It Takes Two. This weekend marks the continuation of a tradition that originated from the show's predecessor, Come Dancing.

Suspended Rassie Erasmus Risks Breach After Hosting Unauthorized Online Matchday Watchalong

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus held a livestream during the Ireland vs Springboks match while serving a six-match ban.

Thumping his table in frustration while watching Ireland secure a 20-17 victory, South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus spent the match in a Dublin hotel room rather than the Aviva Stadium. The 54-year-old, currently serving a six-match suspension handed down by World Rugby, hosted a public watchalong session that saw him appear on camera during both halves of the Nations Championship clash. While the suspension already prohibits him from matchday coaching or contact with team management, the event has drawn scrutiny from rugby's governing body regarding the terms of his ban. Sky Sports reports that officials are evaluating whether his actions constitute a breach of his restricted duties.

The potential fallout extends beyond World Rugby's disciplinary findings. According to The Guardian, Six Nations and Sanzaar executives have monitored the stream for copyright violations. While Erasmus is permitted to discuss the game, any broadcast of official match footage could trigger legal action from tournament organizers and rights holders like SuperSport, ITV, and Virgin Media. These organizations maintain strict anti-piracy measures and assigned staff to track the broadcast.

Erasmus received his suspension following public claims of match-fixing and manipulation after a previous defeat to France. The disciplinary panel ruled that he must refrain from stadium access, media duties, and all communication with players or staff on matchdays. Although he remained muted for most of the broadcast, Erasmus spoke during half-time to critique his team's performance, suggesting they needed to step up to avoid a narrow result. He justified the event on social media by stating he was not keen to watch the game alone. The governing body and tournament organizers continue to review the situation to determine if further sanctions or legal steps are warranted.

Grand Theft Auto VI Matches Ocarina of Time With 99 Metacritic Score

Grand Theft Auto VI has secured a 99 out of 100 Metascore, tying the record held by The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time.

While critics have cemented Grand Theft Auto VI as one of the greatest games of all time, the title has simultaneously faced a polarized reaction from the public. The game currently holds a 99 out of 100 critic rating on Metacritic, matching a record set by the 1998 classic The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. Despite this near-perfect critical reception, the game’s user score on the platform has fluctuated significantly, at one point dropping to 3.7 before climbing to 5.5. This disconnect highlights a divide between professional reviews and a vocal segment of the player base. The commercial performance of the release has been immense, with the game generating $3.79 billion in sales on its opening day alone, surpassing the total box office run of Avengers: Endgame. For players, the game represents a shift in philosophy for the franchise. Rather than leaning into the series' history of rapid-fire chaos, Rockstar Games has opted for a slower, more deliberate pace. This is particularly noticeable in the opening 15 hours of gameplay, which some users find frustrating compared to previous entries. Other complaints center on new mechanics, such as a restricted weapon system that limits players to two guns, and vehicle limitations that prevent users from changing their primary mode of transport. These design choices have led some fans to criticize the game for prioritizing realism over the series' traditional freedom. While critics praise the title as a masterpiece, the ongoing user feedback reveals that this shift in gameplay direction remains a point of contention for those expecting a more traditional experience.
