Kellogg’s Admits Rice Krispies Snap, Crackle and Pop is Quieter After Reformulation

Kellogg's replaced whole rice grains with corn and rice flour in UK Rice Krispies and Coco Pops, resulting in a noticeably quieter sound.

The iconic snap, crackle and pop, a marketing centerpiece for the cereal since the 1950s, is fading as milk no longer triggers the same reaction in the bowl. Kellogg’s spokesperson confirmed that while the sound still exists, it is now quieter and less instant than in previous versions of the product. The change follows a shift from whole rice grains to a blend of corn flour and rice flour, a move necessitated by changing weather conditions that disrupted global grain supplies.

The impact of this modification reaches beyond the bowl’s acoustics, sparking intense debate among consumers. Fans who have consumed the breakfast staple for years report that the texture is now weird and stale, with some parents noting that their children refuse to eat the new version. Because the updated recipe now incorporates wheat gluten as a main ingredient, the cereal may no longer be suitable for shoppers with wheat allergies or celiac disease, requiring new allergen labeling in the UK.

While social media platforms like TikTok are filled with consumer complaints comparing the new formulation to the old, the company maintains that the products underwent careful assessment for taste and appearance. The company is actively monitoring consumer feedback regarding these changes.

Consumers in North America will not experience these issues. WK Kellogg Co. confirmed that Rice Krispies sold in the United States and Canada continue to be made with real rice grains. There are no plans to alter the ingredients or recipes for these markets, and shoppers in those regions can expect the same taste as before.
