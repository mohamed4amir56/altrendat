Fans slam ITV reporter Gabriel Clarke for twisting Declan Rice's match comments

ITV broadcaster Gabriel Clarke faced intense public backlash after misrepresenting Declan Rice's post-match remarks during an interview with manager Thomas Tuchel.

Declan Rice told ITV reporter Gabriel Clarke that England's 3-2 victory over Spain was a matter of resilience, stating, "That's football sometimes you know, that can happen." When Clarke followed up by asking if the high-pressure closing minutes triggered "flashbacks" to England's World Cup semi-final defeat against Argentina, Rice replied, "Yeah of course, but it's football." Moments later, Clarke told manager Thomas Tuchel, "Declan Rice has just said there were flashbacks to the defeat to Argentina," an assertion that directly contradicted the context of the player's previous response.

This incident has sparked significant controversy among fans and media commentators who argue that the journalist intentionally misrepresented the player's words. TalkSPORT presenter Simon Jordan expressed his frustration on social media, accusing the reporter of deliberately phrasing the question to elicit a specific reaction. Sky Sports News pundit Guvna B added to the criticism, suggesting that the interviewer was creating friction between the management and the squad by misquoting the player's sentiments.

The Mirror reports that the exchange occurred following England's win at the Metropolitano in Madrid, which ended Spain's 43-game unbeaten run. The match saw England maintain their lead despite playing with 10 men for the final half-hour after Elliot Anderson was sent off. While the reporter attempted to draw parallels to past tournament defeats, many spectators argued that a more accurate comparison for the hard-fought victory would have been England's previous win at the Azteca Stadium.

This controversy follows previous accusations against Clarke regarding the misrepresentation of comments made by Thomas Tuchel during an earlier interview with Jude Bellingham. Neither ITV nor the reporter have publicly addressed the backlash following the most recent match coverage.
