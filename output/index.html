GTA 6's hurricane system brings heavy winds, flooding, and lightning to virtual Florida

Rockstar Games confirmed Grand Theft Auto 6 features a redesigned weather system with hurricanes and tropical storms that drench the map with rain, wind, and dramatic lightning effects.

Grand Theft Auto 6 will force players to navigate through hurricanes and tropical storms as they build their criminal empire in Rockstar's version of Florida. The game's weather system simulates heavy clouds, strong winds, dense atmospherics, and intense rain that leaves everything dripping wet, with water gushing off rooftops and lampposts while thunder rumbles and lightning flashes across the sky.

Rockstar has completely redesigned the weather system to make the world feel dynamically solid with a sense of scale the series has not achieved before. Owen Shepherd, vice president of art for lighting and rendering at Rockstar, explained that the studio re-engineered the system to be fully localized and persistent, affecting how players experience the environment during missions and exploration.

Beyond the major storms, Rockstar modeled subtle weather details with the same precision. Summer breezes and convectional updrafts will occur after sunset, while wind will blow ripples over water surfaces and bend grass in swamps. Hair and debris will swirl around rooftops as gusts move around buildings. The map features dynamic cloudscapes ranging from clear Miami winter skies to the towering cumulonimbus clouds with sporadic lightning typical of Florida summers and autumns.

Rockstar confirmed the game includes over 170 animal species alongside the weather system. The company has not yet revealed exactly how hurricanes and tropical storms will impact gameplay, though Shepherd suggested the persistent system could alter the environment in significant ways.

He crashes a White House crisis meeting with the President to make his case. Cruise even appears in underwear as a cheeky homage to his breakthrough film Risky Business, while his character's mansion is decorated with more stuffed exotic animals than a Victorian museum—despite his deep worry over his scrawny pet cat.
