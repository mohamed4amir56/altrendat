Mariners land Gold Glove infielder Maikel Garcia in massive six-player blockbuster trade

The Seattle Mariners acquired third baseman Maikel Garcia from the Kansas City Royals in a trade involving six players.

Maikel Garcia is owed $52.5 million through 2030, a figure seen as a very reasonable cost for a player under long-term team control. The 26-year-old infielder arrives in Seattle after a 2026 season where he maintained a 1.8 WAR despite missing time due to hand and hamstring injuries. Just one year prior, Garcia recorded a 5.6 WAR season and received MVP votes for his performance at third base.

This deal provides the Mariners with a proven, defensively sound option at a position currently experiencing a league-wide shortage of talent. While Garcia experienced physical setbacks during the 2026 campaign, his defensive profile and established status as a starter offer Seattle stability at third base. The move is viewed by industry observers as a strategic acquisition of a rare, young infielder in an environment where reliable third-base production is increasingly difficult to secure via trade or free agency.

According to reports from ESPN and the Seattle Times, the Royals are set to receive right-handed pitchers Logan Evans and Bryce Miller along with left-handed pitcher Jose Ferrer. The trade effectively moves three arms to Kansas City in exchange for the infield reinforcement headed to the Pacific Northwest. Analysts note that while the park environment for Garcia will change with his move to Seattle, the acquisition remains a calculated bet on his potential to return to his previous peak form. Neither the Mariners nor the Royals have made an official announcement regarding the trade, which stands as the first blockbuster move of the current offseason.

Hakeem Jeffries clinches House speaker nomination for historic leadership role in January

House Democrats officially nominated Hakeem Jeffries as Speaker on Wednesday, positioning him to become the first Black lawmaker to lead the chamber.

Hakeem Jeffries secured his party's nomination with a commanding 251-11 vote during a closed-door session on Wednesday. This result follows his tenure as minority leader, a position he has held since 2023, and sets the stage for his elevation to the top spot in the House when the new congressional term officially commences in January.

Securing this nomination is significant as it places Jeffries directly in the line of presidential succession, following the vice-president. As Democrats are set to control the House with at least 257 seats, his path to the speakership represents a transition in chamber leadership that will shift the legislative agenda under his guidance. The outcome marks a critical consolidation of power for the party as they prepare to take charge of the House floor.

Despite the clear mandate from his colleagues, Jeffries encountered resistance from a small faction of the party's far-left wing. Seven incoming lawmakers, including Darializa Avila Chevalier and Claire Valdez, issued a statement questioning whether the party's direction would fully align with their constituents' demands. While these holdouts expressed concerns about the pace of progress, the overwhelming majority support ensures that Jeffries maintains the caucus's backing as he heads toward the formal election.

Looking ahead, the full House is scheduled to hold the official election for the new Speaker on 3 January. With seven congressional races currently uncalled, the final Democratic majority count may grow beyond the 257 seats already secured for the upcoming term.
