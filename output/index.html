The Bank of America estimates that interest rates exceeding 5 per cent could push the global economy into crisis, even as major central banks continue raising borrowing costs.

The Bank of America has identified a dangerous threshold: interest rates above 5 per cent would threaten financial stability worldwide, according to Mark Cabana, the bank's head of interest rates strategy. The estimate comes as central banks globally face mounting pressure from low productivity, excess demand, artificial intelligence investment, and geopolitical tensions that are all pushing up borrowing costs.

Higher rates matter because they directly affect retirement savings and asset prices. Australian superannuation funds, which manage billions in retirement money, face growing risks as rates climb. Share markets and property prices have so far weathered recent rate increases, but that resilience may not hold if borrowing costs breach the 5 per cent mark.

The Federal Reserve recently raised America's benchmark rate by 25 basis points to a range between 3.75 per cent and 4 per cent. The Reserve Bank of Australia lifted its cash rate by the same amount to 4.6 per cent. Cabana expects rates to keep rising globally and bond yield curves to continue flattening, since current interest rate levels are not yet restrictive enough to bring down overall economic demand. Meanwhile, major Australian banks including ANZ, National Australia Bank, Commonwealth Bank, and Westpac have raised fixed mortgage rates by almost double the RBA's recent increase, compounding pressure on borrowers as funding costs deepen in a slowing economy.

Analysts warn that further rate rises would increase the risk of a loan-loss cycle in the banking sector next year, which would mark the first major downturn for banks in 35 years.

While the storm itself moves toward Japan, its trough is currently triggering scattered rains and thunderstorms throughout Visayas and Mindanao.

This shift in weather patterns requires residents in the affected southern regions to remain alert for sudden flooding and potential landslides. According to the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), the risks are particularly elevated during severe thunderstorm activity, where heavy downpours can turn localized weather into dangerous hazards for low-lying communities.

Weather specialist Aldczar D. Aurelio confirmed that while Choi-Wan will not enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility, its influence persists throughout the day. PAGASA reports that the typhoon will move further away, ultimately clearing the region's weather. Once the trough dissipates, the Intertropical Convergence Zone—a band of clouds caused by trade winds from both hemispheres—is expected to move in, maintaining a risk of rainfall and flooding for the Visayas and Mindanao.

In contrast to the southern rains, Luzon, including Metro Manila, Baguio, and Legazpi, is forecast to experience generally fair conditions from Monday through Wednesday. Aside from typical localized afternoon and evening thunderstorms, no major weather disturbances are anticipated in the north. PAGASA has not issued any gale warnings, though officials note that offshore thunderstorms may still cause temporary increases in wind speed and wave heights.

Singapore Authorities Impound 91 Deregistered Vehicles in Massive September Islandwide Crackdown

The Land Transport Authority and Singapore Police Force seized 91 vehicles in September for operating without valid Certificates of Entitlement or road tax.

A surge in illegal road activity has prompted a sharp response, as Singapore authorities identified 245 cases of deregistered vehicles on the roads in 2025, a significant rise from 75 in 2024. These vehicles present a severe hazard because they are neither roadworthy nor covered by mandatory insurance, leaving other road users at risk.

Keeping or using these unregistered vehicles carries stiff consequences for owners. Since February, first-time offenders face maximum penalties of a S$20,000 fine, up to two years in jail, or both. Repeat offenders are subject to double these penalties, highlighting the government's push to clear these vehicles from circulation.

The recent operation follows a September enforcement blitz by the Land Transport Authority (LTA) and the Singapore Police Force. The authorities emphasized that owners remain responsible for their vehicles even after handing them over to dealers. They advise that all administrative processes, such as confirming ownership transfers on the OneMotoring website or obtaining proper disposal documents, are vital to avoiding investigation.

Legislative action is now moving forward to close existing loopholes. A new bill introduced in Parliament on September 8 proposes an Authorised Exporter Scheme, which would mandate that all deregistered vehicles be handled only by LTA-authorized exporters. Additionally, the grace period for disposing of a vehicle will be slashed from one month to 14 days, and those who fail to meet these requirements may face daily fines of up to S$500 until disposal is verified. Authorities are also seeking the power to prevent vehicle transfers to individuals flagged as high-risk for failing to dispose of vehicles properly.

The deterioration matters for outdoor plans. Healthy people are advised to reduce strenuous outdoor activity when 1-hour PM2.5 levels are raised. Elderly people, pregnant women, children, and people with chronic heart or lung conditions should avoid strenuous outdoor activity altogether during elevated readings.

Earlier that morning at 6am, the central region's PSI had been at 110 and the west at 102.

The National Environment Agency forecast dry conditions to continue over Singapore and the surrounding region, coupled with prevailing south-easterly or south-westerly winds. This combination means a continued risk of smoke haze affecting Singapore. The agency recommends referring to the 1-hour PM2.5 readings and corresponding personal guide for immediate outdoor activities, and using the 24-hour PSI forecast and health advisories when planning activities for the next day.
