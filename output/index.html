Over 700 Scottish schools hit by hoax bomb threats targeting students

More than 700 schools in Scotland and Northern Ireland received bomb threats that authorities have assessed as non-credible hoaxes.

More than 6,000 schools in Italy also faced similar warnings, bringing the total number of affected institutions to thousands across three regions. Despite the scale of the emails, authorities in all locations confirmed there is no concrete danger or risk to the public. In Scotland, the messages specifically referenced a figure named Mikhail Andropov and identified as the leader of a project called Red Owl. While some local authorities chose to evacuate buildings as a precaution, Police Scotland explicitly stated that there is no requirement to keep children away from classrooms based on the email content alone.

These threats create significant disruption for parents, pupils, and staff who must manage the stress of unexpected warnings during the school day. While police are investigating the origins of the messages, the incidents are being treated as part of a wider trend that has recently affected hospitals, supermarkets, and airports across Europe. Officials are currently working to determine if these events are connected to other international hoaxes, though early indications point to a coordinated strategy intended to incite alarm rather than cause physical harm.

Scottish Education Secretary Mairi McAllan confirmed that specialist cyber-crime officers are actively working to identify the source of the communication. Meanwhile, in Northern Ireland, education officials have advised schools to continue normal operations unless specifically directed otherwise by police. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni stated on social media that the threats lack evidence of real danger, describing the strategy as an attempt to cause concern rather than a genuine security breach. Investigators continue to monitor the situation to ensure the safety of students and staff across the affected regions.
