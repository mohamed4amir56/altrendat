Tsunami advisory cancelled for North Island after mysterious surge hits coastal gauges

The National Emergency Management Agency lifted a coastal tsunami alert for Napier and Gisborne following unusual tidal gauge activity on Wednesday.

Tidal gauges at Napier and Gisborne recorded surges reaching up to 60cm on Wednesday evening, prompting a rapid precautionary response. The activity appeared suddenly on instruments used by geohazards analysts, triggering a national advisory that warned of strong currents and unpredictable surges capable of injuring or drowning swimmers and those near the water. While the threat was perceived as severe enough to activate a specialized tsunami expert panel, no public mobile alerts were issued during the brief window of concern.

This incident highlights the challenges of monitoring coastal hazards where no seismic trigger is present. Because there was no recorded earthquake, emergency officials focused their efforts on managing the risk to people in harbours, marinas, and near estuaries without causing unnecessary widespread alarm. The advisory served as a due diligence measure to ensure civil defence partners were prepared while experts investigated the potential for sudden underwater movements.

According to the National Emergency Management Agency, the threat was officially cancelled at 6:04pm, just 26 minutes after the advisory was first issued at 5:38pm. Officials report that while the signals have since subsided, the exact cause of the phenomenon remains unidentified. Researchers are currently exploring various possibilities, including the potential for an underwater landslide or specific weather-related changes that could have displaced enough water to fluctuate the gauges.

Ongoing monitoring efforts continue to track the coastal waters of Hawke's Bay and Tai Rāwhiti. Experts from Earth Sciences New Zealand are working to pinpoint the source of the activity, though they confirm there is no longer a risk to the shoreline.
