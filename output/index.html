DOJ surges to strip citizenship from 40 Americans in record crackdown

The U.S. Department of Justice has filed 40 new petitions to revoke the citizenship of naturalized Americans accused of committing crimes and immigration fraud.

The 40 new denaturalization petitions were filed by the U.S. Department of Justice between late August and early October. This represents the largest volume of such cases submitted in such a short period under President Donald Trump, according to official statements. Historically, these legal processes have been reserved for grave instances, such as those involving Holocaust perpetrators or war crimes following World War II, but the current administration is expanding their use as part of a broader push to conduct the largest deportation operation in American history. Attorney General Todd Blanche labeled the move the largest denaturalization effort in modern history. The government intends to use these legal tools to maintain national security and protect the public. The individuals facing these petitions include foreign-born Americans from countries such as Mexico, Pakistan, China, and Sierra Leone. Prosecutors allege that these individuals concealed criminal activities and other facts about themselves while applying for citizenship. Among the specific accusations are money laundering, child sexual abuse, visa fraud, and tax evasion. The Department of Justice further alleges that two individuals on the list engaged in espionage on behalf of China. Officials argue that because these crimes were committed or the information was hidden during the application process, the affected individuals lacked the necessary good moral character required for naturalization. While the Department of Justice has previously used similar laws to pursue individuals for serious offenses, the frequency of these actions had decreased significantly since the end of the Cold War. The current administration has signaled that it will continue to employ the denaturalization process as a key component of its immigration enforcement strategy.

Singapore Tightens Safety Rules Following Seven Recent Workplace Fatalities In Construction

The Ministry of Manpower mandates a voluntary safety time-out and harsher penalties after seven workers died in September.

Seven workers died in workplace incidents throughout September, prompting the Ministry of Manpower to demand immediate action. Among the recent casualties, a 22-year-old worker fell from the 12th storey of a construction site, while two others drowned after their work platform dislodged at Tuas Port. Another fatal accident involved a 35-year-old struck by a pressurized hose at a construction site. These tragedies serve as a grim reminder that safety protocols must not be neglected.

For businesses in the construction, transportation, and storage sectors, these changes directly impact operational freedom and financial liability. Companies are urged to review all health procedures, specifically targeting the use of concrete pumps and vehicle safety mechanisms like wheel chocks and handbrakes. Failure to comply with evolving standards now carries more severe consequences, potentially halting work for months or cutting off access to the migrant-worker labor pool.

According to the Ministry of Manpower, firms are encouraged to conduct a voluntary safety time-out from Oct 5 to Oct 18. Simultaneously, the government is rolling out an enforcement operation running from Oct 5 to Nov 4. First-time composition fines will jump from S$2,000 to S$3,000, and minimum stop-work orders are expanding from five weeks to eight weeks. In the most serious cases, companies may face a three-month ban on hiring new migrant workers.

Minister of State for Manpower Dinesh Vasu Dash stated that the government must act decisively to stop the loss of lives. These measures mirror a previous intervention implemented nationwide between June 26 and July 31 following a similar spate of fatalities earlier in the year.
