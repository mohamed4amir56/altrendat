      Treasury auto-enrolls 60 million children in Trump Accounts starting October 1

      The U.S. Treasury Department announced auto-enrollment of Trump Accounts for eligible children beginning October 1, potentially adding 60 million accounts by 2026, up from 7 to 8 million currently enrolled.

      The shift from opt-in to automatic enrollment marks a major expansion for the tax-deferred investment accounts launched July 4. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Congress in mid-September that enrollment would jump from 7 to 8 million to 70 million within a month once auto-enrollment begins.

      The change targets a persistent gap in the program's reach. Only 5% of low- and moderate-income families earning up to $80,000 annually have opened accounts, and just 10,000 sign-ups came from households reporting no income despite 8.6 million eligible children in that group. Automatic enrollment could remove the barrier of filing IRS Form 4547 or visiting TrumpAccounts.gov, according to researchers at the Urban Institute.

      Auto-enrolled accounts are limited until parents claim them. The automatic accounts receive the $1,000 government deposit for children born 2025 through 2028, plus gifts from governments and nonprofits. Parents must then verify their identity through a Treasury app or website to add family money or employer contributions, which can total up to $5,000 yearly including up to $2,500 from employers. Contributions are invested in low-cost index funds and generally cannot be withdrawn before the year the child turns 18.

      The temporary rules also extend large charitable donations, including the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation's $6.25 billion pledge for children born 2016 through 2024 in ZIP codes with median household income below $150,000. In future years, Treasury said auto-enrollment could add roughly 2 million accounts annually as newborns arrive.
