The estimate comes as central banks globally face mounting pressure from low productivity, excess demand, artificial intelligence investment, and geopolitical tensions that are all pushing up borrowing costs.

The Reserve Bank of Australia lifted its cash rate by the same amount to 4.6 per cent. Cabana expects rates to keep rising globally and bond yield curves to continue flattening, since current interest rate levels are not yet restrictive enough to bring down overall economic demand. Meanwhile, major Australian banks including ANZ, National Australia Bank, Commonwealth Bank, and Westpac have raised fixed mortgage rates by almost double the RBA's recent increase, compounding pressure on borrowers as funding costs deepen in a slowing economy.

Analysts warn that further rate rises would increase the risk of a loan-loss cycle in the banking sector next year, which would mark the first major downturn for banks in 35 years.
