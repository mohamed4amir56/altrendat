Ferrari boss slams Hamilton and Leclerc after Mexico City Grand Prix collapse

Team Principal Fred Vasseur voiced frustration after Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc collided on the opening lap, costing Ferrari a likely victory.

For a few brief seconds, Ferrari held a dominant one-two position during the opening lap of the Mexico City Grand Prix. That tactical advantage vanished instantly at Turn 4 when Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc competed too aggressively for the same piece of track, ultimately allowing Max Verstappen and Red Bull to seize the lead. Ferrari Team Principal Fred Vasseur expressed deep frustration, noting that the team had everything in its power to secure their best result of the season before the internal clash turned potential success into a lost race.

This incident marks a critical point for the team, as Vasseur described the behavior as a lack of respect for the 2,000 employees working back at the Maranello factory. The Team Principal emphasized that the drivers are not racing for themselves alone and that wasting a victory is difficult to accept when the entire organization has worked day and night to improve the car. Vasseur noted that in Mexico City, where overtaking is historically difficult, being one-two at Turn 4 should have guaranteed a victory.

While Leclerc blamed the specific layout of the track for the issues, Vasseur insisted that track design cannot serve as an excuse for the drivers' actions. Hamilton later acknowledged that he and Leclerc failed to work together effectively during the opening sequence. Although Ferrari ultimately finished in second and fourth place, the team remains focused on the missed opportunity that occurred when their drivers prioritized their own positions over the team's collective objective.
