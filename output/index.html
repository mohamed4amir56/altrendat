Apple Services Plunge Worldwide As Thousands Report Outages With Payments And Cloud

Widespread technical failures halted Apple Pay, iCloud, and App Store access for users across the globe starting Tuesday afternoon.

Within minutes of the initial failure, reports on the tracking site Downdetector spiked to 2,000 as users discovered they were locked out of their accounts. The disruption began around 12:45 pm ET, preventing customers from activating new devices, completing banking transactions via Apple Pay, or accessing essential cloud storage and support features. While many users reported seeing error messages during identity verification, others noted being unable to log into Apple Business Manager, effectively stalling professional and personal tasks simultaneously.

This outage is significant for millions of users who rely on the integrated ecosystem for everything from device activation to daily financial transactions. When central authentication services like iCloud fail, the impact ripples through hardware and software alike, rendering even high-end devices difficult to manage or initialize. The sudden loss of service serves as a reminder of the fragility of cloud-dependent technology, leaving businesses and individuals scrambling for alternatives while basic diagnostic tools remained largely unresponsive.

According to the New York Post, the trouble extended beyond the US, with confirmed complaints from users in Brazil, Greece, and Latvia. Tom's Guide reported that while the official Apple system status page initially remained mostly green, it eventually acknowledged issues with iCloud account sign-ins, Apple Music, and the App Store. The developer status page also indicated deeper problems affecting specific APIs for music and podcast services.

As of late Tuesday, while some reports indicated that issues with Apple Pay and Apple Card were beginning to subside, several core services remained listed as suffering from outages on official status pages. Apple has not yet provided a specific timetable for restoring full functionality or identified the root cause of the technical failure, leaving users to monitor status dashboards for further updates.

15cm Snow Warning Triggers Travel Disruptions Across Northern England And Southern Scotland

The Met Office has issued a yellow warning for snow in effect until 11:00 GMT on Tuesday as temperatures across the UK plummet.

The temperature dipped to a freezing -7.7C at Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands, marking the coldest night of the autumn so far. As this cold air settles over the region, a weather front moving north is encountering these low temperatures, triggering a transition from rain to snow across hills in northern England and southern Scotland. The Met Office projects that between 5cm and 15cm of snow could accumulate on high ground above 300-400m elevation. Even at lower elevations near 100m, residents may see up to 5cm of snowfall, though meteorologists expect this to be short-lived and melt quickly by mid-morning. The impact of this weather front extends beyond accumulation, as thick, wet snow flakes risk sticking to infrastructure, creating potential hazards. Coupled with wind gusts reaching 40-50mph, this combination threatens power lines and tree branches, heightening the risk of localized power outages and disruptions to telecommunications. Commuters should prepare for potential delays, as the Met Office warns that major transit arteries, including the A74(M), A66, and M62, may face significant travel disruption, alongside longer journey times for rail services. While the highest mountains and hills in Scotland and northern England are expected to contend with wintry conditions through the afternoon, the rest of the UK will experience a shift in weather. By Wednesday, high pressure is forecast to move in, bringing clearer and sunnier skies to replace the current rainy, cold spell that has impacted Northern Ireland, England, and Wales. For now, however, a cold health alert issued by the UK Health Security Agency remains active for northern England to assist those managing the sudden drop in temperatures.
