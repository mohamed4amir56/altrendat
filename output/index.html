Manchester United Sacks Michael Carrick Following Worst Ever Premier League Season Start

Manchester United has sacked head coach Michael Carrick after he oversaw only one win in 10 matches, leaving the club in the relegation zone.

Michael Carrick’s departure came less than six months after he signed a two-year contract with the club. The decision was reached during a summit in Monaco, where minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe ratified the move following recommendations from chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox. Carrick had taken training on Wednesday, but his tenure ended the following morning after the hierarchy concluded a change was necessary to meet the club's ambitions.

This decision marks a critical turning point for a team that currently sits in the relegation zone after 10 league games. United’s management maintains that the current squad is significantly stronger than the results and performances displayed under Carrick. His exit follows a 4-4 draw against Aston Villa, where the team conceded two goals within the first four minutes and trailed 4-2 early in the second half before staging a comeback. While that result and a win against Roma showed resilience, they were insufficient to secure Carrick’s future.

Sky Sports reports that Mauricio Pochettino has verbally agreed to replace Carrick as the next permanent manager. He is set to become the eighth permanent appointment since Sir Alex Ferguson’s retirement in 2013. Pochettino currently manages the USA national team and possesses a £7m release clause in his contract. United expects talks with the United Soccer Federation to accelerate shortly.

As the club searches for a permanent successor, internal changes are expected to continue, with coaching staff members Jonny Evans and Jonathan Woodgate also anticipated to leave. Pochettino is expected to remain with the USA for their upcoming Nations League matches against Haiti this Saturday and Wednesday before potentially beginning his new role next Thursday.
