ASOS hacked: Threatening push notification sent to thousands of mobile app users

ASOS app users received a threatening message on Tuesday morning claiming the company's Snowflake data instance was fully compromised.

On Tuesday morning, ASOS mobile app users were met with an alarming notification declaring 'ASOS HACKED' across their screens. The message, seemingly authored by cyber attackers, explicitly warned, 'Dear Asos DPO and IT, we have fully compromised the Snowflake instance. Engage with us, or we will leak it,' and directed recipients toward a Telegram chat link.

This security incident impacts the millions of customers who rely on the ASOS platform, with reports of widespread distribution via push notifications. The mention of a compromised Snowflake instance suggests a significant breach of the online data platform used by the retailer. For users, the notification highlights a potential risk to the personal information overseen by the company's data protection officer.

According to The Independent and LADbible, the incident began manifesting around 9:41am, coinciding with a surge of user reports on DownDetector regarding functionality issues. While ASOS has yet to issue an official statement or address the breach on social media, the market reacted swiftly to the news. As reported by The Independent, the company's share price plummeted by approximately 5 per cent in the immediate aftermath of the notifications being sent.

Under UK law, British companies are mandated to report data breaches to officials within a three-day window. Regulations further require that businesses notify affected individuals promptly if a cyber attack presents a high risk to their data. Customers are now awaiting official guidance from ASOS as authorities determine the extent of the alleged compromise.

Budget 2027 Ramps Up Help To Buy To €35k For First-Time Buyers

Government leaders have finalized Budget 2027, featuring a €5,000 tax rebate increase, a €10 weekly pension hike, and adjustments to inheritance thresholds.

First-time buyers will see their Help to Buy tax rebate rise by €5,000 to a total of €35,000 as part of the newly finalized financial package. This specific adjustment comes as government coalition leaders concluded crunch talks on Monday night to shift the focus toward workers, following a previous cycle that officials acknowledged left households worse off.

For most citizens, the budget brings immediate changes to social welfare and income tax thresholds. The State pension is set to rise by €10 per week, a figure that also applies to all other core social welfare payments. Additionally, the entry point for the 40% higher rate of income tax will shift from €44,000 to €46,500, a move that is €500 higher than what was initially expected by market analysts and observers.

Beyond basic income adjustments, the government has introduced a new permanent disability payment of €500. Families will receive an additional €6 per week in child support payments, though the standard Children’s Allowance remains unchanged. Those relying on social welfare will also receive a fuel allowance increase of €5 and the customary Christmas bonus. Regarding capital acquisitions, inheritance tax thresholds are rising across all categories, with the parent-to-child limit climbing by €20,000 to reach €420,000.

While the budget aims to alleviate cost-of-living pressures, it notably excludes energy credits, which will not be part of the 2027 package. Finance ministers Jack Chambers and Simon Harris are scheduled to present the formal details to Dáil Éireann on Tuesday afternoon to confirm the public spending and taxation strategy for the coming year.

Singapore Tightens Safety Rules Following Seven Recent Workplace Fatalities In Construction

The Ministry of Manpower mandates a voluntary safety time-out and harsher penalties after seven workers died in September.

Seven workers died in workplace incidents throughout September, prompting the Ministry of Manpower to demand immediate action. Among the recent casualties, a 22-year-old worker fell from the 12th storey of a construction site, while two others drowned after their work platform dislodged at Tuas Port. Another fatal accident involved a 35-year-old struck by a pressurized hose at a construction site. These tragedies serve as a grim reminder that safety protocols must not be neglected.

For businesses in the construction, transportation, and storage sectors, these changes directly impact operational freedom and financial liability. Companies are urged to review all health procedures, specifically targeting the use of concrete pumps and vehicle safety mechanisms like wheel chocks and handbrakes. Failure to comply with evolving standards now carries more severe consequences, potentially halting work for months or cutting off access to the migrant-worker labor pool.

According to the Ministry of Manpower, firms are encouraged to conduct a voluntary safety time-out from Oct 5 to Oct 18. Simultaneously, the government is rolling out an enforcement operation running from Oct 5 to Nov 4. First-time composition fines will jump from S$2,000 to S$3,000, and minimum stop-work orders are expanding from five weeks to eight weeks. In the most serious cases, companies may face a three-month ban on hiring new migrant workers.

Minister of State for Manpower Dinesh Vasu Dash stated that the government must act decisively to stop the loss of lives. These measures mirror a previous intervention implemented nationwide between June 26 and July 31 following a similar spate of fatalities earlier in the year.
