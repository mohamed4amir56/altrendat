Filming North of North in the Arctic at -39 Degrees Celsius

The creators of the Netflix comedy North of North filmed their series in Iqaluit, Nunavut, battling freezing temperatures to bring the Arctic town of Ice Cove to life.

Production crews for the series North of North braved extreme temperatures reaching -39 degrees Celsius while filming in the Canadian Arctic. To manage the harsh environment, the production team relied on extreme logistics, including hiring extra drivers to transport crew members to hotel bathrooms because portable washroom pipes were prone to freezing.

Filming the comedy in Iqaluit, the capital of Nunavut, was a deliberate choice by creators Stacey Aglok MacDonald and Alethea Arnaquq-Baril to ensure authentic representation of the landscape and community. According to the Los Angeles Times, the production required a massive logistical effort, including a feasibility study to prove that local infrastructure, such as hotels and truck rentals, could support a full-scale scripted series. The show is co-commissioned by CBC, Netflix, and the Indigenous network APTN.

The series, which stars Anna Lambe as Siaja, follows a young Inuk mother who returns to her community to reinvent her life. To prepare for the first season, the production team hired a producer a full year in advance to manage the procurement of supplies. Materials were transported via sealift during the brief annual thaw to avoid the high costs of airfreight. Sets were primarily constructed in Toronto before being disassembled and palletized for transport to the Arctic, where they were housed in a repurposed curling club.

While the first season established the fictional town of Ice Cove, Vulture reports that the recently released second season takes a more somber, complex direction. The show continues to balance workplace comedy and romantic elements while exploring Indigenous life, introducing more textured histories and outside perspectives to the town's narrative arc.
