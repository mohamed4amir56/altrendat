Matt Henry rips through India with six wickets in massive 244-run win

Matt Henry claimed six wickets to bowl India out for 89, securing a 244-run victory for New Zealand in the fourth ODI on Friday.

The visitors were left reeling at 0-3 after the very first over of the chase, as Matt Henry dismissed Rohit Sharma, Virat Kohli, and Shreyas Iyer in a devastating opening spell. India never recovered, falling to their sixth-lowest ODI total of just 89 runs at Mount Maunganui. This performance kept the series alive for the Black Caps, who now trail 2-1 heading into the final match.

For New Zealand, the dominant victory provides a crucial boost after earlier struggles in the series. While the team can no longer win the five-match series, the 244-run margin highlights a clinical turnaround from the hosts. With India resting key players including captain Shubman Gill and KL Rahul for the upcoming test matches, the final ODI on Sunday offers New Zealand a chance to draw the series level.

According to RNZ, Matt Henry's clinical display with the ball was matched by a commanding batting performance from Will Young. After being dropped twice in the second over, Young capitalized to score 125 from 108 balls, marking his fifth ODI century and his highest score in the format. Supported by Tim Seifert’s 58 and Finn Allen’s 41, New Zealand posted 334. India’s efforts were limited to a 44-run partnership between Ishan Kishan and Axar Patel before Henry returned to complete his six-wicket haul.

The two teams are set to play the final match of the ODI series this Sunday at the same venue. Following that, attention will shift to the test format, with India preparing for a two-test series against New Zealand beginning on Wednesday.

Global Microsoft Outage Disables Outlook and Teams Across 74 Different Countries

Microsoft services including Outlook and Teams faced a global disruption impacting users in 74 countries for over 12 hours starting Thursday.

At least 43,000 users logged reports on the outage tracker Downdetector by 07:00 GMT on Friday, a figure that represents only a portion of those unable to access their workplace tools. The widespread disruption saw servers unexpectedly become unavailable, forcing Microsoft to attempt infrastructure reboots while battling ongoing connectivity errors that dragged through the US working day and into the night across Europe.

For millions of users, the outage means a total loss of access to essential communication and productivity software, including Outlook emails and the Teams collaboration platform. The instability persists even as the company applies patches, with some previously restored servers failing again, causing significant operational delays for businesses and individuals relying on these core cloud services.

According to Al Jazeera, the technical failure began at approximately 16:00 GMT on Thursday. Microsoft initially identified issues specific to the Americas but quickly expanded the scope to confirm that users in any region could be impacted by the elevated load on their infrastructure. The company explicitly noted that the connectivity errors were not triggered by a sudden surge in user traffic, but rather by internal server management issues.

Microsoft continues to monitor server performance across all affected regions as they work toward a full recovery. The company has publicly reported only marginal success in restoring services, emphasizing that technical teams are currently reviewing server performance to confirm that patches function as intended following the completion of full infrastructure reboots.

France dominated the early exchanges, with Bradley Barcola and Rayan Cherki both hitting the woodwork before Italy regained control. Davide Frattesi added to the Italian tally, while France remained without injured stars Kylian Mbappé and Ousmane Dembélé throughout the match. Both teams must now look ahead to their remaining group fixtures as they battle for the top spot. Belgium remains in the mix, sitting just one point behind the leaders following their victory over bottom-placed Turkey.
