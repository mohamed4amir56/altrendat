The Reserve Bank of Australia lifted its cash rate by the same amount to 4.6 per cent. Meanwhile, major Australian banks including ANZ, National Australia Bank, Commonwealth Bank, and Westpac have raised fixed mortgage rates by almost double the RBA's recent increase, compounding pressure on borrowers as funding costs deepen in a slowing economy.

Analysts warn that further rate rises would increase the risk of a loan-loss cycle in the banking sector next year, which would mark the first major downturn for banks in 35 years.

While the storm itself moves toward Japan, its trough is currently triggering scattered rains and thunderstorms throughout Visayas and Mindanao.

This shift in weather patterns requires residents in the affected southern regions to remain alert for sudden flooding and potential landslides. PAGASA reports that the typhoon will move further away, ultimately clearing the region's weather. Once the trough dissipates, the Intertropical Convergence Zone—a band of clouds caused by trade winds from both hemispheres—is expected to move in, maintaining a risk of rainfall and flooding for the Visayas and Mindanao.

In contrast to the southern rains, Luzon, including Metro Manila, Baguio, and Legazpi, is forecast to experience generally fair conditions from Monday through Wednesday. Aside from typical localized afternoon and evening thunderstorms, no major weather disturbances are anticipated in the north.
