FAA Lifts Nationwide Ground Stop After 3,800 Saturday Flights Are Cancelled

The FAA ended a five-hour nationwide ground stop on Friday after a telecommunications outage involving legacy system routers disrupted thousands of flights.

While the federal airspace is now operating normally, the ripple effects from the five-hour ground stop continue to paralyze major travel hubs, with Delta Air Lines, United, and American reporting hundreds of cancellations each. The crisis began when an aging air traffic control system, described by Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy as "incredibly old," buckled under the weight of new security patches. Officials revealed that the trouble originated from Cisco 600 series routers manufactured between 2001 and 2006, which became overloaded with data and provided inconsistent information, forcing a total system reboot. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin confirmed the outage was caused by these technical failures, noting there was no indication of any nefarious act during the disruption. For travelers, the immediate future remains uncertain as airlines work to reposition crews and clear a backlog of thousands of delays. Secretary Duffy warned that passengers should anticipate continued disruptions as carriers scramble to reschedule flights into early next week. Major airports, including Hartsfield-Jackson in Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare, and New York's John F. Kennedy, have borne the brunt of the chaos. Flight tracking data from FlightAware and Flightradar24 indicates the total impact reached approximately 10,000 flights impacted during the initial grounding period. While safety protocols are being strictly maintained, the scale of the fallout underscores the fragility of the nation's legacy aviation infrastructure. Officials are currently monitoring the system closely to prevent a recurrence of the technical failure.
