Late cattle exporter Johnny Connaughton leaves massive estate worth over €104 million

Johnny Connaughton, the founder of the Irish Simmental Cattle Society and owner of Bloomfield House Hotel, has left an estate valued at more than €104 million.

Johnny Connaughton died in January this year at age 93, leaving behind a fortune accumulated through his diverse business interests. According to official papers published by the Probate Office in Dublin, the estate is valued at more than €104 million. His success spanned from hospitality, as the owner of the Bloomfield House Hotel near Mullingar, to his high-profile career in agriculture and bloodstock.

Connaughton held a significant reputation in the farming community as the founder breeder and a past president of the Irish Simmental Cattle Society. He was instrumental in creating a lucrative export business that shipped the breed to the United States. His legacy also extends to the equestrian world, where he was recognized as the Irish Thoroughbred Breeders’ Association small breeder of the year. His bloodstock portfolio included top-tier racehorses such as Bay City Roller and Dan Excel.

Beyond his cattle and hotel interests, Connaughton maintained residences at Barrettstown House in Dysart, Co Westmeath, as well as an address in Killiney, Co Dublin. His wide-reaching influence across these sectors made him a notable figure in both local business and the international breeding trade.

The scale of the estate reflects a career built on diverse enterprise and industry leadership. While the Probate Office has confirmed the valuation, his contributions to the Irish Simmental breed and his management of major thoroughbreds remain his defining professional achievements.
