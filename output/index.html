Hurricane Polo's remnants trigger historic moisture surge across Southwest, Plains

The former hurricane brought record-level atmospheric moisture to the Desert Southwest on Tuesday and Wednesday, flooding roads and forcing evacuations in New Mexico towns over dam failure concerns before the threat moves into Texas and Oklahoma.

Bands of rain from Hurricane Polo's remnants swept across the U.S. Southwest on Tuesday evening, causing desert rivers to surge and usually dry arroyos to rage with floodwater. The amount of moisture in the atmosphere reached near or above record levels for late September over parts of the Southwest, Texas and Oklahoma, with rainfall rates up to 2 inches per hour in some areas.

The storm's remnants battered the region after Polo struck the Mexican state of Baja California Sur on Tuesday morning with maximum sustained winds of 110 mph before weakening to a tropical storm. The flooding forced residents in small New Mexico towns to evacuate over concerns about a dam failing in south central New Mexico. People in Rincon, Salem, Garfield, Hatch and Placitas were told to seek higher ground immediately.

A meteorologist at the National Weather Service office in Dodge City, Kansas noted that heavy rains from the remnants of tropical systems are very rare across western Kansas, making this event historically significant.

The flood threat was expected to shift eastward Wednesday, expanding into Texas, Oklahoma and Colorado, with tropical moisture extending into Wisconsin and Michigan where flash flooding risk existed. The National Weather Service warned residents to avoid dry washes, slot canyons, burn scars and flooded areas, and urged people not to drive through flooded roadways.

Hegseth orders 20% cut to generals and admirals across U.S. military

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is expected to announce a 20% reduction in general and admiral positions across the U.S. military during a Wednesday address at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Virginia.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth will announce a 20% cut to generals and admirals on Wednesday at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Virginia, doubling a 10% reduction he ordered in May 2025. The latest move marks an escalation in his overhaul of the military's top ranks since taking office.

The cuts affect roughly 800 positions across one- to four-star ranks. As of July, there were 851 active-duty generals and admirals across the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force and Space Force, according to Pentagon data, below the federal law cap of 857. Cutting positions does not necessarily mean immediately dismissing everyone occupying them; some roles would be eliminated while others would be assigned to lower-ranking officers.

Hegseth has justified the reductions as necessary to "drive innovation and operational excellence" and remove "unnecessary bureaucratic layers that hinder their growth and effectiveness." The Pentagon has presented cuts as a way to reduce bureaucracy. Analysts, however, say the changes may be aimed at sidelining experienced commanders and appointing more officers loyal to President Donald Trump.

At least 20 generals, admirals and civilian military leaders have been fired or resigned since Hegseth became defense secretary, including the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the Army, Navy and Air Force's top uniformed officials. The secretaries of the Army and Navy have also been replaced. The argument for reducing senior ranks predates Hegseth, with supporters citing that overall force size has declined significantly since the Cold War without corresponding reductions in flag officers.
