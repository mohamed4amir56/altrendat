Seven Irish Counties Face Cleanup After Heavy Rain and Gusty Winds

Status Yellow wind and rain warnings expired for seven Irish counties this morning following a major weather event.

Meteorologist Mark Bowe described the situation as a double whammy, with heavy rain and very strong, gusty winds causing localized flooding and scattered debris across the country. Falling leaves and trees on roads created difficult travel conditions throughout the warning period, which stretched from southern regions to the northwest. The combined force of the elements posed significant challenges for commuters and residents in impacted areas like Cork, Kerry, Waterford, Clare, Galway, Mayo, and Sligo.

This disruption is far from over, as the incoming week promises little relief for those dealing with the aftermath. Because low-pressure systems remain anchored in the region, the weather is expected to stay unsettled through Thursday. Residents should prepare for blustery showers and inconsistent conditions that make it unlikely the weather will feel as though it has improved significantly in the near term.

According to Met Éireann, the warnings in place for the seven counties expired at 4am today. While these specific alerts have lifted, additional wind warnings for Donegal and maritime gale warnings for the Irish Sea remain active, with gale force 8 to strong gale force 9 winds expected to persist in those sectors until later this morning. The forecaster notes that while occasional sunshine may appear, the overarching theme for the coming days remains cold and unstable, with a continuous potential for further scattered showers.
