Very strong El Niño surges toward peak as authorities urge immediate preparation

Earth Sciences New Zealand forecasts one of the strongest El Niño events in recent history, set to drive dry spells and drought risks nationwide.

Livestock owners are facing the reality of potentially reduced grass growth and water scarcity as El Niño shifts weather patterns from this spring onwards. With summer just over a month away, the Ministry for Primary Industries is emphasizing that this event is expected to rank among the strongest seen in recent history.

The shifting climate driver poses a significant challenge for animal owners, particularly those managing cattle, sheep, or horses. According to the Ministry for Primary Industries, the expected conditions create a heightened risk of drought, wildfires, and heat-related health concerns. Failing to prepare for these environmental changes could result in critical shortages of feed and water, forcing owners to destock or face welfare challenges for their animals.

Andrew Curtis, the ministry's national response manager, advises that proactive measures are essential to manage these obligations under the Animal Welfare Act. Recommended steps include booking water tank refills in advance, prioritizing specific livestock for early destocking, and ensuring all animals are shorn before temperatures climb. The ministry has launched a dedicated web resource for lifestyle block owners, providing access to seasonal calendars, fact sheets, and emergency plan templates to help navigate the season.

As the phenomenon intensifies toward its peak, the weather patterns are expected to deliver dry spells to the north of the country while potentially causing heavy rain in southern regions. Farmers and property owners are encouraged to utilize available support materials now, rather than waiting for the most severe impacts to emerge during the coming summer months.
