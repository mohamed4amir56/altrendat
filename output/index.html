NHS trusts declare critical incidents as flu and Covid cases surge

Nottingham University Hospitals declared a critical incident as respiratory cases climb and emergency department waiting times soar.

At Nottingham University Hospitals, the influx of patients has pushed the emergency department to its breaking point, with roughly 200 patients currently in the department and nearly 100 individuals waiting for beds on wards. Ambulances face delays of up to four hours, while the trust reports that flu cases have jumped from 14 to 60 in a single week. The situation is equally strained in Gloucestershire, where patients face an average wait time of 13 days to be discharged, significantly higher than the 6.3-day national average.

This crisis directly impacts patient safety and the quality of care available. Andrew Hall, chief operating officer at Nottingham University Hospitals, warned that the current level of crowding carries an increased risk of harm. With hospitals struggling to move patients through the system, the lack of available beds limits the capacity for resus and major trauma care, forcing some emergency department staff in Gloucestershire to care for patients in corridors at peak times.

According to Nottingham University Hospitals, the current surge in flu cases has surpassed projections for this time of year and exceeds numbers seen at the same point in 2023. Covid-19 infections also rose from 53 to 73, compounding existing staffing pressures. Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust reports that 60 beds are occupied by flu patients, while 150 individuals who are medically fit to leave remain stuck on wards due to discharge delays.

Health officials are urging the public to use 999 and emergency departments only for life-threatening emergencies. Families are asked to assist by collecting relatives scheduled for discharge as soon as possible to help free up hospital capacity.
