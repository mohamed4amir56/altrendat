RBC blocks third-party AI agents from accessing online banking and client data

The Royal Bank of Canada is banning external AI tools from its digital properties to protect customer information from unacceptable security risks.

Customers will no longer be able to use third-party personal AI agents to access their RBC accounts, transaction history, or credit card data. The bank determined that these tools, which analyze spending patterns and make financial recommendations, create significant liability exposure and may act beyond what users expect. RBC head of technology and operations Naim Kazmi and head of personal banking Erica Nielsen stated that protecting client information is the bank's primary priority in a memo issued Tuesday.

This decision carries significant weight for financial security, as banking leaders warn that sharing passwords and sensitive credentials with AI agents could void fraud protection agreements. Bank of Montreal CEO Darryl White cautioned that providing usernames, PINs, or SINs to these autonomous entities leaves data vulnerable to bad actors. If a customer provides these credentials to an outside tool, the bank may not cover the resulting financial losses, as the action could be considered a breach of the standard user agreement.

Following a detailed security review, RBC became the first Canadian lender to implement a full block on external AI agents across its digital platforms. The move aligns RBC with major U.S. financial institutions, including JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab, which have also limited or barred such technologies. This shift occurs amid rising scrutiny from Canadian regulators regarding how artificial intelligence interacts with the country’s largest financial systems, as banks continue to manage the balance between AI innovation and data safety.
