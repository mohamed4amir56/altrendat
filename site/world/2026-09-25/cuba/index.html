U.S. Military Prepares Contingency Plans For Potential Military Action In Cuba

The U.S. military is laying groundwork and assessing force readiness for possible intervention in Cuba within 90 to 120 days, according to internal Army communications reviewed by CBS News.

The U.S. military is actively preparing contingency plans for potential military action around Cuba, according to internal communications obtained by CBS News. The U.S. Army Reserve has circulated messages asking whether military units could be ready for deployment in as little as 3 months, with specific timeframes of 90 to 120 days mentioned in the internal assessment, according to the New York Post's reporting on the same matter.

The groundwork appears to be part of broader strategic planning rather than an imminent operation. Retired Lt. Gen. H.R. McMaster, commenting on the developments, stated that the end of the Cuban regime would "be a great thing" for the Western Hemisphere, as reported by Yahoo News. McMaster's remarks suggest the discussions are connected to potential geopolitical shifts in the region.

The timing of these military preparations coincides with heightened U.S. focus on hemispheric security concerns. While officials have not publicly detailed the specific triggers or scenarios that would necessitate such action, the systematic approach to assessing troop readiness indicates the military is treating the contingency seriously. The 90-to-120-day readiness window suggests planners are considering medium-term deployment scenarios rather than immediate crisis response. The exact nature of the potential action and what circumstances would trigger it remain part of classified military planning, though the communications suggest comprehensive preparation across multiple Army Reserve units.
