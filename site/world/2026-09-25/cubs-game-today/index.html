Cubs Clinch 2026 Postseason Berth as Crow-Armstrong Achieves Historic 40-40 Season

The Chicago Cubs secured their playoff spot on Thursday at Wrigley Field, highlighted by Pete Crow-Armstrong becoming only the seventh player in MLB history to achieve 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in a single season.

The Chicago Cubs officially clinched their 2026 postseason berth on Thursday afternoon at Wrigley Field, marking a significant milestone for the franchise despite the challenging path to reaching the October stage. According to MLB.com, while the Cubs fully anticipated returning to postseason play this fall, the team did not expect the daunting and winding road required to secure the clinching moment.

The historic day was punctuated by an exceptional individual achievement from Cubs outfielder Pete Crow-Armstrong, who became the first player in Chicago Cubs franchise history to record a 40-40 season—joining an exclusive club of only seven players in MLB history. According to the Chicago Tribune, Crow-Armstrong accomplished this milestone with a stolen base in the first inning Thursday, reaching 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in the 2026 season. This rare statistical feat places him among baseball's elite performers, demonstrating exceptional versatility combining both power hitting and base-stealing ability.

The dual significance of Thursday's game—securing postseason eligibility while witnessing an individual record achievement—makes it a banner day for the Cubs organization. Crow-Armstrong's performance exemplifies the type of dynamic talent that has helped drive Chicago's successful 2026 campaign, while the postseason clinching validates the team's overall strategic approach to the season.
