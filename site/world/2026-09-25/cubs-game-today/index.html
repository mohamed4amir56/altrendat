Cubs Clinch Postseason Berth As Crow-Armstrong Achieves Historic 40-40 Season

The Chicago Cubs secured their playoff berth on Thursday at Wrigley Field, highlighted by Pete Crow-Armstrong becoming only the seventh player in MLB history to achieve a 40-home run, 40-stolen base season.

The Chicago Cubs clinched a postseason berth on Thursday afternoon at Wrigley Field, marking a significant milestone for the franchise as they return to October baseball. According to MLB.com, the Cubs fully expected to compete for a playoff spot this season, but the path to clinching proved more challenging and winding than anticipated. The dramatic achievement unfolded during the same game that saw Cubs outfielder Pete Crow-Armstrong make his own history.

In a remarkable individual performance, Crow-Armstrong stole second base in the first inning Thursday, becoming the first player in Chicago Cubs franchise history to achieve a 40-40 season—recording at least 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases in a single year. According to the Chicago Tribune, Crow-Armstrong joined an exclusive club of only seven players in MLB history to accomplish this rare feat. This elite group highlights just how difficult it is to combine power hitting with base-stealing speed at such elite levels.

The convergence of these two historic moments—the Cubs' postseason clinch and Crow-Armstrong's record-setting season—created a banner day for the franchise at their iconic home stadium. The achievement underscores the Cubs' competitive roster strength and Crow-Armstrong's exceptional all-around performance as a key contributor to the team's success this season.
