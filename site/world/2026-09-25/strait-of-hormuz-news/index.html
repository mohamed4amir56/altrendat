Iran Proposes Seven-Day Plan To Reopen Strait Of Hormuz Amid Tensions

Iran has proposed a seven-day diplomatic deal to reopen the strategically critical Strait of Hormuz if the United States agrees to conditions from a previously failed June agreement.

Iran's government has put forward a new proposal to resolve the ongoing Strait of Hormuz closure, offering to reopen the critical waterway within seven days if the United States meets specific conditions. According to CBS News, Tehran is seeking to finalize a deal before the U.S. midterm elections, signaling urgency in the negotiations. The proposal closely mirrors the Memorandum of Understanding negotiated in June, which collapsed shortly after being reached, according to the Anchorage Daily News.

Iran's Foreign Minister has explicitly requested that the U.S. return to the same conditions outlined in the June 17 memorandum, CNBC reported. The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most vital chokepoints for global oil trade, making its closure a significant concern for energy markets and international commerce. The proposal triggered immediate market reaction, with oil prices falling following the announcement of this potential diplomatic solution, as markets responded to the possibility of renewed negotiations.

The timing of Iran's proposal—aimed at reaching a deal before the U.S. midterm elections—suggests Tehran believes this political window offers an opportunity for breakthrough negotiations. However, given that the June agreement unraveled rapidly, skepticism remains about whether this new initiative will succeed where the previous effort failed. The proposal represents Tehran's latest attempt to resolve the escalating tensions through diplomatic channels rather than military confrontation.
