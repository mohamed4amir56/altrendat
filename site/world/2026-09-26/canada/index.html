📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Maine's economy is reeling from President Trump's trade war with neighboring Canada, as fishers, loggers, and manufacturers face mounting losses. The Guardian reported that anger is intensifying across the state, which maintains deep economic ties to Canada spanning decades. A concrete business owner told the Bangor Daily News that tariffs would have cost his small company approximately $150,000 monthly—a figure Senator Susan Collins reportedly helped mitigate through intervention, according to the outlet.

The damage extends far beyond Maine's borders. Fortune's analysis of automotive trade data reveals a catastrophic collapse in U.S. vehicle sales within Canada. The American market share of new cars in Canada plummeted from 35.4% to 28.4% in just one year—a stunning 7-percentage-point drop that marks a historic low. This represents a critical backfire on U.S. automakers, who face reduced demand in their second-largest export market.

Industry experts quoted by Fortune argue that Trump's tariff strategy is fundamentally damaging American automotive competitiveness. As Canadian consumers pivot toward non-American vehicles, manufacturers from Detroit to smaller suppliers face production cutbacks and layoffs. The escalating trade tensions now loom large over the midterm electoral landscape, with Maine voters signaling potential displeasure over economic hardship tied directly to administration trade policy.
