📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Wisconsin voters are confronting three consequential constitutional amendments this November that could reshape the state's foundational governing structure. According to FOX6 News Milwaukee, the statewide referendums touch on pivotal issues spanning religion, taxes, and race—areas traditionally reserved for legislative debate.

Question 2, the most contentious amendment, has drawn significant public commentary. The Wisconsin State Journal reported that the measure involves religious freedom provisions, prompting prominent voices like Annie Laurie Gaylor to urge voters to reject it. Gaylor argued that citizens shouldn't have to choose between religious freedom and public safety, advocating for a "no" vote to preserve existing protections for both individual rights and community welfare.

A second amendment addresses gubernatorial power directly. According to wjfw.com, one proposed constitutional change would restrict the governor's partial veto authority—a significant constraint on executive flexibility in budget and legislative matters. This amendment has emerged as voters approach Election Day within less than six weeks, with the gubernatorial race itself dominating the ballot.

The third amendment involves tax-related constitutional language, though specific details remain complex. Together, these three referendums represent a rare moment of direct constitutional intervention by Wisconsin's electorate. Each amendment requires majority voter approval to pass, making November's ballot a critical inflection point for Wisconsin governance. Election officials and advocacy groups across the political spectrum are actively mobilizing voters to understand the implications before casting their ballots.
