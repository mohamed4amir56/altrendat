📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

A strong and shallow earthquake with a magnitude of 6.6 struck the Loyalty Islands, located off the coast of New Caledonia, on September 25, 2026, at 21:23 UTC, according to the USGS. The Watchers reported that the seismic event occurred at a depth of just 10 kilometers (6.2 miles), making it a relatively shallow quake in the tectonically active South Pacific region.

Despite the significant magnitude, authorities swiftly assessed the situation and determined there was no tsunami threat following the tremor, according to Maui Now. The Loyalty Islands, part of New Caledonia's territory, sit in a seismically volatile zone where tectonic plate movements frequently trigger earthquakes. The shallow depth of this particular event—typical for the region's geological structure—contributed to its being felt across a wide area, though damage reports remain limited.

Additionally, a separate 5.1 magnitude earthquake was recorded in New Caledonia around the same timeframe, according to Qatar News Agency (QNA), suggesting heightened seismic activity in the broader region. The successive tremors underscore the ongoing geological instability characterizing this portion of the Pacific Basin, where the Australian and Pacific plates continuously interact. Residents and authorities in New Caledonia and neighboring island territories remain vigilant for further aftershocks, though the initial all-clear from tsunami monitoring agencies provided reassurance to coastal communities in the aftermath of the primary event.
