Mets-Nationals Sunday Showdown Moved Up Two Hours Due to Threatening Weather

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

The Washington Nationals and New York Mets will kick off their Sunday matchup two hours earlier than originally planned, with first pitch now set for 1:05 p.m. ET instead of the scheduled 3:05 p.m. ET start, according to MLB.com. The decision comes as expected severe weather threatens the region, potentially creating dangerous playing conditions if the game were to proceed at its original time.

All gates at the stadium will open at 11:45 a.m. ET to accommodate the earlier start time. According to MLB.com, fans who have already purchased tickets for the 3:05 p.m. slot need not take any action—their tickets remain fully valid for the rescheduled 1:05 p.m. first pitch.

The Mets are turning to pitcher Sean Manaea to take the mound in hopes of securing a series victory and capping off their regular season on a positive note, according to Yahoo Sports. Capital Weather reported that shower chances increase significantly as Sunday afternoon progresses, making the earlier start time a strategic move to complete the full nine innings before conditions deteriorate.

This scheduling adjustment reflects MLB's commitment to player safety and game integrity, ensuring that neither team is disadvantaged by weather-induced delays or postponements. Fans planning to attend should note the new gates-open time of 11:45 a.m. and arrive accordingly for what could be a pivotal final weekend contest.
