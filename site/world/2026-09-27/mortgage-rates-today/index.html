Mortgage Rates Surge Past 7.45%, Hitting Highest Level Since April 2024

By Karim Fouad · Published 27 Sep 2026, 05:30 UTC

Thirty-year fixed mortgage rates have jumped sharply to 7.45%, marking the steepest climb in months as bond yields soar and the already-frozen housing market faces another crushing blow.

Mortgage rates have reached their highest point in over five months, according to CNBC, as the 30-year fixed rate surged to 7.45% on Thursday. This represents a significant spike driven by a broader selloff in bonds and rising Treasury yields, creating fresh headwinds for an already stalled housing market.

The escalation extends beyond this single day. CNN reported that rates have topped the psychologically significant 7% threshold, further deepening the crisis in an already frozen real estate sector. The combination of elevated rates and limited housing inventory has squeezed both buyers and sellers, with affordability reaching crisis levels across major markets.

This surge reflects broader economic pressures, including persistent inflation concerns and the Federal Reserve's restrictive monetary stance. Higher mortgage rates directly reduce purchasing power for homebuyers, as monthly payments climb substantially on fixed-income budgets. A borrower seeking a $400,000 home now faces monthly payments that are hundreds of dollars higher than they would have been at lower rate environments.

The housing market has already absorbed significant strain from previous rate hikes. Construction activity has declined, home sales have weakened, and prospective buyers have been priced out entirely in many regions. Real estate professionals warn that continued rate elevation could trigger further market contractions, potentially destabilizing an already fragile sector of the American and global economy.
