📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

New York's tax debate just shifted dramatically. Contrary to warnings that aggressive taxation would trigger a mass exodus of millionaires, Fortune reports that the wealthy have largely remained in New York City, San Francisco, and Los Angeles despite unfriendly business climates.

The data undermines one of the financial elite's most potent arguments against higher taxes. "Taxes aren't really high enough for them to be sensitive," sources told Fortune, suggesting wealthy residents were never as flight-prone as feared.

City Comptroller Mark Levine seized on this momentum, releasing a report this month that frames New York as "millionaire friendly"—a striking rebranding that New York Daily News notes contradicts ordinary New Yorkers' struggles. Food, rent, and essential costs are climbing faster than most workers' salaries, creating sharp inequality even as the wealthy remain.

The political calculation is clear: if millionaires won't leave despite higher taxes, progressives argue New York has room to hike top tax rates. City Journal reports the state is now pushing for increased levies on top earners, framing the move as justified economic policy.

This represents a turning point in New York's tax politics. Officials previously held back, fearing capital flight. With that fear now empirically challenged, the wealthy face mounting pressure to contribute more to a state where their presence persists but ordinary residents face a deepening affordability crisis.
