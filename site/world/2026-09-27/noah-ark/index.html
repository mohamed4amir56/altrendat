Archaeologists have launched a dramatic investigation into a boat-shaped mound discovered near Mount Ararat in Eastern Turkey, asserting that fresh evidence points definitively to human construction. According to Yahoo News, the research team claims the site is "100%" man-made, reigniting the centuries-old debate over whether physical remnants of Noah's Ark exist.

Drilling operations have commenced at the remote location to gather subsurface data that could validate the team's hypothesis. Fox News reports that archaeologists are working to establish whether this mound represents the actual final resting place of the legendary vessel described in biblical and Islamic texts.

The boat-shaped geological formation has long captivated researchers and believers alike, with Mount Ararat's proximity lending credibility to historical accounts. The new investigative push represents one of the most substantive archaeological efforts to examine the site systematically. The team's declaration of "100% man-made" construction challenges previous theories that dismissed the mound as a natural formation.

While skepticism persists within mainstream archaeology regarding Noah's Ark claims, the ongoing drilling campaign and structural analysis could yield definitive answers about the mound's origins and composition. The research unfolds amid renewed global interest in the site, as documented by the surge in public searches regarding Noah's Ark. Authorities in Turkey continue to permit excavation work at the location, recognizing both its archaeological and cultural significance to multiple religious traditions worldwide.
