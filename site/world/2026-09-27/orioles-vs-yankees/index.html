📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

In a weather-driven scheduling pivot, the New York Yankees and Major League Baseball coordinated to advance the start time of Sunday's regular-season finale against the Baltimore Orioles to 1:05 p.m. ET, according to MLB.com. The decision came as a powerful nor'easter swept through the tri-state region, threatening to disrupt play with inclement conditions throughout the late afternoon and evening, as reported by the New York Daily News.

The game, originally slated for its standard time slot, now begins three hours earlier to provide both teams and fans a window before deteriorating weather arrives. This type of proactive scheduling adjustment is common in professional baseball when forecasters predict significant atmospheric interference that could delay or postpone competition.

The Yankees' strategic move reflects MLB's commitment to completing regular-season contests on schedule while prioritizing player safety and fan experience. By moving the game into midday hours, the league aims to secure a full nine innings before conditions become dangerous for on-field play.

The matchup holds added significance as a season finale between two AL East competitors, making the weather adjustment particularly consequential for both franchises' final regular-season positioning heading into the postseason stretch.
