📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Venezuela has released 39 political prisoners in a major development following negotiations between the interim government and opposition leaders, according to Al Jazeera. The releases occurred after a second round of talks aimed at stabilizing the South American nation following the departure of longtime leader Nicolás Maduro.

Human rights organizations in Venezuela confirmed the prisoner releases, which took place on Saturday, according to the Livingston Enterprise. The exact circumstances surrounding the releases remain partially unclear, though sources indicate the negotiations involved key political figures working toward reconciliation and democratic reforms.

The releases represent a significant humanitarian milestone for Venezuela, a country that has faced international scrutiny over its treatment of political detainees. The interim government's decision to free the prisoners signals a potential shift toward addressing human rights concerns that plagued previous administrations.

These negotiations reflect broader efforts to establish political dialogue and institutional reform in Venezuela following recent political upheaval. The involvement of multiple stakeholders—including international observers and opposition representatives—suggests a coordinated approach to resolving longstanding tensions within the country's political landscape.

While the total number of political prisoners still held in Venezuela remains undisclosed, the release of this cohort demonstrates tangible progress in confidence-building measures between competing political factions, according to reports from Al Jazeera and the Livingston Enterprise. Observers view the prisoner releases as a positive indicator for future negotiations and potential reconciliation efforts in the troubled nation.
