📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

The Dallas Cowboys head to Rio de Janeiro on Sunday for the NFL's inaugural game in Brazil, facing a Baltimore Ravens team led by dynamic quarterback Lamar Jackson in what promises to be a defensive nightmare for Dallas.

Cowboys owner Jerry Jones has already acknowledged the mismatch, admitting his defense faces a "bad matchup" against Jackson's dual-threat abilities, according to Yahoo Sports. The concern is well-founded: Dallas has repeatedly failed to contain mobile quarterbacks early in the season. In Week 1, Giants quarterback Jaxson Dart rushed for 54 yards against the Cowboys defense. Washington's Jayden Daniels then added 69 rushing yards in a subsequent matchup, illustrating a troubling pattern of vulnerability.

The Ravens bring more firepower than just Jackson's legs. Running back Derrick Henry, acquired to bolster Baltimore's ground game, is poised to test a Cowboys defense that has already shown susceptibility to the run, according to NFL.com's preview analysis. Henry's power-running style could prove punishing against a Dallas unit still finding its footing this season.

The historic venue adds intrigue to an already significant matchup. Playing in Brazil represents uncharted territory for the NFL, with the league making its first-ever regular-season appearance in South America. The unique circumstances of international travel and an unfamiliar environment could amplify both teams' challenges, though the Ravens appear positioned as the favorites given Dallas's defensive struggles against exactly the type of offense Baltimore is built to deploy.
