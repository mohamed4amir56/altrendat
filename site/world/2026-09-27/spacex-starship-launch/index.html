SpaceX Starship Launches to Orbit for First Time in Historic Megarocket Test

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

SpaceX is preparing for a watershed moment in spaceflight history as its Starship megarocket undertakes its most ambitious test yet—a full orbital launch scheduled for Monday, September 28, at 8:15 a.m. EDT (12:15 GMT), according to Space.com.

The mission represents the pinnacle of engineering after 13 previous test flights, as documented by BBC coverage of Starship's development arc. Each prior test has progressively pushed the boundaries of what the fully-stacked vehicle can achieve, from early pad aborts to increasingly complex flight sequences.

CNN reports that this is "the most powerful rocket ever built" and describes the upcoming test as both the biggest and riskiest challenge the program has faced. The orbital attempt carries enormous stakes for SpaceX's long-term vision of establishing human settlements on Mars and revolutionizing space transportation.

The first orbital flight will validate critical systems including full-stack integration, engine performance at scale, stage separation, and reentry dynamics. Success would demonstrate that SpaceX has overcome the extraordinary technical hurdles inherent in operating a 400-foot-tall vehicle with unprecedented thrust.

Failure remains a statistical possibility given the test's complexity, but each previous flight has yielded invaluable data. The aerospace community and space enthusiasts worldwide are closely monitoring this milestone, which could fundamentally reshape humanity's approach to deep-space exploration and interplanetary travel.
