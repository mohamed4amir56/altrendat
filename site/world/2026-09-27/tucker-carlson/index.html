📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Tucker Carlson, the prominent conservative commentator formerly of Fox News, has dramatically reconsidered his relationship with President Trump and the broader MAGA movement, according to NPR's Newsmakers interview. The rupture centers on U.S. foreign policy toward Iran, which Carlson has opposed publicly and privately.

Carlson recounted an Oval Office meeting that occurred before military tensions escalated, claiming he directly warned Trump that the U.S. was being drawn into war with Iran by Israeli influence. According to Carlson's account reported by NPR, Trump allegedly responded with a fatalistic dismissal: "Yes, you're right. But in the end we all die anyway, so it doesn't matter." Carlson called this response "nihilism" and expressed shock at what he characterized as a dangerous worldview for any leader.

When NPR requested comment, the White House did not address Carlson's specific allegations. Instead, it issued a social media post from the president describing Carlson and others sharing his views as "stupid people" who want Iran to possess nuclear weapons.

The rift has intensified further. According to The Independent, Carlson has escalated his criticism, now arguing that Trump's Cabinet should invoke the 25th Amendment to remove the president from office, citing threats of nuclear action against Iran as the justification. In his NPR interview, Carlson also criticized both major political parties, asserting they have fundamentally failed the American people. His comments mark a significant public departure from his previous Trump alignment and reflect deeper ideological disagreements on war, leadership, and governance.
