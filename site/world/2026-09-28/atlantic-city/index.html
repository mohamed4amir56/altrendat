Atlantic City's Iconic Sheraton Hotel Faces December Closure Without State Rescue

By Karim Fouad · Published 28 Sep 2026, 11:00 UTC

The Sheraton Atlantic City Convention Center Hotel teeters on the brink of closure as its developer declares convention business alone cannot sustain operations without a state bailout or partial residential conversion.

Atlantic City's convention infrastructure faces a critical juncture as the Sheraton hotel owner warns that traditional business models no longer work. According to WHYY, the developer behind a proposed $100 million overhaul states that partial residential conversion is the only viable path forward without direct state financial support. The hotel risks shuttering in December, jeopardizing both the venue and the city's fragile economic recovery centered on casino and tourism revenue.

The stakes extend beyond one property. CDC Gaming reported that a Casino Reinvestment Development Authority official warned Governor Phil Murphy that building a competing convention center in the nearby Meadowlands could prove catastrophic for Atlantic City's already-strained hospitality sector. "Imagine the devastation," the official declared, highlighting fears that duplicate facilities would cannibalize Atlantic City's convention business.

Focus Gaming News noted that board members of the Casino Reinvestment Development Authority are actively seeking a state-backed rescue plan. The proposed solution—converting portions of the Sheraton into residential units—represents a dramatic strategic pivot for a property historically dependent on transient business travelers and conference attendees. Without intervention, Atlantic City risks losing a cornerstone asset that has anchored its convention ecosystem for decades, further eroding the city's ability to compete for lucrative event bookings and corporate conferences.
