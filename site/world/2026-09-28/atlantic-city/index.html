Atlantic City's Iconic Sheraton Hotel Faces December Closure Without State Rescue

⚡ Real-Time Coverage & Breaking Insight

The Sheraton Atlantic City Convention Center Hotel risks permanent closure this December unless state officials intervene with emergency funding or subsidies to save the struggling property.

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Atlantic City's convention infrastructure faces an existential crisis as the Sheraton hotel owner warns that traditional business models can no longer sustain the property. According to WHYY, the developer spearheading a proposed $100 million overhaul states that partial residential conversion represents the only viable path forward without direct state financial backing.

The stakes have intensified as officials sound alarms about competing threats. CDC Gaming reported that an Atlantic City official told Governor Phil Murphy that a new convention center in the Meadowlands would trigger devastating consequences for the city's existing convention economy, potentially forcing both the Atlantic City Convention Center and its adjacent Sheraton hotel into closure.

The Sheraton's December deadline has triggered urgent action. According to Focus Gaming News, a Casino Reinvestment Development Authority board member is actively seeking a comprehensive state rescue plan to explore all available options for keeping the property operational. The hotel, which anchors Atlantic City's convention business, has become a flashpoint in debates over the city's economic future.

Without intervention, industry insiders warn the closures would cripple Atlantic City's ability to attract large conventions and conferences—a revenue stream crucial to the city's casino economy and employment base. State officials now face mounting pressure to either fund the Sheraton's transformation or block competing projects like the proposed Meadowlands convention center that could accelerate the hotel's decline. The outcome will determine whether Atlantic City can maintain its relevance as a convention destination or surrender that market entirely.
