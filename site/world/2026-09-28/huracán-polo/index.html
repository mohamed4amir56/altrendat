Hurricane Polo Weakens But Threatens Mexico and Southwest U.S. Coasts

⚡ Real-Time Coverage & Breaking Insight
🔥 Inside the Story: What Just Happened

The remnants of a once-fearsome Category 5 hurricane are now set to drench Mexico's Baja California region while triggering dangerous flooding and coastal hazards across the southwestern United States.

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Hurricane Polo, which previously achieved Category 5 status, is on track to strike Baja California on Monday, according to Yale Climate Connections. As the storm system weakens, its moisture-laden remnants pose a dual threat: beneficial rainfall for drought-stressed regions alongside hazardous flash-flooding risk across Mexico and U.S. border areas.

Southern California beach cities are already mobilizing defenses against the tropical system's impact. CBS News reports that weather officials have issued a coastal flood advisory for the region, signaling preparations for potential surge and dangerous wave conditions. The advisory underscores heightened concern about storm surge vulnerability in populated coastal zones from San Diego northward.

The National Weather Service and regional meteorologists are emphasizing the paradox of Polo's remnants: while the widespread rainfall will provide much-needed moisture to arid Southwest landscapes, the same precipitation patterns create acute flash-flood danger in susceptible canyon areas and desert basins. According to Yale Climate Connections, the storm's approach is already influencing weather patterns in the Northeast and New England, demonstrating the far-reaching atmospheric effects of tropical systems.

Meteorologist Amber Lee's forecast indicates residents should prepare for gusty winds, heavy downpours, and potential power outages in coastal communities. Emergency management officials are urging residents to secure outdoor items, clear storm drains, and finalize evacuation plans if required.
