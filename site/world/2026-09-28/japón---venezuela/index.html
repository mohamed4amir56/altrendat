📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Japan and Venezuela squared off in an international friendly match on Monday, September 28, in Hiroshima, marking a significant test for both squads. According to Telemundo, the match kicked off at 6:15 AM ET, drawing major viewership across the Americas. OneFootball reported that Japan's attack centered on Takefusa Kubo, one of Asia's most dynamic offensive talents, facing a Venezuelan side led by midfield anchors Yangel Herrera and Jon Mikel Aramburu. The encounter took place in Hiroshima at 12:25 local time, according to OneFootball's reporting. Telemundo noted this fixture served as a crucial test for Venezuela's squad renovation project, pitting the South American side against a Japanese team reshaping its competitive identity. The Copa Kirin tournament provided the stage for both nations to evaluate their progress ahead of upcoming continental competitions. Japan's reliance on Kubo's creativity reflected the team's attacking philosophy, while Venezuela's inclusion of Herrera and Aramburu underscored the squad's midfield strength. The match represented a meaningful international window opportunity for both federations to assess player development and tactical cohesion ahead of critical qualifying campaigns.
