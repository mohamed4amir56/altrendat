Stock Market Tumbles As Oil Surges And Bond Yields Hit Fresh Peaks

By Karim Fouad · Published 28 Sep 2026, 14:30 UTC

U.S. stocks fell sharply Monday as climbing oil prices and soaring Treasury yields ignited fresh rate-hike concerns ahead of next week's critical Fed decision.

Markets opened weak Monday as a confluence of economic headwinds pressured equities across the board. CNBC reported that stocks fell, weighed down by a sharp jump in oil prices and Treasury yields to start the trading week. The moves signaled investor anxiety over inflation and monetary policy as the economic calendar tightens.

According to Yahoo Finance, long-dated bond yields climbed substantially amid surging crude prices, sparking concerns that the Federal Reserve may need to raise rates at its policy meeting scheduled for next week. The yield surge has become a central flashpoint for market sentiment, with investors reassessing their positioning ahead of the Fed's critical announcement.

Notably, not all equities moved in lockstep with the broader selloff. Yahoo Finance highlighted that Nvidia stock bucked the downward trend and rose during the session, suggesting selective strength in mega-cap technology names despite the challenging macro backdrop. This divergence underscores how energy costs and interest-rate expectations are reshaping portfolio allocations across different sectors.

The combination of elevated oil prices and climbing Treasury yields has reignited debate about stagflation risks and whether policymakers can navigate the current economic environment without triggering a sharper downturn. Traders are now laser-focused on next week's Fed outcome as a potential inflection point for markets.
