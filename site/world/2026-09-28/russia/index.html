Russia's Jet-Powered Drones Now Attack Ukraine by Thousands With Turbojet Speed

⚡ Real-Time Coverage & Breaking Insight
🔥 Inside the Story: What Just Happened

Russia has dramatically expanded production of advanced turbojet-powered Geran-4 drones that fly twice as fast as earlier models, overwhelming Ukrainian air defenses with thousands of attacks as access to global supply chain components surges.

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Russia has achieved a major tactical breakthrough by mass-producing high-speed jet-powered drones that are fundamentally changing the aerial warfare dynamic over Ukraine. According to Business Insider, Russia's once-rare turbojet Shaheds are now attacking Ukraine by the thousands, signaling the country has greatly expanded access to vital parts Ukraine said could only be sourced on the global market.

The Geran-4 represents a significant technological leap. Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty conducted a technical breakdown of the jet-powered UAV, revealing it features a turbojet engine, advanced camera systems, and foreign-made communications and navigation components that enable it to operate deep inside Ukrainian territory at speeds roughly double earlier drone variants. This combination makes the Geran-4 faster, harder to intercept, and capable of penetrating Ukrainian airspace with greater lethality.

The surge in attacks underscores Russia's expanded industrial capacity and international supply chain access. Ukrinform reported that Ukraine's air defense forces neutralized 134 drones in a single operation since the evening of September 25, demonstrating both the volume of attacks and Ukraine's defensive efforts. However, the sheer scale of drone deployments—now in the thousands—strains Ukrainian air defense resources and illustrates Russia's ability to sustain massive attrition rates.

Military analysts warn this represents a critical vulnerability in Western supply-chain security, as components for advanced Russian weaponry continue flowing from international markets despite sanctions. The Geran-4's proliferation signals Russia intends to sustain high-intensity drone operations indefinitely.
