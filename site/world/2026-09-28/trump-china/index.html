Trump Proposes Selling U.S. Weapons to China in Xi Jinping Meeting

⚡ Real-Time Coverage & Breaking Insight

Former President Trump reportedly asked China's leader Xi Jinping whether Beijing would purchase American weapons, a stunning proposal that would reverse decades of U.S. military policy protecting Taiwan.

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Former President Donald Trump proposed selling U.S. weapons to China during a conversation with President Xi Jinping, according to remarks made by David Perdue, the American ambassador to China, as reported by both The Guardian and NBC News. The extraordinary offer would fundamentally overturn generations of established U.S. foreign policy that has restricted advanced weaponry sales to Beijing to protect the self-ruled island of Taiwan. Perdue's disclosure came shortly after a Taiwanese defense minister publicly warned of the "severe" military threat posed by China to the island nation, underlining the geopolitical sensitivity of any such arms arrangement. The proposal, if realized, would represent a dramatic departure from the carefully calibrated stance the United States has maintained since establishing diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China in 1979. NBC News highlighted that such a move would constitute an unprecedented reversal of longstanding strategic principles governing U.S.-China relations and Washington's security commitments to Taiwan. The remarks from Perdue signal a significant shift in Trump's approach to China policy, suggesting a willingness to consider unconventional diplomatic and commercial arrangements with Beijing. Taiwan's leadership has grown increasingly concerned about cross-strait military capabilities, making the timing of Trump's reported proposal particularly contentious. The development raises critical questions about future U.S. defense strategy in the Indo-Pacific region and the potential ramifications for American allies dependent on Washington's security guarantees in the region.
