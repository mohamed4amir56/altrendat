📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

In a remarkable departure from established US-China relations, Donald Trump reportedly proposed selling American weapons directly to China during conversations with President Xi Jinping, according to David Perdue, the American ambassador cited by The Guardian and NBC News.

Perdue's public remarks represent an extraordinary revelation about Trump's approach to one of the world's most sensitive geopolitical relationships. The proposal, if implemented, would fundamentally overturn nearly 50 years of consistent US policy that has prohibited direct weapons sales to mainland China while simultaneously arming Taiwan, the self-ruled island that Beijing claims as its own territory.

The timing of these revelations is particularly significant. NBC News reports that such an arms sale "would upend generations of U.S. policy toward China and Taiwan." The disclosure comes amid heightened tensions following remarks from a Taiwanese minister about the "severe" military threat Beijing poses to the island, according to The Guardian.

The proposal underscores Trump's unpredictable approach to major powers and his willingness to discard longstanding diplomatic frameworks. Taiwan's security depends substantially on American military support, making any shift in US weapons policy toward China a matter of profound concern for both regional allies and US strategic interests. The Biden administration and international observers have expressed alarm at the potential consequences of such a dramatic policy reversal, which could embolden Chinese military action against Taiwan and destabilize the broader Indo-Pacific region.
