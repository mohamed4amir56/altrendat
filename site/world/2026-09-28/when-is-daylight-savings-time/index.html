Daylight Saving Time Ends November 1: States Push For Permanent Shift

⚡ Real-Time Coverage & Breaking Insight
🔥 Inside the Story: What Just Happened

As clocks fall back this November, multiple U.S. states including Ohio and South Carolina are intensifying efforts to abandon the twice-yearly time change and adopt permanent daylight saving time.

📰 Full Coverage & Verified Details:

Daylight saving time concludes on November 1, 2024, when Americans set their clocks back one hour, marking the end of extended evening daylight that began in March. However, this seasonal shift has sparked renewed debate over whether the practice should continue at all.

Several states are actively pursuing permanent daylight saving time legislation. South Carolina has long sought to lock in extended daylight year-round, and according to the Post and Courier, time is running out this year as federal and state lawmakers weigh the proposal. The state has made multiple attempts to secure permanent daylight saving time, reflecting growing frustration with biannual clock changes.

Ohio faces similar considerations. FOX 8 News examined how permanent daylight saving time would fundamentally alter winter conditions across the state, with significantly darker mornings during critical school and work commute hours. The shift would mean sunrise times pushing closer to 8 a.m. or later during winter months, raising concerns about public safety and child visibility.

The push for permanence reflects broader national frustration. Proponents argue eliminating the twice-yearly adjustment reduces confusion and health disruptions associated with time changes. Critics counter that permanent daylight saving time would leave winter mornings dangerously dark, potentially impacting student safety and productivity.

Federal law currently allows states to adopt permanent standard time, but permanent daylight saving time requires congressional approval under the Uniform Time Act. As November 1 approaches, these legislative battles underscore the ongoing tension between convenience and seasonal reality.
