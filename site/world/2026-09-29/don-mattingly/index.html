Phillies snatch playoff ticket on final day after near-historic collapse

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Sep 2026, 21:00 UTC

The Philadelphia Phillies beat Tampa Bay 7-3 on the final day of the 2026 regular season to clinch a postseason spot, avoiding becoming the first team since 1903 to blow a four-game lead with five games remaining.

The Phillies nearly joined one of baseball's worst collapses. With five games left, they held a four-game lead over Arizona for the final NL Wild Card spot—a margin no team had blown since postseason play began in 1903. Then the Phillies lost four straight while Arizona won four straight, forcing a do-or-die matchup against Tampa Bay on Sunday.

For Philadelphia fans, the ghosts of 1964 loomed large. That year's Phillies team squandered a 6½-game lead with 12 games to play, costing owner John Middleton his childhood dream of a World Series appearance. This collapse felt like it could repeat history.

But the Phillies delivered. They beat Tampa Bay 7-3 to punch their postseason ticket and avoid immortality alongside those 1964 Phillies. Zack Wheeler pitched six innings of one-run ball, Kyle Schwarber drove in two runs with two hits, and Bryce Harper homered. After scoring just three runs in their previous four losses, seven runs felt like a breakthrough.

The Phillies started 2026 at 9-19, costing manager Rob Thomson his job on April 28. Don Mattingly took over, and the team rebounded dramatically, posting one of baseball's best records for months before stumbling down the stretch. The clincher marked their fifth consecutive postseason appearance and a fresh start heading into October, with Bryce Harper noting that everything resets in the playoffs.
