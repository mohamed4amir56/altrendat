OpenAI prepares to launch Aeon agent as rivals dominate consumer AI race

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Sep 2026, 17:15 UTC

OpenAI will likely announce a consumer-facing AI agent called Aeon at its DevDay event on September 29, as competitors like Meta's Muse and Google's Gemini Spark have already gained millions of users.

Meta's Muse agent has topped the App Store charts and reached 600,000 daily active users in the US since its release earlier this month, while Google's Gemini Spark boasts upwards of 30 external service partners including Dropbox, Uber, and Spotify. OpenAI currently stands as the last major holdout without a dedicated, consumer-facing agent product, despite being the engine of the LLM revolution.

AI agents represent one of the industry's hottest categories—platforms capable of booking travel, buying groceries, scheduling appointments, and handling work tasks. Competing agents are already filling out paperwork for doctor visits, canceling subscriptions, organizing group trips, and paying bills. For OpenAI, the shift matters because the era of the general-purpose API is receding, replaced by a more aggressive paradigm: the opinionated agent that dictates how users interact with software.

Rumors suggest OpenAI will release Aeon as its answer to Meta's Muse, SpaceX's Grok Bot, and others. To succeed, Aeon would need to combine the usefulness of open-source OpenClaw, the affordability of Meta's Muse, and the simplicity of emerging competitors like Instinct—while solving the serious security risks that have plagued agents since OpenClaw's rise. On September 28, OpenAI retired four legacy models and pushed developers toward gpt-5.6-terra, a structural move clearing the path for a more unified, agent-centric architecture.

Sam Altman takes the stage at 10 a.m. PT on September 29 for the opening keynote, which will be livestreamed for those unable to attend in person.
