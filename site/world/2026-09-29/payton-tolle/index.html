Payton Tolle starts Game 1 as third-youngest Red Sox playoff pitcher ever

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Sep 2026, 11:46 UTC

The Boston Red Sox named 23-year-old Payton Tolle to start Game 1 of their American League Wild Card series against the Yankees on Tuesday at Yankee Stadium, making him the third-youngest pitcher to open a Red Sox playoff game.

At 23 years and 332 days, Tolle will join an exclusive historical group the moment he takes the mound. Only Smoky Joe Wood (22 years and 349 days in 1912) and Babe Ruth (23 years and 211 days in 1918) have been younger when starting a Red Sox playoff opener, according to MLB.com.

Tolle's selection for this spotlight comes after a breakthrough 2026 season in his first full year in the majors. He posted a 9-6 record across 26 starts with a 3.03 ERA, striking out 171 batters over 148.2 innings. He walked just 40 hitters and allowed only 15 home runs while maintaining a 1.10 WHIP—numbers that suggest he can hold his own against a potent Yankees lineup.

The Red Sox drafted Tolle in the second round of the 2024 MLB Draft. Along with fellow starters Sonny Gray and Ranger Suarez, he anchors a rotation that represents a significant upgrade from last season, when the team's pitching collapsed after losing Garrett Crochet. The best-of-three series begins Tuesday at Yankee Stadium.
