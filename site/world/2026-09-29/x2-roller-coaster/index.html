Six Flags closes X2 roller coaster permanently after years of reported injuries and deaths

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Sep 2026, 17:15 UTC

Six Flags Magic Mountain has permanently closed the X2 roller coaster following a history of injuries and deaths, with the park announcing the decision on Tuesday after a temporary closure in July.

The X2 at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California, will not operate again. The ride's seats spin riders 360 degrees forward and backward while positioned on the side of the track, leaving their feet dangling as they plunge headfirst toward the ground.

The closure follows multiple lawsuits from guests who suffered brain injuries after riding X2. Christopher Hawley, 22, died in 2022 shortly after riding the coaster when he lost consciousness after disembarking. Hilda Farias developed a brain bleed that led to her death after riding in 2010, and at least two other riders filed suits claiming traumatic brain injuries in 2020 and 2021, according to court records.

Brian Oerding, Six Flags Magic Mountain president, said in a statement: "While X2 consistently passed a multitude of safety tests, we have decided to close the ride because we believe it's the right thing to do. Ride safety is a cornerstone of our business, and when we see guest confidence affected, we take it seriously." The park temporarily closed X2 in July following reports of back-to-back brain injuries before announcing the permanent shutdown.

X2 opened as X in 2002 but was closed months later to repair design flaws. The ride was updated and reopened as X2 in 2008 with lighter trains following another closure in 2007. It had become one of Magic Mountain's main attractions and was noted by ride enthusiasts for its distinctive design allowing riders' seats to spin independently from the track.
