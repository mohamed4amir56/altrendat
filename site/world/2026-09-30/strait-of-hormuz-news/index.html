Iran reviews US ceasefire proposal as attacks hit Strait of Hormuz tankers

By Karim Fouad · Published 30 Sep 2026, 14:07 UTC · Updated 30 Sep 2026, 15:01 UTC

Iran's leadership is reviewing a U.S. counterproposal for a seven-day ceasefire, while three commercial vessels including an oil tanker were hit by unknown projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday.

Quick facts

Ceasefire duration proposed
Seven days
Strait of Hormuz share of global oil and LNG
Roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas
Conflict start date
Late February
Vessels hit Wednesday
Three commercial vessels (oil tanker and LNG tanker reported)

Three commercial vessels, including an oil tanker and a liquefied natural gas tanker, were struck by unknown projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran reviewed the latest U.S. proposal aimed at ending the conflict. The timing underscored the high stakes for global energy markets, as the waterway previously carried roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas.

Iran has demanded sanctions relief for its oil sector, the release of frozen assets, an end to the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports, and a seven-day ceasefire across the region. The U.S. counterproposal calls for a seven-day ceasefire, though it remains unclear whether Washington has accepted Iran's broader conditions tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi presented the U.S. proposal to President Masoud Pezeshkian during a Cabinet meeting on Wednesday.

Iran has maintained a blockade on commercial shipping through the strategic waterway since the conflict began in late February, while the U.S. has sought to restrict Iran's oil exports through a counterblockade of its ports. Qatari mediators have been shuttling messages between the sides, though negotiations have made limited progress as the two countries remain divided over key concessions. The counterproposal follows talks in New York last week involving Araghchi and U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner.
