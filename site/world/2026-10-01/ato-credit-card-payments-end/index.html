Reserve Bank surcharge ban ends extra card fees on retail purchases

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 13:00 UTC

New Reserve Bank of Australia reforms effective October 1 prohibit extra checkout fees on credit, debit, and prepaid card transactions.

While consumers no longer face added fees at checkout, some merchants are responding by raising base prices, as reported by PerthNow. For instance, one cafe owner in Perth lifted the price of a 12-ounce coffee from $6 to $6.50 following the mandate, noting that bank charges remain a cost for businesses to absorb or pass on. The Reserve Bank estimates that consumers previously paid $1.6 billion annually in surcharges, and it forecasts that lower merchant processing costs will eventually flow through to final prices.

The reform is significant because it aims to provide price certainty, ensuring that the sticker price displayed is the amount a customer pays. As noted by the SMH, the ban covers major networks including Visa, Mastercard, Eftpos, and American Express. Businesses are now required to disable surcharge functions on payment terminals and remove any existing notices about additional card fees from their stores.

According to the ABC, banks are simultaneously reducing interchange fees—the costs paid between banks—to a flat 0.3 per cent for consumer credit cards and lowering ceilings for debit and prepaid cards. As banks seek to recoup lost revenue, financial expert Nicole Pedersen-McKinnon warns that cardholders may see changes to reward points, increased annual fees, and the removal of perks like complimentary travel insurance. Banks are required to notify customers of these product updates directly.

While payment providers have disabled surcharge functions on most terminals, the SMH reports that some older machines may not update automatically, potentially delaying the removal of surcharges at specific locations.
