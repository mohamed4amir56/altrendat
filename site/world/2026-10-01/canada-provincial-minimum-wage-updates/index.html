Minimum wage surges in 5 provinces as pay rates climb nationwide

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 16:31 UTC

Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia, Manitoba and Saskatchewan hiked minimum hourly wages on October 1, 2026.

While Nunavut continues to lead Canada with a minimum wage exceeding $20 per hour, workers in five other provinces saw pay bumps ranging from 25 to 40 cents as of October 1, 2026. This latest adjustment leaves Alberta as the only province that has not increased its base pay rate since 2019, keeping it at the country's lowest level of $15 an hour.

For roughly 700,000 workers in Ontario alone, the new $17.95 rate represents an annual increase of approximately $728 for those working 40 hours per week. Officials state these adjustments are tied to the Consumer Price Index to help families manage rising costs of living, international trade uncertainty, and shifting labour market demands.

Despite the increases, critics suggest the new rates fail to bridge the gap between government-mandated minimums and the actual costs of living. Anne Coleman of the Ontario Living Wage Network noted that the current living wage in the Greater Toronto Area sits at $27.20 an hour, nearly $10 above the updated provincial minimum. She emphasized that rental and food expenses are currently rising faster than general inflation, making it difficult for minimum wage earners to pay their monthly bills.

Nova Scotia’s move to $17 per hour follows a unanimous recommendation from the province's Minimum Wage Review Committee. The adjustment process in Nova Scotia is calculated based on the consumer price index plus one per cent. Similar adjustments are now in effect across Prince Edward Island at $17.30, Manitoba at $16.40, and Saskatchewan at $15.70. With these changes, every Canadian province and territory has successfully increased its minimum wage rate within the 2026 calendar year.
