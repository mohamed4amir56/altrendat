DIRCO demarches US Ambassador Brent Bozell following intense Trump administration demands

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 14:30 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 15:01 UTC

South Africa’s Department of International Relations and Cooperation has officially demarched US Ambassador Leo Brent Bozell III amid escalating diplomatic friction over five specific American demands.

This formal reprimand marks the third time Ambassador Bozell has been demarched since beginning his official duties in South Africa. The confrontation follows a public statement where Bozell pressed Pretoria to address a set of conditions established by the Trump administration, a move that prompted South African officials to characterize his recent conduct as undiplomatic. For the government in Pretoria, the standoff challenges its constitutional redress policies and its sovereign position on international legal matters, such as the genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice.

The diplomatic tension holds significant weight for the future of bilateral relations, as the US State Department has explicitly stated it is not interested in mere procedural dialogue. Washington’s stance, framed by President Donald Trump’s Executive Order 14204, places the burden squarely on the South African government to respond to the “Five Asks.” These demands include the protection of private property, the reversal of the Expropriation Act, amendments to Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment policies, the condemnation of the “Kill the Boer” chant, and enhanced rural security for farmers.

According to SABC News and official U.S. Embassy communications, Washington has recently escalated its pressure by announcing new visa restrictions. These measures target individuals deemed complicit in uncompensated land seizures, racial discrimination, or the incitement of violence against minority groups. While Pretoria has rejected these characterizations, the US government insists that its concerns, first delivered in November 2025, remain unanswered. With no immediate signs of a diplomatic reset, the State Department maintains that the path forward depends on whether South Africa chooses to meet the executive order's requirements.
