Steelers trade cornerback Joey Porter Jr. to Cowboys after failed contract extension talks

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 04:00 UTC

The Pittsburgh Steelers traded cornerback Joey Porter Jr. to the Dallas Cowboys on Wednesday after the two sides could not reach a contract extension agreement, with Porter in the final year of his rookie deal at a $4.9 million cap hit.

$4.9 million

Joey Porter Jr. has not played a snap this season for Pittsburgh, sitting out the first three games after being placed on the reserve/non-injury list in early August with a back injury that many believed masked a deeper contractual dispute.

For the Steelers, the trade represents a reset after months of failed negotiations. General manager Omar Khan had stated in late August that the franchise would not negotiate extensions during the season, and no deal materialized before Pittsburgh's Week 1 deadline. Steelers coach Mike McCarthy acknowledged the two sides were at an "impasse" in talks. Porter, drafted 32nd overall in 2023, wanted compensation matching top-tier cornerbacks—Christian Gonzalez signed a four-year, $135 million deal this offseason and Devon Witherspoon a four-year, $132 million contract—but Pittsburgh declined to match those figures.

For Dallas, the trade accelerates the Cowboys' defensive overhaul. The team started last season by acquiring Kenny Clark and Quinnen Williams, then added Rashan Gary before the draft. The secondary has struggled early this season after losing Cobie Durant to injury, and Dallas enters the trade with a 1-2 record. Adding Porter addresses that weakness as the franchise attempts to improve pass protection.

Porter's three-year NFL resume includes 165 combined tackles, 3 interceptions, and 31 passes defended. Last season he appeared in 14 games with 52 tackles, one sack, one interception, and 14 passes defended. His father, Joey Porter, played linebacker in the NFL from 1999 to 2011 with Pittsburgh, Miami, and Arizona.
