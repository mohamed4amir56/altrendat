Nigeria Labour Congress demands immediate petrol price cut to N500 per litre

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 14:01 UTC

The NLC has demanded an immediate reduction in fuel prices and a new wage negotiation as inflation erodes workers' purchasing power.

Petrol currently sells at ₦1,430 per litre or higher in major cities across Nigeria, with costs in remote areas climbing even further. The Human Rights Writers Association of Nigeria has also reported pump prices reaching approximately ₦1,500 per litre, fueling a deepening cost-of-living crisis that has left many citizens struggling to meet basic household expenses.

This demand is critical for the Nigerian workforce, as the NLC argues that surging transportation costs are the primary channel through which inflation impacts the economy. With the real value of the current ₦70,000 minimum wage effectively wiped out by rising prices for food, rent, education, and transport, the union warns that workers have reached a full-scale survival crisis point.

According to NLC President Joe Ajaero, the May 2023 petrol subsidy removal was a systematic assault on the working class. The NLC is now demanding a transparent account of where the promised subsidy savings were directed, noting that expected improvements in infrastructure and social services have not materialized three years later. The Joint National Public Service Negotiating Council has specifically called for a reduction to ₦500 per litre to alleviate this economic pressure.

Looking ahead, the NLC has formally requested that the government immediately begin negotiations for a new national wage benchmark for 2027. The union maintains that Nigeria’s continued reliance on imported refined petroleum, despite being a major oil producer, leaves the population overly vulnerable to international market fluctuations.
