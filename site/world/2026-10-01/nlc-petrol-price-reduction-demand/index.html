Nigerian Workers Threaten Three-Day Strike Demanding Petrol Price Drop to ₦500

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 14:01 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 15:03 UTC

The Nigeria Labour Congress and public service unions have issued a strike threat starting October 2 to protest soaring fuel costs and stagnating wages.

Petrol now sells at ₦1,430 per litre or higher in major cities, a price point the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) labels a systematic assault on the working class. While the federal government previously cited subsidy removal as a path to improved infrastructure and social services, the NLC asserts that these promised gains remain mirages three years after the initial price hikes. The current cost of fuel is directly inflating prices for essential goods including food, rent, and school fees.

The high cost of fuel represents a fundamental survival crisis for the Nigerian workforce. Because transportation costs serve as a primary driver of structural inflation, the union argues that reducing petrol prices is essential to preventing further economic erosion for workers and their dependants. The Joint National Public Service Negotiating Council (JNPSNC) reports that the current ₦70,000 minimum wage is no longer sufficient to sustain public workers under these harsh economic conditions.

According to the NLC President, Joe Ajaero, the union is demanding both an immediate reduction in fuel prices and the commencement of negotiations for a new national minimum wage. The JNPSNC has specified a target of ₦500 per litre for petrol. Both bodies maintain that the government must provide transparent answers regarding where subsidy savings have been directed since May 2023.

The JNPSNC confirmed that a three-day warning strike is set to commence on Friday, October 2, if the government fails to address these demands. Workers are looking toward a national broadcast from President Bola Tinubu for a resolution to the dispute.
