Singapore minister leaves son final Math puzzle to unlock laptop after PSLE

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC

A Singapore government official posed a Math problem based on this year's Primary School Leaving Examination to his son after the boy completed the national exam.

Quick facts

After finishing the Primary School Leaving Examination, a Primary 6 student in Singapore faced one more Math challenge—solving a puzzle to unlock his laptop. Senior Minister of State Goh Pei Ming left a sheet of paper on his son's computer with a final test, since he was away on a business trip and unable to celebrate in person.

The problem was inspired by an actual question from this year's PSLE Mathematics paper. It asked the boy to calculate the minimum number of days he must have resisted using his laptop during a 25-day revision period to reach more than 90 hours of study time. Goh told his son: "You've earned a good break and your laptop back. Just one small problem. It has a six-digit PIN."

Beyond the playful challenge, Goh's note carried a deeper message. "Whatever the results may be, I'm already proud of the effort, resilience and determination he has shown throughout the year," he wrote. "Looking forward to celebrating properly when I'm back. Well done, son." The message was shared on Instagram on September 30. The PSLE oral examinations took place on August 12 and 13, while written examinations ran from September 24 to 30. Goh, 44, is an MP for Marine Parade–Braddell Heights and holds the role of Senior Minister of State for Home Affairs and Social and Family Development.
