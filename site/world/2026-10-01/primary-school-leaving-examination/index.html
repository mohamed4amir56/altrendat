Minister Poses Final Math Problem to Son Post-PSLE to Unlock Laptop

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 17:36 UTC

Following the conclusion of Singapore's Primary School Leaving Examination on September 30, Senior Minister of State Goh Pei Ming required his son to solve a math puzzle to regain laptop access.

The Primary School Leaving Examination, or PSLE, has concluded for students across Singapore, but one Primary 6 pupil faced a final hurdle at home. Senior Minister of State for Social and Family Development Goh Pei Ming, currently traveling for work, left a handwritten note on his son’s laptop upon his return from the exam period. The note contained a specific six-digit PIN challenge inspired by a math question from this year’s national testing cycle, effectively turning the boy’s first day of freedom back into a study session to earn his digital privileges back. The minister noted that he was unable to be present in person for the final paper, but expressed pride in his son’s resilience throughout the year. The playful math problem asked the boy to calculate the minimum number of days spent resisting laptop use to reach a total of more than 90 hours of revision, based on a 25-day study window. Beyond this specific family milestone, the national examination remains a focal point in Singaporean life. While the Ministry of Education has implemented policy changes over recent years—such as the removal of PSLE T-scores and the elimination of weighted assessments—a survey by the Institute of Policy Studies indicates that parental stress and spending on tuition and enrichment classes remain elevated compared to a decade ago. Education experts and observers continue to examine the pressures surrounding the system, highlighting how academic milestones like the PSLE contribute to a broader environment of anxiety for students and families. As the written examination period for 2024 finished on September 30, the student now joins thousands of peers awaiting their results, following oral components held on August 12 and 13.
