Singapore watchdog busts 45 businesses, fake review provider fabricating customer ratings

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:04 UTC

Singapore's Competition and Consumer Commission took action against a fake review provider and 45 businesses that purchased fake reviews through websites offering S$1 to S$2 per review, marking the regulator's largest investigation of this kind.

Quick facts

Fake review price range
S$1 to S$2 per review
Businesses involved
About 100 procured fake reviews; 45 admitted and agreed to terms
Investigation announcement date
September 30
Fake review websites
BuyReviewSG and GetReviewSG
Operator
Julian Tung Yan Kai, sole director of Reputifly

Julian Tung Yan Kai operated two websites—BuyReviewSG and GetReviewSG—that sold fake reviews to about 100 businesses across restaurants, medical practices, legal firms and tuition agencies. The operation involved AI-generated reviews, recruiting paid reviewers via Telegram, and providing businesses with dashboards to manage their ratings.

Fake reviews undermine consumers' ability to make informed purchasing decisions, according to the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCS). The 45 businesses that admitted to the practice acknowledged they had engaged in unfair conduct that could mislead shoppers.

Of the 47 businesses initially investigated, 45 agreed to remove fake reviews, stop the unfair practices, and publish public apologies for six months across their websites, social media and physical locations. Companies including Burma Social, Harmony Funeral Care, Picasso Hair Boutique and The Hamper Story issued statements saying "We regret our conduct and have taken corrective measures." The fake review provider also committed to cease operations and donate proceeds from the scheme to charity. Two businesses declined to accept the commission's terms. CCS called this its largest fake review investigation to date by number of businesses involved.
