Kenya Power Slaps New Fines On Unauthorized Solar Energy Grid Feeding

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:04 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 15:03 UTC

Kenya Power and EPRA have introduced a dumping surcharge for businesses and homeowners exporting solar electricity without a valid net-metering agreement.

Wind and solar sources provided 34 per cent of Kenya’s energy mix during daytime peak demand on August 11, 2026. This reliance on variable generation has prompted officials to tighten oversight of rooftop solar installations across the country to protect infrastructure. Under the new 2023 tariff schedule, any electricity fed into the network without prior authorization or a net-metering contract is classified as illegal dumping. Consumers found engaging in these unauthorized connections will be billed at the standard base tariff, while Kenya Power maintains the authority to disconnect systems that threaten the safety or reliability of the distribution network. The company warned that fluctuations in renewable generation can disrupt grid frequency and voltage, necessitating the activation of additional generators to ensure stability. These rules, reinforced by the Energy (Net-Metering) Regulations of 2024, aim to standardize how excess power is managed as renewable adoption grows. Owners of systems up to one megawatt are eligible to export surplus electricity, provided they secure the required net-metering agreement through the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority. Kenya Power officials indicated these measures are essential to mitigate risks faced by maintenance workers and to preserve the overall security of the national electricity grid.
