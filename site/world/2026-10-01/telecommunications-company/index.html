Swift Telephone Network clinches licence to challenge Nigeria's telecom market giants

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 16:31 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 17:34 UTC

Swift Telephone Network Limited has secured a Unified Access Service Licence from the Nigerian Communications Commission to provide nationwide telecommunications services.

Quick facts

Company
Swift Telephone Network Limited (STN)
Licence Type
Unified Access Service Licence (UASL)
Effective Date
October 1, 2026
Issuer
Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC)
Licence Duration
10 years

Starting from humble origins as a table-top calling spot under an umbrella in 1998, Swift Telephone Network Limited has now secured a federal Unified Access Service Licence to compete directly with giants like MTN, Airtel, and Globacom. The company, which faced a complete operational shutdown in November 2010 before being revived by Oluwole Adetuyi in February 2017, marks this regulatory milestone as a definitive return to the national stage. According to the Nigerian Communications Commission, this new licence officially takes effect on October 1, 2026, and is renewable after a decade of operations.

This expansion is significant for Nigeria's digital infrastructure as the company positions itself to provide voice, data, and mobile services. By entering this space, the firm aims to impact the industry by narrowing the digital divide and creating economic opportunities. For the Nigerian telecommunications market, the arrival of another operator signifies a shift toward increased competition in a sector currently dominated by a handful of established international and local players.

STN’s leadership confirmed that the focus is on a business model rooted in Nigeria with global competitive standards. Management plans to emphasize the training of local engineers and digital professionals while forging strategic partnerships with local vendors to build a sustainable value chain. The company previously pioneered digital landline services in Yaba in 2003 and provided essential connectivity for the Nigerian Military and the World Health Organisation.

Looking ahead, the firm plans to release specific details regarding its national rollout strategy and upcoming partnerships in the coming weeks. The company continues to rebuild its infrastructure following years of regulatory reinstatement and economic pivots, transitioning from its previous enterprise-focused model to this broad, mobile-ready mandate.
