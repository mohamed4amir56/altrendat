T-Bills Surge As Top Performing Income Asset Class Over Five Years

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 13:32 UTC

Treasury bills have emerged as a high-performing income investment following recent Federal Reserve interest rate hikes.

T-bills have outperformed other income asset classes over the last five years as Federal Reserve rate increases pushed yields higher while bond prices fell. This shift in the interest rate environment has positioned Treasury bills as a strong choice for risk-averse investors currently looking for competitive income streams. For investors holding sidelined cash, the current economic landscape highlights the utility of specialized funds that track these debt securities. These instruments offer a blend of diversified exposure and liquidity, which can serve as a buffer against high capital costs. Vanguard’s VBIL index ETF, for instance, maintains a low expense ratio of 0.06% to capitalize on this trend. Similarly, the Vanguard Ultra-Short Treasury ETF (VGUS) provides an alternative for those seeking to mitigate the impact of high inflation while maintaining asset security. The primary appeal of these vehicles lies in their ability to provide stable returns in volatile markets where rising rates have traditionally pressured long-term bond values. As the financial environment remains sensitive to global economic factors, these funds continue to draw attention from those prioritizing capital preservation and consistent income. Investors are advised to evaluate these options based on their individual financial goals, as past performance does not guarantee future results in shifting interest rate cycles.
