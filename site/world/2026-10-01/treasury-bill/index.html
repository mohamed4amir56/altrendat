T-Bills Surge as Best Performing Income Asset Class Over Five Years

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC · Updated 01 Oct 2026, 15:04 UTC

Treasury bills have provided strong competitive income for investors following Federal Reserve interest rate hikes over the last five years.

Best-performing income asset class of the past five years

T-bills have emerged as one of the best-performing income asset classes during the last five years, a period defined by Federal Reserve rate hikes that pushed rates higher while bond prices fell. This current interest rate environment makes T-bills an attractive option for risk-averse investors looking to secure competitive income while maintaining diversified exposure. Seeking Alpha reports that the combination of high capital costs and market volatility has turned short-term treasury investments into a strategic haven for sidelined cash.

For investors, the primary appeal of treasury-backed instruments like ETFs lies in their ability to offer stability against high costs of capital. Managing exposure through low-cost vehicles is becoming a primary focus for those looking to protect their portfolios. Market analysts suggest that these instruments provide a reliable path for capital preservation while simultaneously capturing the benefits of elevated treasury yields.

Specific options now drawing attention include the Vanguard Ultra-Short Treasury ETF (VGUS), which is noted for its low expense ratio and high liquidity. Additionally, the Vanguard T-bills index ETF (VBIL) has gained recognition for its competitive 0.06% fee structure and consistent, growing dividend payments. While Seeking Alpha disclosures clarify that past performance does not guarantee future results, these funds are increasingly highlighted for their utility in high-inflation environments. Investors are encouraged to consider these tools as part of a broader strategy to navigate the current financial landscape without taking on excessive risk. No specific dates or future deadlines have been set regarding changes to these particular asset classes or fund structures, leaving the decision to current market participants to evaluate their own suitability.
