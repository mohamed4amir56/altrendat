Sir Alex Ferguson reveals terror before emotional Old Trafford return after surgery

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 08:00 UTC

Manchester United legend Sir Alex Ferguson describes his fear of losing his voice and his nerves before returning to Old Trafford after a 2018 brain haemorrhage.

Following his emergency brain haemorrhage surgery in May 2018, Sir Alex Ferguson faced a frightening reality where he lost the ability to speak. The legendary manager, who spent 26 years leading Manchester United, recalled the difficulty of communicating with his grandsons during his recovery, an experience that required intensive therapy to regain his voice through naming animals, birds, and rivers. This period of vulnerability stood in stark contrast to his reputation as one of the most explosive figures in football history. Returning to Old Trafford for a match against Wolves less than four months after his procedure, Ferguson admitted to BBC Breakfast that he was not comfortable and felt terrified about how fans would receive him. The anxiety quickly vanished as he and his wife, Cathy, took their seats in the directors' box to receive a standing ovation from the supporters. Reflecting on the moment, Ferguson described his wife being near tears, moved by the generosity of the crowd. Now 84 years old and three months short of his 85th birthday, Ferguson maintains a positive outlook, stating that he feels quite good despite minor health challenges. The former manager, who secured 38 trophies during his tenure at United, continues to be a regular presence at the club's home matches. Beyond his health journey, Ferguson is set to release a new book titled 'Games of My Life'. This project chronicles 21 matches that defined his career, detailing his path from his working-class upbringing in Glasgow to his success on the European stage. He expressed gratitude for his ability to share these stories, which document the pivotal moments that shaped his professional and personal life.
