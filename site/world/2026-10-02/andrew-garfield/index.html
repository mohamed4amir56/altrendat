Andrew Garfield Recalls Robert Redford Arriving Four Hours Late To Film Set

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 08:00 UTC

Actor Andrew Garfield revealed that the late Hollywood icon Robert Redford was habitually four hours late while filming the 2007 drama Lions for Lambs.

Quick facts

Andrew Garfield spent his film debut constantly waiting for director and co-star Robert Redford, who maintained a famously relaxed attitude toward the shooting schedule. While the younger actor stood ready to film scenes for the 2007 war drama Lions for Lambs, Redford would frequently roll in hours behind, operating entirely on his own internal clock. Garfield admits that while he was initially a bundle of nerves as a 23-year-old newcomer, the long waits eventually helped dissolve his professional anxiety. The environment on set shifted from high-pressure tension to a more casual atmosphere, defined by Redford’s effortless demeanor and preference for denim-on-denim attire.

This insight into the production offers a glimpse into the working habits of a Hollywood titan who remained influential until his death at age 89. For fans of the industry, the anecdote humanizes the process behind a serious political film featuring stars like Tom Cruise and Meryl Streep. It highlights the stark contrast between the disciplined, perfectionist nature of a major movie set and the unpredictable, loose style of a legend like Redford.

Speaking on Amy Poehler’s Good Hang podcast, Garfield clarified that he recounts these experiences with genuine affection. Despite the significant delays, Redford maintained a collaborative spirit, frequently asking the young Garfield for his honest opinion on specific takes. This collaborative approach made a lasting impression on the Spider-Man actor, even if it meant adjusting to a pace that kept the rest of the cast and crew waiting for hours on end.
