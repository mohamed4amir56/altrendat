€8.5 Billion Budget 2027 Looms As Irish Households Struggle With Daily Costs

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 06:00 UTC

The government is set to announce an €8.5 billion budget package on October 6, targeting tax relief amid rising fuel and food prices.

Quick facts

Budget announcement date
October 6
Total budget package
€8.5 billion
Tax cut allocation
€1.5 billion
Expert Q&A start time
7am on October 7

While the upcoming Budget 2027 is expected to be finalized within the next week, many workers report that current fuel and food costs are already pushing them to the brink. For commuters like Jason Stapleton, who spends approximately €110 to fill his car for a single day of travel and pays €240 weekly for groceries, the financial strain has reached a point where he describes his situation as living paycheck to paycheck. Others, including Jane McGuirk, report that they are forced to cut back on essential food and home heating to cope with the rising price of living.

The announcement of the €8.5 billion package holds significant weight for taxpayers as officials weigh specific allocations against these widespread household pressures. Minister for Finance Simon Harris has indicated that the tax strategy will focus on individuals who work long hours, with €1.5 billion specifically earmarked for tax cuts. The practical impact of these adjustments on income tax bands, pensions, and welfare remains a primary concern for the public.

According to The Irish Times, the official budget speech is scheduled for Tuesday, October 6. In the immediate aftermath, a panel of experts from PwC and The Irish Times will begin responding to specific taxpayer inquiries regarding how the new measures will affect net pay, disability supports, and inheritance tax. Interested parties can submit their questions via the publication's website starting on the day of the announcement, with professional responses becoming available to the public beginning at 7am on Wednesday, October 7.
