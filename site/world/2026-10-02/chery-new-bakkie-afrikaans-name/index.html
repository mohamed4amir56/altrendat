Chery snatches local crown as new bakkie becomes the official Grootman

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 07:03 UTC

Following a national voting campaign, Chery South Africa has officially named its upcoming double-cab hybrid bakkie the Grootman for its 2027 market debut.

Over 20,000 name suggestions poured into Chery South Africa during a public naming competition that eventually narrowed the field down to six final contenders: Bakkie, Brahman, Chief, Orca, Nduna, and Grootman. The term Grootman, which is rooted in Afrikaans and literally translates to "big man," emerged as the winner. In South African culture, the title is a familiar expression of respect used to describe someone who has earned their standing through experience, achievement, and the ability to take responsibility when problems need solving.

For the automaker, the name choice represents a strategic integration of local identity into its global engineering efforts. General Manager for Chery South Africa, Jay Jay Botes, stated that because South Africans themselves chose the name, the vehicle carries meaningful expectations that the company intends to live up to. The bakkie is intended to challenge established local players like the Toyota Hilux and Ford Ranger.

The vehicle, known internally as the KP31, is confirmed for a South African launch during the first quarter of 2027. Preliminary specifications for the four-wheel drive hybrid indicate it will be powered by a 2.5l turbodiesel engine paired with an electric motor. The powertrain is slated to deliver a combined output of 350kW and 800Nm, with a braked towing capacity rated at 3,500kg. While the bakkie will initially arrive as a full import, Chery is considering local assembly at its new Rosslyn plant, which is expected to begin operations in the second half of 2027. Meanwhile, the same vehicle will be marketed as the Stockman in Australia.
