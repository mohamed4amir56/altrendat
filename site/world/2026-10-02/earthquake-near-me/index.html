7300 people report violent 5.3 magnitude quake hitting New Zealand's North Island

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 14:30 UTC · Updated 02 Oct 2026, 15:01 UTC

A magnitude 5.3 earthquake struck 25km east of New Plymouth early Friday morning, jolting thousands of residents across the North Island.

One resident described the early-morning tremor as the worst experience in 78 years, while another compared the impact to a bomb causing the floor of a shed to jump upwards. Reports across the region detailed a loud rumble followed by sharp shaking that sent pictures, pot plants, and household items crashing to the floor. The quake hit at 2:57 a.m. at a depth of 11km, according to data from GeoNet, which officially classified the event as strong shaking. More than 7,300 people across the North Island reported feeling the motion, with about 4,000 residents noting moderate to strong intensity. For many, the incident served as a stark reminder of earthquake safety protocols, with monitoring services reiterating the importance of the drop, cover, and hold procedure during active seismic events. While the shaking caused some belongings to break and left some residents without internet access for up to 20 minutes, Fire and Emergency New Zealand confirmed by 3:25 a.m. that they had received no emergency callouts related to the tremor. In contrast, a separate, smaller magnitude 3.7 earthquake occurred in the Lake Tahoe area of California at 3:00 a.m. on the same day. That quake hit at a depth of approximately 4 miles, about 6 miles west-southwest of South Lake Tahoe. While tremors were believed to have been felt in Reno and Carson City, Nevada, there were no initial reports of shaking submitted to the United States Geological Survey, and no information regarding damage or injuries was available.
