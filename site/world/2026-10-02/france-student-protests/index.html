1,949 arrested as French student protests shut 400 schools into urban violence

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 10:00 UTC

More than 400 schools across France have closed as student protests over teacher shortages and crumbling facilities escalated into widespread unrest, with nearly 2,000 arrests and over 300 police officers injured.

Riot police deployed to confront increasingly violent protests outside French high schools, with stone-throwing demonstrators clashing with officers across the country. The unrest has spread from the Paris region to hundreds of schools nationwide, forcing the prime minister to call a crisis cabinet meeting and order ministers to cancel travel plans.

Students are demanding action on long-standing complaints: insufficient teachers, overcrowded classrooms, run-down buildings, and excessive hours. The escalation matters because the French government says the violence now makes it virtually impossible to maintain public education and is exposing staff and pupils to danger. Classes have shifted to remote learning at affected schools to protect teachers and students from harm.

About 900 demonstrations, blockades and related incidents are under way outside high schools, with 1,027 lycées affected so far and blockades at 222 of them, according to the interior ministry. At least 30 universities have also been blockaded, including campuses in Paris, Marseille, Rennes and other cities. Since the protests began last week, French police report 1,949 arrests on Thursday alone and nearly 3,000 detained overall. The French interior ministry says 305 police officers have been injured, while 65 staff members and dozens of pupils and firefighters sustained injuries. Education minister Édouard Geffray reported significant damage to school facilities and is scheduled to meet pupil representatives on Friday.
