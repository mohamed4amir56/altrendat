1,949 Arrested as Violent Student Protests Ignite French Schools Nationwide

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 05:00 UTC

Massive student demonstrations against school conditions and funding led to 1,949 arrests and 305 police injuries on Thursday.

Police reported that a fireman was engulfed in flames after an aerosol can exploded at a protest site in Bordeaux, while a headteacher was injured by a firework mortar at a lycée in Aubervilliers. Nationwide, 1,027 schools have been affected by the student-led movement, with 222 experiencing active blockades and several buildings sustaining fire damage. The Interior Ministry confirmed that 1,949 individuals were arrested on Thursday alone, marking a significant escalation in the unrest that has seen 305 police officers injured across the country. The protests are driven by student demands for better school funding, more teachers, and improved facilities, including reports of overcrowded classrooms and run-down buildings. These demonstrations, which began on September 21 in a Paris suburb, now impact both high schools and at least 30 universities, with students and staff citing a lack of resources as the primary catalyst for their actions. The French government has labeled the situation as urban violence, with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu convening a crisis meeting on Thursday to address the security threat. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned of a significant rise in violent tactics, while the Ministry of Education reported that 119 pupils and staff members have been injured since the protests commenced. The movement remains a volatile political issue with presidential contenders observing the developments as the country faces ongoing disruptions to the education sector.
