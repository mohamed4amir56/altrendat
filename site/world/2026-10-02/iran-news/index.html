US bolsters Iran war presence with massive third aircraft carrier deployment

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 13:52 UTC

The United States is deploying a third aircraft carrier strike group and up to 10,000 additional troops to the Middle East by late November.

9,000 to 10,000

More than 60,000
War start date
28 February
Deployment completion
End of November

The arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt and the Makin Island Marine Expeditionary Unit marks the largest military buildup in the region since the 2003 invasion of Iraq. This strategic expansion adds to the two carrier strike groups currently operating in the Arabian Sea, placing three carriers and two amphibious landing groups near Iranian waters by the end of November. The USS Theodore Roosevelt departed San Diego on Sunday without ceremony, carrying Carrier Air Wing 11, which includes F-35C and F/A-18 Super Hornet fighter jets.

This troop surge increases the total American military footprint in the region to more than 60,000 personnel. According to US officials, the concentration of naval and aerial assets aims to provide President Donald Trump with a wider range of military options while maintaining pressure on Iran as the conflict enters its eighth month. The deployment ensures that varied operational choices remain on the table for the White House regardless of the progress of ongoing diplomatic efforts.

The deployment follows the sustained two-carrier presence maintained since the war began on 28 February. Reports from the US Naval Institute confirm the USS George HW Bush and USS George Washington are currently active in the Arabian Sea. The Makin Island unit, which includes the USS John P Murtha and the USS Anchorage, adds over 2,000 Marines to the theater. These assets join the USS Boxer amphibious-ready group already operating in the region.

Military analysts and Pentagon sources expect all arriving ships and personnel to be fully positioned by the end of November. The timing coincides with looming midterm elections, where the administration’s handling of the war remains a critical point of public and political scrutiny.
