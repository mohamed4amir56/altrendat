Four astronauts launch from Florida to International Space Station aboard Falcon 9

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 04:30 UTC

Crew-13 lifted off from Kennedy Space Center at 11:10 a.m. on Thursday with two NASA astronauts, a Canadian Space Agency astronaut, and a Russian cosmonaut heading to the International Space Station.

Quick facts

Launch time
11:10 a.m. Thursday
Launch site
Kennedy Space Center, Florida
Destination

Pre-launch weather probability
60% chance of launch

Despite a 60% chance of weather scrubbing the mission that morning, Crew-13 blasted off right on schedule at 11:10 a.m. from Kennedy Space Center on Florida's Space Coast. The four crew members—NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov—are now en route to the International Space Station. Watkins serves as commander while Delaney pilots the spacecraft, with Kutryk and Teteryatnikov operating as mission specialists.

The mission carries scientific and technological importance for NASA's broader exploration goals. According to NASA, Crew-13 will conduct scientific investigations and technology demonstrations designed to help prepare humans for future exploration missions to the Moon and Mars.

The Falcon 9 rocket performed flawlessly, with its first stage successfully touching down at Landing Zone 40 following stage separation in space. Delaney, who grew up in DeBary, Florida, and graduated from Deltona High School in 1997, represents the local community's connection to the mission. The crew had spent the days leading up to launch in quarantine and conducting final preflight operations at Kennedy Space Center.
