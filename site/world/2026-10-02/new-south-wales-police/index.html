Canberra procession honours four fallen officers on National Police Remembrance Day

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 04:00 UTC · Updated 02 Oct 2026, 08:31 UTC

Hundreds of officers marched to the National Police Memorial on September 29 to enshrine the names of four fallen personnel.

Quick facts

Date
September 29, 2026
Event
National Police Remembrance Day
Procession size
About 300 officers

A solemn procession of approximately 300 officers on foot, horseback, and motorbikes journeyed from the Australian Federal Police headquarters to the shores of Lake Burley Griffin. This pilgrimage marked the 2026 National Police Remembrance Day, where tearful families gathered to witness the formal addition of four new touchstones to the National Police Memorial.

This national recognition serves as a vital moment for the community to acknowledge the risks taken by those in uniform. Beyond the official ceremony, local services—including one at St John's Anglican Church in Wagga—highlighted the deep personal loss felt by colleagues and families, reminding the public that every name on the honour roll represents a parent, partner, or friend who served their community.

The memorial officially added names including Victoria Police Detective Leading Senior Constable Neal Thompson and Senior Constable Vadim de Waart-Hottart, who were killed in August 2025. Also honoured were Western Australia Detective Senior Constable Maxine Bembridge, who died following a vehicle crash, and retired New South Wales Sergeant Mark Bransgrove, whose death was attributed to his service. Acting AFP Commissioner Ian McCartney noted the profound weight of the day, acknowledging the presence of families and colleagues who had not faced such loss just one year prior. Meanwhile, further tributes were held across the country, with regional police districts like the Great Southern also gathering to honour those who had lost their lives while serving in various eras and situations.

The service stands as an annual commitment to remembering the selfless and the brave who accepted the inherent dangers of policing. It remains a cornerstone of the national calendar for emergency services personnel to reflect on their fallen brothers and sisters.
