Anne Hathaway and Dakota Johnson thriller Verity falls flat with baffling twist

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Oct 2026, 07:00 UTC

The Colleen Hoover adaptation starring Anne Hathaway, Dakota Johnson, and Josh Hartnett struggles with a self-defeating and woolly climactic ending.

Quick facts

Author of original novel
Colleen Hoover
Director

Screenwriter
Nick Antosca
Primary filming location setting
Vermont

The film adaptation of Colleen Hoover’s novel, directed by Michael Showalter, features a scene where a character leaves teeth marks on a headboard during a sex scene. However, critics suggest audiences might find themselves gnawing on seatbacks or armrests out of frustration instead of excitement. The narrative follows Lowen Ashleigh, played by Dakota Johnson, a struggling writer recruited to ghostwrite for Verity Crawford, an incapacitated, mega-bestselling author portrayed by Anne Hathaway. Lowen finds herself residing in Verity’s luxury lake house in Vermont, a structure described as having modernist proportions with roaring fires and blond wood floors. While stationed there, she develops a strange attraction to Verity’s sensitive husband, Jeremy, played by Josh Hartnett. The story centers on the dark, saucy mystery surrounding the household and the completion of Verity’s lucrative book series. According to The Telegraph, director Michael Showalter attempts to inject a lurid, Brian De Palma-like sheen into the project, offering moments of seedy, slinky fun. The plot revolves around a love triangle between the catatonic, wheelchair-bound author, her husband, and the ambitious ghostwriter. Ultimately, the film suffers from a climax that critics have characterized as deeply unsatisfying. The Guardian notes that the adaptation, written by Nick Antosca, struggles to capture the desired erotic mystery-thriller tone, resulting in a production that fails to deliver on its premise. Despite the high-profile casting of Hathaway, Johnson, and Hartnett, the consensus points to a disappointing conclusion that undermines the buildup of the mystery.
