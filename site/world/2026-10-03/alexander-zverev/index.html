Alexander Zverev Blasts Extended Masters 1000 Tournaments While Targeting Beijing Open Glory

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 11:40 UTC · Updated 03 Oct 2026, 12:31 UTC

Alexander Zverev has criticized the current structure of Masters 1000 events while competing at the China Open in Beijing.

Alexander Zverev has taken a public stance against the extended format of Masters 1000 tournaments, signaling a desire for change in the professional tennis schedule. The German star, currently competing at the China Open in Beijing, highlighted his preference for the intensity found in ATP 500 events over the longer, drawn-out tournament structures now seen on the tour. Zverev pointed out the oddity of the current ranking matchups in these events, noting that high-ranked competitors like the number 16 and number 17 in the world could face each other early in the draw, a situation he implied undermines the traditional prestige of the competition. His comments come as the ATP tour continues to adjust its tournament calendar, with players increasingly vocal about the physical and scheduling demands placed upon them during the long season. Zverev is aiming to capitalize on his performance in Beijing, a venue where he remains a top contender, especially as other major names navigate their own tournament challenges. Meanwhile, the China Open continues to be a focal point for the sport's biggest stars. Novak Djokovic has maintained an unbeaten record at the tournament despite requiring a medical timeout during his recent second-round victory over Bu Yunchaokete. As the field thins out, all eyes are on the remaining competitors to see if Zverev can translate his vocal criticisms into a deep run on the court, potentially setting up high-profile clashes against the likes of Djokovic who is also navigating his path through the draw.
