Cadmium detected in 96 percent of top selling cat food brands tested

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 04:00 UTC

A recent investigation of 100 popular cat food products discovered toxic contaminants, including cadmium, in nearly every sample analyzed.

Quick facts

Samples tested
100
Cadmium prevalence
96%
Contaminants screened
Over 120
Variety types
Wet, dry, and freeze-dried

Cadmium, a toxic metal associated with significant kidney and bone damage in animals, has been identified in 96% of the cat food products evaluated in a new study. Researchers conducted comprehensive testing on 100 top-selling cat food varieties to determine the safety and composition of commonly purchased pet sustenance.

This investigation encompasses a wide range of options, specifically testing wet, dry, and freeze-dried products to provide a complete picture of the market. For pet owners, these findings highlight the urgent need for transparency regarding the manufacturing processes and ingredient sourcing used by major pet food brands. Understanding what constitutes the daily diet of domestic cats is essential, as prolonged exposure to heavy metals and other toxins can have severe long-term health implications for pets.

The research methodology involved screening for more than 120 different contaminants across the selected products. According to reports from CBS News New York, the presence of lead and various other toxins was also confirmed during this assessment of consumer-available pet food. This wide-scale screening underscores the prevalence of potential health hazards hiding in mainstream cat food offerings across the industry.

While the study identifies the widespread presence of these substances, it serves as a critical call to action for further regulatory oversight and safety standards. Consumers are encouraged to remain vigilant and informed about the products they select for their pets as further information regarding specific brand testing and safety thresholds becomes available following this widespread investigation.
