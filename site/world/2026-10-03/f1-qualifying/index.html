Ferrari Paces Challenging Friday Practice As Red Bull Remains The Team To Beat

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 09:00 UTC

Charles Leclerc led the field during the Bahrain Grand Prix practice in Malaysia, edging out Red Bull’s Isack Hadjar by 0.099 seconds.

Kimi Antonelli narrowly escaped the elimination zone during his initial run, clocking a time just 0.263 seconds behind the benchmark set by Max Verstappen. While the timesheets show a tight field, the underlying pace tells a different story as teams grapple with a dirty, slippery track at the Sepang International Circuit. Charles Leclerc, who claimed the fastest time, admitted that the conditions were tricky and noted that the track surface is expected to evolve significantly before qualifying begins. Despite Ferrari leading the session, George Russell and Leclerc both identified Red Bull as the team to beat for the weekend. Max Verstappen opted to bypass a qualifying simulation run on soft tyres, choosing instead to prioritize race preparation as rain forecasts loomed over the circuit. This decision kept him fourth on the charts, 0.257 seconds off the top pace. Meanwhile, Mercedes remains a difficult team to read, with Lewis Hamilton finishing fifth, 0.305 seconds behind his rival. Technical director Pierre Wache noted that Red Bull began the weekend with low expectations, yet their performance suggests they remain a formidable force. With the top four teams currently separated by less than four tenths of a second, the competitive nature of the field ensures that any marginal gains in car setup or track positioning will be critical for Saturday’s qualifying session. Fans can follow live text updates and BBC Radio 5 Sports Extra commentary to see which team successfully masters the evolving track conditions to secure pole position.
