Government set to lift State pension and Living Alone allowance by €10

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 13:30 UTC

State pension and core welfare payments are expected to increase by €10 as part of the upcoming Budget 2027.

The government is moving beyond its initial proposal of a €7.50 hike, now leaning toward a €10 increase for primary welfare payments and the State pension. This change follows ongoing discussions as the Department of Public Expenditure aims to limit the overall spending increase for next year to 6%. Alongside the broader pension boost, a cost of disability payment totaling more than €400 annually has been largely agreed upon, and the Living Alone allowance is also slated for an increase.

These adjustments arrive as the administration balances record tax revenues against the reality of rising grocery and energy bills for households. While the government pushes these changes, some analysts highlight that a projected 2.6% pension increase sits below the current 3.7% inflation rate, which is expected to climb further next year. The budget remains a central focus for officials, with final department allocations expected to be signed off within a 24-hour window.

The potential for wider financial relief remains a point of contention, with fuel protest groups actively lobbying Minister for Finance Simon Harris. These groups have submitted formal proposals requesting emergency measures for businesses and households, specifically targeting relief on carbon taxes, energy costs, and commercial transport to help maintain viability and employment. Meanwhile, the government continues to weigh these demands against the need to move away from the cycle of massive annual spending hikes seen in previous years.

Negotiations have been described as robust as officials finalize the details before Tuesday's Budget announcement. Beyond welfare, the government also intends to provide funding for a new €100 culture card for 16 and 17-year-olds, though the specific delivery methods and participating venues are still being determined.
