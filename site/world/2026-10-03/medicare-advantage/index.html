UnitedHealth and Humana Slash One Million Medicare Advantage Spots Before 2027

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 11:31 UTC

Major insurers UnitedHealth, Humana, Aetna, and Centene are discontinuing hundreds of thousands of Medicare Advantage plans ahead of the 2027 enrollment year.

More than one million seniors face the loss of their current healthcare coverage as insurance giants retreat from markets deemed less profitable. UnitedHealth is discontinuing plans covering approximately 390,000 members, while Humana has confirmed cuts impacting roughly 600,000 enrollees. Analysts studying government data confirm that CVS Health’s Aetna and Centene are also reducing their Medicare Advantage footprints, contributing to a significant industry-wide contraction.

This shift carries direct consequences for beneficiaries who must now navigate plan renewals or return to original Medicare. Insurers cite a convergence of mounting challenges within the healthcare system, including persistent funding pressures, rising medical costs, and increased patient utilization. UnitedHealthcare President Bobby Hunter stated that the company can no longer ignore these financial realities, forcing a strategic withdrawal from markets dominated by expensive preferred provider organization plans.

In response to these changes, UnitedHealthcare will issue non-renewal notices to affected members dated October 2. Individuals impacted by these cancellations have a window from October 15 to December 31 to enroll in new plans for coverage starting January 1, 2027. Those who do not actively select a new option will be automatically reverted to original Medicare. Members who return to original Medicare retain a special election period through February 28, 2027, to transition into a new Medicare Advantage offering. While some providers are exiting, Aetna is shifting its strategy by expanding its health maintenance organization footprint to focus on more controlled, lower-cost provider networks.
