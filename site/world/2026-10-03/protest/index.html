1,747 Arrests As Violent Education Protests Shut Over 700 French Schools

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Oct 2026, 08:01 UTC

Riot police clashed with protesters and 735 schools faced disruptions across France this week as students demonstrated over education standards.

On Friday alone, 1,747 people were arrested as student-led demonstrations spiraled into arson and clashes with riot police across France. While the movement originated from high school pupils calling for better conditions, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez stated that the country is now sliding into riot and urban violence. In the north-eastern town of Belfort, a 17-year-old was arrested on suspicion of attempted homicide after a police officer was beaten and kicked by a group of individuals.

The scale of the unrest has hit the French education system hard, with 735 of the nation's 3,700 schools experiencing disruptions. For the students on the frontline, such as those at the Lycée Voltaire in Paris, the blockades are a last resort to protest against overcrowded classrooms, crumbling buildings, and insufficient teacher staffing. They argue that these measures are necessary to force the government to address long-standing grievances regarding state investment in education.

Government officials have expressed concern over the shifting nature of the protests. Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin characterized the situation as a transition from student demonstrations to a breakdown of public order. Meanwhile, Education Minister Édouard Geffray reported that teachers had encountered individuals at the barricades who were not students, suggesting the presence of outside agitators. Beyond the administrative impact, the ministry confirmed that at least 65 staff members have been injured throughout the recent wave of unrest.

As the government works to regain control, students involved in the blockades have expressed a desire for the demonstrations to continue into the coming week. The volatility remains a focal point for authorities as they deploy intermediate measures to quell the ongoing fires and street violence that have spread from Paris to cities including Marseille, Montpellier, and Nantes.
