BUA Group Chairman Abdul Samad Rabiu Visits President Tinubu For Independence Day

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 04:00 UTC

Billionaire businessman Abdul Samad Rabiu and his son met with President Bola Tinubu to celebrate Nigeria's 66th Independence anniversary.

Quick facts

Occasion
Nigeria's 66th Independence Day
Date of Independence
October 1, 1960
Primary Attendees
Abdul Samad Rabiu, Isyaku Abdulsamad Khalifa, Bola Tinubu

Abdul Samad Rabiu, the Chairman of BUA Group, brought his son, Isyaku Abdulsamad Khalifa, to visit President Bola Tinubu. The businessman shared photographs of the meeting on his official X account on Friday. Rabiu stated that it was an honour to meet with the President and personally extend his warm wishes for the national occasion. The visit served as a moment to reflect on Nigeria's historical journey and discuss future prospects for the nation. During the interaction, Rabiu expressed his hope for continued peace, unity, progress, and prosperity for all Nigerians residing within the country and abroad. According to Punch Newspapers, October 1 marks the annual national public holiday commemorating the nation's independence from British colonial rule, which was first achieved in 1960. The 2026 celebrations specifically recognize the country's 66th year of independence. This recent engagement with the President adds to the ongoing dialogue between the government and major industrial leaders. Notably, President Tinubu also recently conducted a meeting with the President and Chief Executive of the Dangote Group, Aliko Dangote, at the President's residence in Ikoyi, Lagos. While the Rabiu visit focused on the Independence Day milestone, the meeting with Dangote followed public announcements regarding major investment plans, including the expansion of the Lagos refinery and a new $16 billion project in Kenya. These interactions highlight the active communication between Nigeria's presidency and top business figures who remain central to the country's economic and industrial sectors. The government has not announced further public meetings with private sector leaders following these sessions.
