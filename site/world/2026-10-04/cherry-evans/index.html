Daly Cherry-Evans hunts fairytale NRL premiership in final career game

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 10:30 UTC

Sydney Roosters playmaker Daly Cherry-Evans plays his 379th and final NRL match in Sunday's grand final against the Newcastle Knights.

It has been 13 years since Daly Cherry-Evans last featured in a grand final, a drought he describes as having fostered a deep appreciation for finals football after enduring several lean seasons. Now 37 years old and a father, the veteran halfback prepares for his 379th and final NRL appearance before heading to Hull FC, marking a career milestone 15 years after he claimed a premiership as a Manly rookie in 2011.

This final push represents more than just a farewell; it is a tactical effort by the Sydney Roosters to bolster their squad around captain James Tedesco. Club powerbrokers sought Cherry-Evans’ experience to act as a calm influence and help develop halfback Sam Walker, specifically aiming to ease the playmaking burden on Tedesco. His former Manly teammate Kieran Foran noted that the veteran's drive to return to the sport's biggest stage has been a long-held ambition discussed during their private meetings over the last few years.

Reflecting on his evolution, Cherry-Evans admits he was once an instinctive five-eighth wearing a number seven jersey who lacked structural discipline. He has since transformed into a ball-playing halfback, relying on his kicking game and matured understanding of team needs rather than just his initial speed. While he previously earned a Clive Churchill Medal in a 2013 grand final loss, he now hopes to cap his tenure at the Roosters with a title win.

The build-up to Sunday’s match has largely focused on the Newcastle Knights’ quest for their first premiership in 25 years. However, Cherry-Evans remains focused on the task at hand, aiming to secure his second career title before transitioning to his new club in the upcoming season.
