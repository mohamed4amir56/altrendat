Cartel founder Christy Kinahan vanishes as Dubai residency visa officially expires

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 10:30 UTC

Irish police are searching for the 69-year-old crime figure after his mandatory departure deadline from Dubai passed on September 29th.

Christy Kinahan snr, the founder of an international drug cartel, has disappeared from view following the expiration of his United Arab Emirates residency visa. Authorities in Dubai ordered the 69-year-old to leave the country by September 29th after cancelling his legal residency status at the end of August. While some reports suggest he may have departed the desert state on Monday, Irish police confirm they currently possess no verification that he has actually exited the jurisdiction.

The uncertainty regarding his location follows over a decade of residence in Dubai. For law enforcement, locating the figurehead is a priority as his movement options are limited. Kinahan is currently under American sanctions, a status that investigators believe will lead many nations to deny him entry if he attempts to establish a new base. Though he faces no active criminal charges, his long-term residency in the UAE has been terminated due to a shift in the attitude of local authorities toward him.

According to sources, the penalty for remaining in the country beyond the visa deadline involves daily fines of approximately 11 euro. While this cost is considered negligible for the leader of a syndicate valued at 1.5 billion euro, extended overstaying could eventually result in arrest and formal deportation. Gardaí are now working to determine if Kinahan has successfully relocated to a new country, with Turkey identified as one potential destination given his partner's family background. Despite previous, extensive investigations into the leadership of his criminal network, no charges have been recommended against Kinahan by the Director of Public Prosecutions.
