Iran Rial Plunges to Record 2.5 Million Per U.S. Dollar

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 05:01 UTC · Updated 04 Oct 2026, 06:01 UTC

The Iranian rial hit a record low of over 2.5 million to the U.S. dollar on Tuesday, down from 2.2 million in early September.

The currency has plummeted by approximately 170% since August 2025, when it was trading at 920,000 rials to the dollar. This sharp decline follows a period where the rial began 2018 at 35,000 to the greenback and started this year at 1.5 million. Iran did not load any oil at its export terminals last month, marking the first time such an event has occurred since the 1979 Islamic revolution, according to Kpler crude oil analysis head Homayoun Falakshahi. The U.S. naval blockade has driven oil exports to near zero, causing a significant cash crunch for the regime.

Economic instability is compounding as the nation faces inflation near 90% and an expected GDP contraction of 5.4% this year. With energy being rationed and unemployment rising, Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has signaled concern regarding social cohesion. Households and investors are increasingly scrambling to protect their remaining wealth by shifting assets into gold and the stock market as the rial loses value.

Iran is currently struggling with severe trade limitations, as it cannot easily import goods by sea and land routes remain heavily congested. While there is increased activity across the North–South Transport Corridor and the Caspian Sea, Russia’s Ministry of Transport reports do not confirm these routes are effectively replacing lost oil export infrastructure or revenue.

Financial pressure has intensified since the U.S. and Israel launched a war on Iran in February, leading to the latest record low. Future market stability remains uncertain as the regime contends with both the U.S. naval blockade and the ongoing impact of the military conflict on the national economy.
