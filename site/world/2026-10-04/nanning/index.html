Pinglu Canal breakthrough cuts shipping routes by 560 kilometers for Nanning trade

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 04:30 UTC

The newly opened 134.2-kilometer Pinglu Canal officially links Nanning to maritime trade networks, slashing logistics costs by 30%.

Quick facts

Pinglu Canal length
134.2 kilometers
Logistics cost reduction
Up to 30%
Canal opening date
September 16
Route distance saved
Over 560 kilometers

The Pinglu Canal has officially reshaped Nanning’s economic landscape by trimming over 560 kilometers from traditional inland sea routes. This newly opened waterway serves as a critical maritime gateway, successfully connecting landlocked southwestern Chinese provinces directly to Southeast Asia via a seamless river-sea link. The infrastructure project, which spans 134.2 kilometers, aims to reduce logistics costs by as much as 30% for regional industries. The freighter Beigang Nanning Borun recently demonstrated this new connectivity by completing a voyage to Vietnam’s Can Tho Port, marking the first China-bound cargo vessel to utilize the route.

For residents and businesses, the canal represents a move to transform the region from an economic backwater into a logistics hub. While local residents weigh the long-term benefits against daily prospects, tech companies like Apple see potential in using the waterway to distribute products assembled in Vietnam, such as wearables and smart speakers, more efficiently into the Chinese market. This logistical shift allows goods to bypass the lengthy detour through Guangzhou’s port, which previously added over 500 kilometers to journeys destined for cities like Kunming and Chongqing.

Meanwhile, Nanning continues to attract international corporate interest, as seen during the China-ASEAN Expo. More than 50 Singaporean exhibitors participated in the September event to explore distribution partnerships, undeterred by recent legal investigations involving pyramid schemes in the area. Businesses currently navigating this market report that building stable connections in Nanning often requires several months of preparation and careful filtering of local partners.

As the integrated multimodal network matures, the canal is expected to bridge the northern overland Silk Road Economic Belt with the southern 21st Century Maritime Silk Road. Local exporters have already begun utilizing the return legs of shipping routes to transport goods like Vietnamese rice bran, further cementing the region's position within the integrated trade corridor.
