Experts warn record Godzilla El Nino could trigger major polar vortex breakdown

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 08:00 UTC

Households in the UK are preparing for potential heavy snowfall and extreme cold this winter as climate patterns weaken the polar vortex.

Quick facts

Potential temperature swing
Up to 50C in two days
Recommended food supply
At least three days
Key risk factor
Godzilla El Nino

A surge in stratospheric temperatures of up to 50C in just two days is threatening to destabilize the atmosphere, according to the Met Office. This rapid warming process, known as Sudden Stratospheric Warming, can force the polar vortex—the circular band of strong winds trapping Arctic air—to become wavy and break down, potentially spilling freezing conditions southward across Europe and North America.

For residents, the breakdown of this weather system signifies a shift from mild, wet conditions to potentially severe disruptions, including heavy snowfall and freezing temperatures. Experts emphasize that an unprecedented Godzilla El Nino is currently driving these global climate anomalies, raising the risk of prolonged cold spells that can paralyze travel and strain home heating infrastructure.

British Gas advises households to prepare immediately by maintaining at least three days of essential supplies, including non-perishable food, medicine, and drinking water. Homeowners are encouraged to reinforce insulation, protect exposed pipes, and keep vehicle fuel tanks at least half-full to maintain functionality during sudden snow events. Meteorologists from the Met Office and the NOAA Chemical Sciences Laboratory have linked these winter threats directly to the current peak in sea surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean.

Looking ahead, officials are monitoring the stratospheric conditions closely as the winter progresses. While early forecasts indicated a wet and windy start to the season, the potential for a weakening polar vortex creates a high-stakes pivot point toward extreme winter weather in the coming months.
