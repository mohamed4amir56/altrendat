Trump Predicts Big Midterm Surprise While Campaigning For Struggling Ohio Republicans

By Karim Fouad · Published 04 Oct 2026, 08:30 UTC

Donald Trump rallied in Ohio on Saturday to boost Senate candidate Jon Husted and gubernatorial nominee Vivek Ramaswamy as midterm elections approach.

Quick facts

Rally Date
Saturday
Location
Vandalia, Ohio
Election Deadline
November 3

Donald Trump told supporters in a Vandalia high school gymnasium on Saturday that the upcoming midterms will produce a “big surprise” and that the party will “win big” if voters turn out. The rally, held in Montgomery county, focused on shoring up support for incumbent US senator Jon Husted and Vivek Ramaswamy, the GOP candidate for governor, both of whom face competitive and expensive races in the state.

This visit is part of a broader strategy identified by White House officials, involving campaign stops in at least 17 states before November 3. The stakes are high for the Republican party, as internal memos from the National Republican Senatorial Committee previously highlighted concerns regarding Husted’s campaign performance and data center policy. For local voters, the rally comes at a time of economic pressure, with some residents citing rising costs of gas and groceries as major concerns, placing increased scrutiny on the administration's impact.

While Ohio has remained a Republican stronghold for over a decade—with Trump previously securing comfortable wins in the state—reports from The Guardian indicate that his current popularity in the industrial midwestern state has seen a notable decline. Voters expressed varying perspectives on the administration, with some grievances stemming from economic struggles and concerns over domestic incidents. Despite these challenges, Trump also extended his support during the event to local Representative Mike Turner and several House candidates, including Derek Merrin.

With the midterm cycle intensifying, the Republican party is looking to capitalize on this push through early November to maintain its influence in key state and national races.
