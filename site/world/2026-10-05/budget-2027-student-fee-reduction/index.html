Budget 2027: Childcare costs slashed by 1000 euros for working families

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Oct 2026, 09:30 UTC

Families with children in full-time care are set for annual savings of at least 1,000 euros starting next September under the new budget measures.

Quick facts

Annual savings
1,000 euros
Weekly subsidy
96.30 euros
Maximum weekly fee cap
280 euros
Implementation date
September 2027

Parents paying for 45 hours of full-time care each week will see their annual expenses drop by at least 1,000 euros. This relief arrives through a two-pronged strategy: increasing the existing 96.30 euro weekly subsidy and lowering the current maximum weekly charge of 280 euros that providers are permitted to bill. While these changes specifically target younger children up to Senior Infants class, the government also intends to extend cost-reduction benefits to families using after-school care services during a child’s first two years of primary school.

For many households, these adjustments represent a significant stride toward the government’s long-term pledge to bring childcare expenses down to 200 euros per month. Beyond direct parental savings, the funding package includes provisions to boost the wages of early-years educators, aiming to stabilize the sector while alleviating the broader cost-of-living pressures facing workers.

Negotiations involving Taoiseach Micheál Martin, Tánaiste Simon Harris, and Minister for Public Expenditure Jack Chambers are currently finalizing these details. According to RTÉ, while childcare remains a primary focus, the possibility of a further reduction in the student contribution fee has become increasingly remote as the cabinet confirms final measures. The Government is simultaneously reviewing an income tax package and energy cost reductions to be included in Tuesday's announcement.

These new rates are scheduled to take effect in September 2027. Officials continue to meet to conclude the exact distribution of funding for the early-years sector, including the specific adjustments to fee caps across creche and aftercare services.
