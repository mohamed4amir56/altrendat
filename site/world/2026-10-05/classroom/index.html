UCLA Scraps Dozens of Classroom Computers to Address Recent Budget Shortfalls

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Oct 2026, 09:30 UTC

UCLA eliminated desktop computers in general assignment classrooms this quarter, forcing instructors to transition to a Bring Your Own Device policy.

For many instructors, the shift means relying on personal laptops instead of institutional hardware, a change that has resulted in unforeseen technical hurdles. Some lecturers now report needing to carry two separate devices to campus just to manage basic lecture projections, while other classrooms lacked the necessary equipment to connect personal computers to projectors during critical first-day sessions. This sudden departure from provided desktop setups has led to lost instruction time as faculty members attempt to troubleshoot hardware issues on the fly.

The policy change is intended to align university operations with current fiscal realities, according to Lucy Avetisyan, UCLA’s associate vice chancellor and chief information officer. While the university seeks to mitigate connectivity issues by installing USB-C cords across 22 campus buildings, many faculty members remain concerned about the impact on their ability to deliver consistent lessons without reliable, pre-installed classroom equipment.

In stark contrast to the removal of equipment at UCLA, other regions are undergoing a massive digital overhaul of their learning environments. In Malaysia, Samsung is partnering with the Ministry of Education to transform traditional school spaces into fully integrated AI-powered classrooms. These environments are being outfitted with Galaxy Tab A9+ devices for students, interactive smartboards for instructors, and climate control systems. This initiative aims to democratize digital access by embedding advanced technology directly into the daily learning experience, ensuring students are equipped with skills necessary for a digital future. While one university scales back its physical infrastructure to manage a budget deficit, international efforts are simultaneously pushing to deepen digital integration through dedicated hardware investments in schools.
