Indianapolis Colts Rout Washington Commanders 30-13 Amidst London Ticket Price Cuts

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Oct 2026, 04:30 UTC

The Indianapolis Colts defeated the Washington Commanders 30-13 in London after the NFL slashed ticket prices to boost attendance at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

While the Indianapolis Colts secured a 30-13 victory over the Washington Commanders, the Sunday match at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium served as a stark indicator of shifting market conditions for the NFL in the UK. Despite an official UK fanbase of 18.6 million, the stadium crowd reached only 60,907, a figure that followed multiple rounds of ticket price reductions by the league. Fans noted a significant contrast in costs, with some spectators seated next to others who paid vastly different amounts for the same experience due to last-minute discounts.

The price adjustments arrive as the league faces scrutiny from long-term supporters. Critics point out that the cheapest ticket prices have increased by 43.9% since 2019, far outpacing the 32.4% rate of UK inflation during the same period. For many, these costs, combined with the removal of season-ticket packages, have turned a premier sporting event into an inaccessible prospect, leading to unsold seats for upcoming fixtures.

Despite the attendance challenges, the NFL maintains its international expansion strategy, with nine regular-season games played abroad during this 2026 season. The league has already hosted matches in Brazil and Australia, with upcoming games scheduled for France, Spain, Germany, and Mexico. Research from American Express estimates that the current trio of London games could generate £90m in economic activity, with visiting fans spending an average of £450 per person.

The London series continues as the Jacksonville Jaguars face the Philadelphia Eagles at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. The final London match of the month is scheduled for 18 October at Wembley Stadium, where the Jacksonville Jaguars will play the Houston Texans.
