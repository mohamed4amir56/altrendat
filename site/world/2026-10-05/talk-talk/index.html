BT rescues TalkTalk from collapse with £400m takeover deal

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Oct 2026, 07:30 UTC

BT has agreed to buy struggling broadband provider TalkTalk out of administration for £400m, serving 2.5 million customers across retail and wholesale operations.

BT, the UK's biggest broadband provider, has agreed to rescue TalkTalk in a £400m deal that ends months of uncertainty over the debt-ridden company's future. TalkTalk, which reported revenues of £1.2bn last year, has been on the brink of administration after accumulating significant debts while losing customers. The company faced a cliff-edge situation with a major monthly payment to network supplier Openreach due at the start of October.

The rescue matters because TalkTalk serves 2.5 million customers across retail and wholesale operations, including vulnerable households and connections that support critical national infrastructure providers across health, emergency services, defence, education, transport, banking and government. If TalkTalk had collapsed, there would have been a genuine risk to these essential services. The government intervened to override competition rules, with Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy stating that phone and broadband services are vital national infrastructure.

TalkTalk began as a challenger to BT in the broadband market before being taken over by private equity in 2021. The company had held talks with various potential buyers including private equity firm Epiris, venture capital investors at Octopus Investments, Vodafone and Opus Broadband, but struggled to reach agreement with any of them. BT chief executive Allison Kirkby said the deal is "the only viable option to keep millions of customers connected and supported."

The takeover still requires approval from the UK's Competition Markets Authority (CMA), which will need to balance concerns about BT's increased control over the broadband market against other considerations. Once approved, TalkTalk's customers will have access to BT's network and services.
