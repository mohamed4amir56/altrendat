John Minto Faces Trial Over $10,321 Damage From Alleged Anti-Genocide Stickers

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Oct 2026, 09:00 UTC

Protester John Minto faces charges of intentional damage for placing stickers on SodaStream products at a Christchurch Noel Leeming store.

Quick facts

Alleged damage cost
$10,321
Next court date
December 11
Charge
Intentional damage

John Minto told a Christchurch District Court judge he believes the matter of his stickers does not warrant the intervention of criminal law. The veteran protester and Palestine Solidarity Network Aotearoa chairman Ben Vorderegger face allegations of causing $10,321 worth of damage to SodaStream products when they allegedly plastered them with anti-genocide stickers at a retail store. Minto argued the items were merely covered in "annoying stickers" and questioned whether taxpayer money should be spent on a trial. Judge Gerard Lynch noted that the $10,000 figure remained a "kicker" if established by the prosecution, though the judge acknowledged the potential cost to taxpayers for a trial. Minto told the court he intends to elect a jury trial while asking the judge to consider dismissing the case before reaching that stage. The court has requested that police return with more specific details regarding the case. The matter was adjourned by Judge Lynch to December 11 to allow for further review of the charges and the financial claims associated with the alleged damage to the store inventory. The proceedings highlight ongoing legal debates regarding protest actions and the thresholds for criminal prosecution in relation to property damage versus political expression in retail environments. As the legal process continues, both the defense and the prosecution are expected to provide further evidence concerning the actual impact and valuation of the stickers on the store's merchandise.
