Christine Fréchette and CAQ routed as Quebec voters demand major change

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Oct 2026, 04:00 UTC

Coalition avenir Québec leader Christine Fréchette conceded defeat in her home riding of Trois-Rivières on October 5, 2026.

The Coalition avenir Québec saw its electoral support crumble, with Christine Fréchette confirming the loss before a small group of supporters in Trois-Rivières shortly before 10:00 p.m. Facing a result far from what was expected, Fréchette assumed full responsibility for the defeat. She acknowledged the clear desire for change among the population, stating that serving as premier was the greatest of privileges. The outcome marks a collapse for a party that previously held 90 seats in 2022 and faced a difficult campaign as it attempted to distance itself from the record of former leader François Legault. The atmosphere at the gathering was described by one activist as resembling a funeral home.

The election results carry significant weight for the province, as the CAQ could be entirely erased from the political map after years of dominance. Multiple high-profile party members suffered losses, including France-Élaine Duranceau, Bernard Drainville, Simon Jolin-Barrette, and Health Minister Sonia Bélanger, who all fell to opposing candidates. The shift reflects broader trends identified in polling, which showed 74% of Quebecers seeking a new government heading into the final week of the campaign.

Despite the electoral rout, Fréchette insisted that the party’s platform—defined as a third way between federalism and nationalism—remains relevant. She indicated that the coalition will continue to advocate for a vision focused on asserting identity, protecting the French language, and bolstering the regional economy. As the dust settles on the 2026 election, the political landscape in Quebec undergoes a transformation, ending the tenure of the current governing party.
