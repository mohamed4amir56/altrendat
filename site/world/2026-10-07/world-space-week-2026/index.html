World Space Week Marks Decades of Human Flight and 80 Nations Orbiting

By Karim Fouad · Published 08 Oct 2026, 00:31 UTC

World Space Week runs annually from October 4 to 10, commemorating humanity's expansion into orbit and space exploration milestones.

If Earth were the size of a standard apple, the boundary to space would begin no further from the surface than the thickness of the fruit's skin. This slim margin, defined by the Karman line at 100km, has been breached by at least 746 people from 52 different nations over the last sixty-nine years. Today, the reach of humanity extends significantly further, with more than 80 countries having successfully placed satellites into orbit since the launch of Sputnik 1. Global participation in space activities remains vital for the continued development of space technology meant for the benefit of all humanity. During the annual week-long observation, schools, astronomy clubs, museums, and space agencies organize science classes, exhibitions, and stargazing sessions designed to educate the public on our progress beyond the atmosphere. Established by the United Nations in 1999, the event centers on the window between October 4 and October 10. This specific timeframe recognizes the launch of Sputnik 1 in 1957 and the entry into force of the Outer Space Treaty in 1967. The latter remains a fundamental piece of international law, ensuring that space is used exclusively for peaceful purposes and preventing any nation from claiming territory within it. While the 2026 celebration highlights a year of significant rocket launch milestones and planetary discoveries, future efforts look toward expanding human presence in space. Ongoing debates among international bodies persist regarding the exact definition of space, with the United States military and NASA utilizing an 80km benchmark while others adhere to the 100km Karman line.
