British Covid cases surge 230 per cent as XFG strain spreads worldwide

By Karim Fouad · Published 08 Oct 2026, 19:31 UTC

The XFG variant, known as the American strain, has driven a sharp increase in UK infections and hospital admissions since August.

Patients diagnosed with the XFG variant frequently present with a characteristic hoarse, croaky voice that distinguishes it from previous iterations of the virus. While other symptoms remain consistent with earlier variants, such as sore throat, cough, fever, and loss of taste or smell, this specific vocal change serves as a common identifier for the strain first identified in January 2025.

Understanding the behavior of XFG is essential for managing the current surge, as health authorities monitor rising transmission rates. The UK Health Security Agency reports that between August and September, infections in Britain climbed from 1,374 to 4,589, a 230 per cent increase. With hospitalizations rising by 15 per cent—and other data points suggesting even higher admission spikes—the strain is now considered the most common in North America and a significant factor in global circulation.

According to the UKHSA, the rise in infections is linked to waning population immunity roughly a year after the previous significant wave. Additionally, the shift toward colder, wetter weather has forced more people indoors, facilitating easier transmission. Experts state that the current Covid-19 vaccine is specifically engineered to target XFG and its related strains to provide protection against severe illness.

To curb the spread, the UKHSA advises individuals experiencing symptoms, such as high temperatures, to remain at home. Those unable to isolate are encouraged to wear well-fitting or surgical face masks in public, avoid crowded spaces, and practice stringent hygiene, including frequent handwashing. Vaccination efforts have currently prioritized those aged 75 and over, residents in care homes, and individuals with weakened immune systems.
