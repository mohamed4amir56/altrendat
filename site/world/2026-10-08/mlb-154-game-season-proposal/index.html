MLB Proposes 154-Game Regular Season Plus Expanded Seven-Game Division Series Format

By Karim Fouad · Published 08 Oct 2026, 19:31 UTC

Major League Baseball has submitted a proposal to the MLB Players Association to reduce the regular season to 154 games while restructuring the postseason.

Quick facts

Proposed regular season games
154
Proposed Division Series format
Best of seven

Major League Baseball’s latest proposal to the MLB Players Association seeks to overhaul the sport's structure by shortening the regular season to 154 games. This reduction aims to introduce a weekly off-day for players while establishing a new national broadcast window designed to showcase top teams and players, according to MLB spokesman Glen Caplin.

For fans, this change intends to minimize weekday afternoon games and create a platform for longer postseason series. The league suggests that a shorter regular season allows for a more significant October, characterized by an expansion of the Division Series from a best-of-five to a best-of-seven format. By modifying the schedule, the league states it can better feature the game's top pitchers on the postseason stage.

The proposal introduces significant shifts in playoff logistics, specifically regarding how opponents are selected. Under the plan, the top seeds in both the Wild Card and Division Series rounds would be granted the power to choose their opponents. In the Wild Card round, the No. 3 seed would select between the No. 5 or No. 6 seed. For the Division Series, the No. 1 seed would select which Wild Card winner they face after that round concludes. MLB expects this mechanism to amplify fan interest, heighten the importance of regular-season records, and fuel new rivalries.

While the league emphasizes player health and broadcast drama as primary drivers, industry analysis from MLB Trade Rumors suggests the focus remains on long-term financial goals, noting the increased revenue potential of expanded postseason broadcast rights. The proposal remains under consideration by the Players Association, with discussions ongoing regarding the impact on total games and the structure of the postseason calendar.
