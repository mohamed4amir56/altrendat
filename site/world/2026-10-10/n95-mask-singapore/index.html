Singapore readies N95 mask distribution as air quality nears very unhealthy levels

By Karim Fouad · Published 10 Oct 2026, 04:30 UTC

Eligible residents can collect N95 masks from community venues when the 24-hour PSI index rises above 200.

Quick facts

24-hour PSI reading
192
Trigger for mask distribution
24-hour PSI above 200
Support offer date
Saturday, Oct 10

The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index recently climbed to 192 in the central region, marking the highest air quality reading recorded during this year's haze season. As the air quality fluctuates, officials are preparing to distribute masks to specific groups when the 24-hour PSI exceeds 200 to protect public health from deteriorating conditions caused by forest fires in Indonesia. Minister for Sustainability and the Environment Grace Fu confirmed that the government is monitoring the situation closely and remains in contact with Indonesian counterparts to offer support for firefighting efforts as dry weather intensifies the crisis. For many Singaporeans, the primary concern is the potential for further spikes in pollution levels that could necessitate increased use of N95 masks and air-conditioned shelters. Access to these protections is critical for vulnerable populations, including seniors, pregnant women, and children, as well as individuals with pre-existing heart or lung conditions. According to the Ministry for Sustainability and the Environment, eligible residents include holders of CHAS Blue and Orange cards, members of the Pioneer and Merdeka Generations, and those receiving ComCare assistance. Masks will be available at designated vending machines or community clubs once the threshold is crossed. Additionally, air-conditioned community clubs and select residential centers are being activated as shelters to provide relief for those needing a cleaner breathing environment. The Home Team and Singapore Armed Forces have also signaled they will reduce outdoor physical training and move activities indoors if air quality indices continue to worsen.
