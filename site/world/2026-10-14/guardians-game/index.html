Fastball Freddy Silences Guardians as Rays Clinch Crucial ALCS Game 2 Win

By Karim Fouad · Published 14 Oct 2026, 10:30 UTC

Freddy Peralta tossed six scoreless innings to lead the Tampa Bay Rays to a 3-1 victory over the Cleveland Guardians, evening the series at 1-1.

Freddy Peralta unleashed a career-high 71 four-seam fastballs on Tuesday, reaching 99.4 mph to keep the Cleveland Guardians off the scoreboard for six dominant innings. Despite the Guardians knowing his reliance on the heater, Peralta's intensity and velocity proved unstoppable at Tropicana Field. The performance marked the longest postseason outing of the right-hander's career as he powered Tampa Bay to a 3-1 victory, effectively leveling the American League Championship Series.

This win is a pivotal result for the Rays, who faced a must-win situation following a comeback loss in the series opener. By countering the Guardians' momentum, Tampa Bay preserved its championship aspirations, forcing a tie that shifts the tactical balance for the remainder of the series. The game highlighted the high-stakes unpredictability of the postseason, where elite pitching can dismantle even the most informed hitting plans.

According to MLB.com, Peralta’s 99-pitch effort included hitting 97 mph or faster 29 times, a significant jump from his previous regular-season metrics. Catcher Liam Hicks guided the strategy, consistently calling for the fastball that the Guardians could not square up. Fox News reports that a defensive error by José Ramírez further hampered Cleveland's ability to stay within striking distance. With the series now knotted at 1-1, the contest moves forward with both teams vying for a trip to the World Series.
