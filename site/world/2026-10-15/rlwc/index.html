Late Jack Bostock try clinches Australia 24-22 comeback win over New Zealand

By Karim Fouad · Published 15 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC · Updated 15 Oct 2026, 13:01 UTC

Australia defeated New Zealand 24-22 in the opening match of the 2026 Rugby League World Cup following a decisive 75th-minute try.

Quick facts

Final Score
Australia 24-22 New Zealand
Event
2026 Rugby League World Cup
Decisive Moment
75th-minute try by Jack Bostock
Injury
Kalyn Ponga, suspected broken arm

The margin of victory arrived in the 75th minute when Jack Bostock secured his second try of the game, snatching the win away from a dominant New Zealand side. The contest featured a display of raw athleticism, including a miraculous opening try from Casey McLean, who managed to ground the ball while his entire body was forced over the sideline. Throughout the match, the New Zealand pack maintained significant control, but Australia relied on opportunistic runaway intercept tries to stay competitive against the persistent pressure.

This loss represents a difficult start for the Kiwis, who now face the prospect of a more challenging semi-final path, potentially against Samoa. The high-intensity performance serves as a reminder of the narrow margins in international play, where defensive lapses or lost possessions can swing a result in the final minutes.

According to the NZ Herald and RNZ, the match was marred by a significant injury for New Zealand. Debutant Kalyn Ponga, who scored two tries and was a central figure in the Kiwis' offensive efforts, suffered a suspected broken arm during the second half. He is expected to miss the remainder of the tournament. The first half saw Australia’s Kotoni Staggs sent to the sin bin for interfering with Kodi Nikorima, which allowed New Zealand to level the score at 10-apiece heading into the break. While New Zealand held the lead multiple times throughout the second half, Reece Walsh also contributed a long-range try for Australia, utilizing his speed to bypass the defense and ultimately seal the outcome for the defending champions.
