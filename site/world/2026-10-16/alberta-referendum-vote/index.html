Prime Minister Mark Carney Warns Foreign Nations Against Meddling In Alberta Referendum

By Karim Fouad · Published 16 Oct 2026, 04:31 UTC

Prime Minister Mark Carney urges Albertans to vote in the upcoming October 19 referendum to ensure the integrity of the process against foreign interference.

Quick facts

Referendum Date
October 19
Primary Official
Prime Minister Mark Carney
Key Federal Project
Pacific Link pipeline

Leading figures in the separatist movement have traveled to the United States seeking assistance, with U.S. officials Scott Bessent and Howard Lutnik openly discussing Alberta as a potential partner or commenting on federal trade talks. Prime Minister Mark Carney told Global News that national security agencies are actively monitoring for covert foreign interference, though he declined to provide specific details regarding those investigations. The upcoming vote is critical as it will decide whether Alberta initiates the legal process to hold a binding referendum on separation from Canada. The outcome of this decision carries significant weight for provincial politics, where the separatist debate has remained a dominant issue for more than a year. Carney emphasized that the most effective defense against outside influence is for residents to participate directly in the democratic process.

Speaking in Calgary, Carney stated that Ottawa has made it absolutely clear that foreign governments must remain uninvolved in domestic elections. He positioned voting as a fundamental duty for Albertans to ensure their own voices define the province's future. During his visit to the province, the Prime Minister highlighted recent federal investments, including the Pacific Link pipeline project intended for the British Columbia coast, as part of his administration's commitment to Alberta.

In addition to the separation question, ballots include five distinct immigration inquiries. While a rally in Calgary highlighted immigration as a central theme, Calgary Mayor Jeromy Farkas was the only official at the event to explicitly call for a "No" vote on the remaining nine items appearing on the ballot.
