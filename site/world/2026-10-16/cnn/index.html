James Talarico defends moderate platform as tight Texas Senate race intensifies

By Karim Fouad · Published 16 Oct 2026, 18:31 UTC

Texas Democratic Senate nominee James Talarico faced direct questioning from voters on contentious policy issues during a televised CNN town hall Wednesday.

James Talarico addressed a skeptical crowd including Republicans and independents during Wednesday’s event, directly answering questions regarding his past comments about God, his stance on abortion, and his approach to foreign aid. The town hall, which featured a young Republican activist who questioned Talarico's faith, highlighted the Democrat's attempt to bridge political divides in a state currently central to national Senate control. Talarico explicitly identified himself as a moderate candidate, promising to prioritize the needs of Texans over party allegiances.

This town hall matters as the race remains highly competitive, with recent polling placing Talarico in a narrow lead over his Republican opponent, Ken Paxton. For voters, the event provided a rare, unscripted look at how the Democratic nominee handles pressure on issues ranging from the military campaign in Gaza to reproductive rights. As Talarico seeks to appeal to a broad base, his ability to manage these encounters with undecided or critical voters could determine the outcome of the close contest.

According to The Guardian, Talarico engaged with voters such as Jodi Hunt, who challenged his pro-choice position through a Catholic lens. Talarico responded by framing abortion access as a public health issue while suggesting that the Bible remains silent on the topic. Host Erin Burnett noted at the beginning of the broadcast that Ken Paxton declined an invitation to appear alongside his challenger. Talarico also used the platform to position himself against the ultra-wealthy, designating them as a common enemy in his campaign rhetoric. The event, co-hosted by the Jubilee Media digital platform, marks a critical moment in the ongoing campaign as both candidates navigate the final stretches of their race for the Texas Senate seat.
