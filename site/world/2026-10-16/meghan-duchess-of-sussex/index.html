Duchess of Sussex Relaunches Public UK Appearances with Nottingham Charity Away Day

By Karim Fouad · Published 16 Oct 2026, 07:10 UTC

Meghan Markle returned to Nottingham for her first joint UK engagement in years, visiting local charities and meeting with volunteers.

The atmosphere in Nottingham mirrored the couple's first visit in 2017, with the Duchess of Sussex engaging in informal activities such as baking cupcakes, preparing West African food, and playing basketball. This event, which involved accepting flowers and greeting crowds, marked the first time the Duchess participated in a public outing of this nature on British soil in several years. Observers noted that the Duchess displayed a familiar American confidence while interacting with a largely youthful crowd, mostly aged under 30.

This return to public engagement signifies a move by the Duke and Duchess to carry out their own events on their own terms. While the Royal Family classifies the pair as private citizens without official royal status, the visit highlighted the Sussexes' continued ability to draw public interest. The event served as a sharp contrast to the official duties undertaken by King Charles III, who was simultaneously present at the ceremonial opening of the Senedd in Cardiff.

Reports indicate that the Sussexes do not consider themselves bound by previous agreements regarding their royal roles, with sources suggesting the couple never consented to being fully excluded from public life. The visit occurred following the couple's return to the UK with their children, Archie and Lilibet. While Buckingham Palace maintains that the Duke and Duchess operate independently, their actions remain a source of complexity for the monarchy. The visit is framed by the couple as they establish their own path, picking up activities that resemble their previous royal engagements but without the associated formal duties.
