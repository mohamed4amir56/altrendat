All Blacks seek 13th straight win as injury-hit Wallabies chase redemption

By Karim Fouad · Published 17 Oct 2026, 06:31 UTC

The All Blacks and Australia meet in Sydney for the second Bledisloe Cup test following a dominant 50-19 result in Auckland.

Quick facts

Venue
Accor Stadium, Sydney
Previous result
50-19 to All Blacks
Referee
Luke Pearce
Bledisloe Cup streak
24 seasons

The All Blacks arrive in Sydney on the back of a crushing 50-19 win at Eden Park, a result that marked just the ninth time in history that Australia has conceded a half-century in a men's international match. New Zealand now holds an unbeaten streak of 12 consecutive victories against their rivals, a run they intend to maintain at Accor Stadium despite significant personnel challenges.

This match represents a pivotal moment for the Wallabies, who are attempting to rebuild confidence under coach Les Kiss. For spectators, the fixture anchors a massive quadruple-header in Sydney, though the home side faces intense pressure to improve upon last week’s defensive collapse. The result remains critical for Australia as they navigate a challenging period of squad instability, particularly at the fly-half position.

Both teams are battling extensive injury lists. New Zealand will field a reshuffled side, with George Bower, Fabian Holland, and Josh Moorby entering the starting lineup due to absences including Asafo Aumua and Ethan Blackadder. Australia is managing similar turmoil, forced to bench 22-year-old Harry McLaughlin-Phillips, who is in line to become Wallaby No. 1,001. The Wallabies are also without Len Ikitau and Jordan Petaia, while Declan Meredith steps into the No. 10 jersey following a quad injury to Ben Donaldson.

Referee Luke Pearce will officiate the encounter, having been promoted after serving as an assistant last week. While New Zealand has already secured the Bledisloe Cup for the 24th consecutive season, Saturday’s test serves as the final opportunity for the Wallabies to find a positive result before the end of their current campaign.
