SpaceX Falcon 9 launch could create spectacular space jellyfish over Florida skies

By Karim Fouad · Published 20 Oct 2026, 02:01 UTC

SpaceX targets a 6:30 a.m. Tuesday launch window from Cape Canaveral for the Bandwagon-5 rideshare mission.

Quick facts

Launch Time
6:30 a.m. Tuesday
Mission
Bandwagon-5
Rocket
Falcon 9
Location
Cape Canaveral, Florida
Backup Window
Wednesday

The glowing, bulb-shaped cloud with wispy trails, known as a space jellyfish, occurs when rocket exhaust expands in the high atmosphere and is illuminated by sunlight while the ground remains in darkness. This visual phenomenon recently captivated observers just a week ago, offering a display that creates an otherworldly effect for those watching from below. The mission was originally scheduled for Monday morning but faced a delay due to unfavorable weather conditions affecting recovery operations. Following the launch, the first stage booster will attempt a landing on the A Shortfall of Gravitas droneship stationed in the Atlantic Ocean. This flight marks the 19th mission for this specific booster, which previously supported NASA’s Crew-9 flight and numerous Starlink launches. If the Tuesday morning attempt is unsuccessful, SpaceX has established a backup launch window for Wednesday. The Bandwagon-5 mission is carrying a variety of smaller satellites along with re-entry vehicles designed to retrieve these assets from orbit at a later time. Observers in Central Florida are encouraged to monitor the skies during the one-hour window beginning at 6:30 a.m. to capture the potential light display. SpaceX has requested that individuals who manage to photograph the formation submit their images for potential feature on local media platforms. This launch continues the company's active schedule of orbital missions from the Florida coast.
