Trump demands new Ukrainian leadership after Zelenskyy rejects controversial Russian diesel fuel deal

By Karim Fouad · Published 20 Oct 2026, 11:30 UTC

President Donald Trump openly called for the replacement of President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on October 10 following Ukraine's continued strikes on Russian energy infrastructure.

The tension between the two leaders reached a breaking point after Trump announced a deal with Vladimir Putin to import Russian diesel to the United States. While Trump argued this move would combat rising American fuel prices, experts warn the agreement provides billions to Moscow for its ongoing military campaign and will fail to lower global costs. President Zelenskyy criticized the arrangement, leading to a sharp escalation in rhetoric from Washington. On October 10, Trump publicly told reporters that Ukraine should find a new leader capable of making deals. The administration has since pursued measures that appear designed to punish the Ukrainian government for prioritizing its defense over the energy ceasefire proposal that both Kyiv and Moscow have already denied. The political conflict carries significant weight as the U.S. approaches midterm elections, with the White House blaming Ukraine’s attacks on Russian refineries for domestic diesel price hikes. This shift in policy has directly impacted military operations on the ground as well. Access to Starshield, the military-grade satellite internet service provided by SpaceX, was interrupted last week. While SpaceX CEO Elon Musk confirmed the service stoppage, he stated the directive originated from the United States government rather than the company itself. Following these developments, Ukraine resumed its retaliatory strikes against Russian energy infrastructure, defying the administration's stated goals for an energy ceasefire. The diplomatic rift continues to deepen as Ukraine maintains its commitment to attacking key Russian industrial sites despite mounting pressure from the U.S. government. Future relations remain uncertain as both administrations navigate this clash over military strategy and energy policy.
