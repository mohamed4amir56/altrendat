UK launches 'Be Ready' campaign urging households to stock three-day emergency supplies

By Karim Fouad · Published 22 Oct 2026, 17:01 UTC

The UK government has started a national resilience conversation warning the public to prepare for cyber attacks, natural disasters, and supply chain disruptions by stockpiling food, water, and first aid supplies.

The Defence Secretary Wes Streeting announced the "Be Ready" campaign, telling the public to prepare for threats ranging from wildfires and flooding to sophisticated cyber attacks that could disable supermarket payment systems. "How would we cope if the payment systems at the local supermarket went down?" Streeting asked, describing these as "everyday challenges" the country might face.

The campaign matters because the government says severe weather and cyber attacks can disrupt critical services like power, water, phones, and the internet. By preparing households now, emergency services can focus resources on those who need more support during crises, according to Security Minister Dan Jarvis MP. The advice aims to improve the UK's overall resilience as a nation.

The government recommends households maintain three-day emergency kits including non-perishable food, bottled water, a torch, sleeping bags, first aid items, and an FM radio tuned to local news stations. Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle previously warned the public to prepare for extreme weather as a strong El Niño event develops. Streeting made his announcement at the Rheinmetall Telford Gun Hall, where production of tank gun barrels returns to the UK for the first time in 20 years. Similar campaigns already exist in France, Ireland, and the Netherlands, where such preparedness is considered "a normal part of everyday life," Jarvis noted.
