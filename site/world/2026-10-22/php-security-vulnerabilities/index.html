Global scramble as critical PHP flaw forces millions of sites offline

By Karim Fouad · Published 22 Oct 2026, 20:31 UTC

Governments and businesses took services offline after a critical PHP flaw threatened to expose 70% of the world's websites to potential hacks.

Millions of automated attempts to exploit the flaw were recorded globally following the discovery of the vulnerability in the PHP programming language. This surge in malicious activity prompted international organizations and government entities to pull websites off the internet to install necessary security patches. Described by former NCSC chief executive Ciaran Martin as the most serious vulnerability discovered in five years, the flaw poses a severe risk to web servers worldwide.

The widespread disruption highlights the fragility of digital infrastructure, as PHP underpins approximately 70% of all websites. The vulnerability allows attackers to potentially take control of servers, steal sensitive data, and disrupt critical digital services. While the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) in the UK has issued 50,000 alerts to impacted organizations, the situation remains precarious for smaller entities like schools, charities, and local governments that may lack the specialized cybersecurity support required to implement these complex updates.

According to the BBC, the UK government proactively took 32 services offline to prevent potential breaches, emphasizing that these were precautionary measures rather than evidence of successful hacking. Tech minister Baroness Lloyd of Effra confirmed that services are currently being reviewed and updated. The NCSC, which called the scale of the vulnerability unprecedented, noted that organizations running older legacy systems face a steeper challenge, as standard patches may not be compatible, forcing them to replace hardware or software entirely.

While no successful breaches have been confirmed to date, experts warn that the full impact may take months to manifest. The NCSC continues to collaborate with international partners and technology firms to mitigate the exposure. Organizations are advised to prioritize system updates immediately to secure their infrastructure against ongoing exploitation attempts.
