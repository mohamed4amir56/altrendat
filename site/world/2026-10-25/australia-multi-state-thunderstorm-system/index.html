Massive Multi-State Storm System Triggers Severe Alerts Across Eastern Australia

By Karim Fouad · Published 25 Oct 2026, 08:01 UTC

The Bureau of Meteorology has issued severe thunderstorm warnings for southern Queensland, western NSW, the ACT, and eastern Victoria.

Wind gusts reaching 100km/h were recorded in the central west of New South Wales around midday on Sunday as a broad, unusually large storm system swept across the region. The event follows intense weather conditions that saw the Sunshine Coast record 289mm of rain this month, more than triple the October average of 90mm, according to the Bureau of Meteorology.

This multi-state system carries significant risks, including flash flooding, power outages, large hail, and damaging winds. The volatile conditions result from a cold front pushing against warm, humid air along the eastern seaboard, finally displacing a high-pressure system that had been blocking weather fronts for 10 days. Beyond property damage, Victorian authorities have issued a high-risk warning for epidemic thunderstorm asthma, specifically for the East Gippsland region.

The Bureau of Meteorology notes that a continuous line of storms currently extends from the Snowy Mountains to the Queensland border. Meteorologist David Crock stated that while conditions will persist through Sunday evening, Monday marks the final major phase of this system, which should provide relief for residents in south-east Queensland who have experienced recent weather whiplash. The SES has already managed hundreds of callouts, including 124 requests for assistance in a single night following heavy rain on the Sunshine Coast.

As the cold front advances, residents in central and eastern Victoria, as well as northern NSW, should expect the weather to intensify further. Airlines have begun preparing for potential travel disruptions caused by the hazardous conditions.
