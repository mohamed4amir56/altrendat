Peter Berg's Netflix film traces All-American footballers from Pearl Harbor to Okinawa

By Karim Fouad · Published 25 Oct 2026, 14:31 UTC

Director Peter Berg's new Netflix war drama "The Mosquito Bowl" tells the true story of elite college football players who volunteered for the Marines after Pearl Harbor and fought in World War II's Pacific theater, including a legendary Christmas Eve 1944 football game at Guadalcanal.

Peter Berg directs this adaptation of Buzz Bissinger's book about four All-American football stars—Nicholas Galitzine as John McLaughry, Ray Nicholson as Tony Butkovich, Tom Francis as David Schreiner, and Bill Skarsgard as Bob Bauman—who enlisted in the Marines following the 1941 bombing of Pearl Harbor. The film centers on their journey from the college gridiron to combat in the Pacific.

The story gains its distinctive angle from a legendary football game played between two Marine divisions stationed at Guadalcanal on Christmas Eve 1944. This match between the young men trained for war sets the film apart from traditional war dramas, offering both a sports narrative and a meditation on sacrifice. The cast represents rising Hollywood talent, with Galitzine a Brown alumnus in the film, Nicholson playing a Purdue alumnus projected as an NFL prospect, and Francis and Skarsgard both portraying Wisconsin graduates.

Berg, known for "Friday Night Lights," tackles the eternal theme of war's burden on youth. The film opens with a Herbert Hoover quote: "Older men declare war. But it is youth that must fight and die." The narrative builds toward the Battle of Okinawa, described as the last and costliest World War II battle in the Pacific theater, examining the human cost of conflict through these athletes' experiences. The film premiered at AFI Fest before its Netflix release.
