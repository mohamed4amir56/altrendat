NFL Spotter and Broadcaster David Moulton Dies Following Six-Year Cancer Battle

By Karim Fouad · Published 27 Oct 2026, 02:31 UTC

The longtime sports media figure and former NFL broadcast assistant died at age 59 while in hospice care.

Quick facts

Age at death
59
Date of death announcement
Saturday, October 24
Hospice location
Douglas Center for Caring
Professional roles
NFL spotter, ABC7 Sports Director, radio co-host, voice of FGCU Athletics

David Moulton spent six years fighting stage four renal cell cancer, a condition that eventually metastasized to his bones two years ago. His wife, Julie, confirmed he passed away in hospice care at the Douglas Center for Caring on the night of Friday, October 23, according to reports citing his death announcement on Saturday, October 24. Even while undergoing various medical treatments, Moulton maintained his professional commitments, including his role as an NFL spotter and his career in local media.

The impact of his loss is felt throughout the sports broadcasting community, where he was highly regarded for his work behind the scenes. Troy Aikman, who worked closely with Moulton in the broadcast booth starting in 2018, described him as a loyal and dependable colleague who became a cherished friend. Aikman noted that Moulton was a warrior until the very end, serving as his right-hand man while helping the broadcast team track players and analyze games for millions of viewers.

Moulton’s career spanned decades, beginning with his arrival in Fort Myers in the late nineties as a sports anchor. He served as the Sports Director at ABC7 and became a recognizable voice as a co-host of the "Miller and Moulton" radio show. Beyond his national work with the NFL, he was known as the voice of FGCU Athletics and a staple of sports media across the Gulf Coast. He first publicly shared his diagnosis in 2020 after the discovery of a large tumor led to the surgical removal of his left kidney, but despite persistent treatment efforts, the cancer progressed to stage four.

His family has expressed gratitude for the widespread support received throughout his long battle. Colleagues and listeners remember him as a friendly face who brought consistent dedication to his craft throughout his time in sports journalism.
