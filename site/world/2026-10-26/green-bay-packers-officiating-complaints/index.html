Packers safety Xavier McKinney slams NFL officials following controversial Lions game loss

By Karim Fouad · Published 26 Oct 2026, 19:57 UTC

Safety Xavier McKinney faces an expected league fine after publicly criticizing officiating during the Green Bay Packers' loss to the Detroit Lions.

Quick facts

Game result
Packers loss to Lions
Critic
Xavier McKinney
Key official involved
Terry Killens Jr.

Xavier McKinney did not hold back after Sunday’s loss, calling the league’s officiating "bulls--t" during a one-minute postgame rant. The Packers safety’s frustration peaked regarding a third-quarter sequence where Detroit’s Amon-Ra St. Brown collided with umpire Terry Killens Jr. while rising from a tackle. Although no whistle had blown, officials flagged defensive lineman Devonte Wyatt for unnecessary roughness after St. Brown’s leg struck the official in the groin. The penalty aided a Lions drive that resulted in a touchdown and a 17-14 lead.

Public criticism of game officials by players or club employees is strictly prohibited by NFL policy, making a fine for McKinney all but certain. Beyond the locker room, the broadcast booth also questioned the officiating standard. Fox analyst Tom Brady repeatedly challenged calls throughout the game, including a roughing the passer penalty against Packers linebacker Brenton Cox Jr. and an illegal hit call against Lions linebacker Derrick Barnes. Brady expressed disbelief at a late-game roughing the passer penalty assessed to Packers defensive tackle Karl Brooks, labeling the contact as a clean play.

McKinney voiced his lack of confidence in the current officiating, highlighting specific drives where he felt the flags lacked logic. The incident with the umpire, where Killens Jr. threw a flag while on the ground, served as a primary point of contention for the safety. According to McKinney, the official acted out of anger without clearly identifying the player involved in the collision. The league’s stance on such public comments remains firm, as the organization seeks to protect the integrity of its officiating crews from external questioning by personnel and team stakeholders.
