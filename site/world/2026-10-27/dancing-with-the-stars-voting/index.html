Alfonso Ribeiro Denies Dancing With The Stars Rigging Claims After Podcast Comments

By Karim Fouad · Published 28 Oct 2026, 01:01 UTC

Host Alfonso Ribeiro rejected allegations that show eliminations are fixed after former contestants suggested producers influence final results.

During the October 22 episode of the LexLos podcast, former contestant Alexa PenaVega recounted a conversation from season 21 with fellow competitor Hayes Grier. Grier, who commanded millions of social media followers, reportedly felt the show was rigged after landing in the bottom three. Alexa noted that while she believes viewer votes and judge scores matter, the program is also heavily produced to follow a specific narrative. She pointed to season 21 winner Bindi Irwin, suggesting producers maintained her presence on the show because of her compelling story. Further, Alexa and Carlos PenaVega claimed that production often knows who will be eliminated before the live broadcast because travel arrangements for press appearances on programs like The View must be finalized in advance. According to Alexa, her professional partner, Mark Ballas, was notified of their impending elimination through such industry connections before it was announced on air.

The discussion highlights ongoing tensions regarding transparency in reality competition formats. For viewers, these claims question the weight of public participation versus behind-the-scenes decision-making in determining which celebrities advance. The debate is not limited to season 21; season 18 contestant Sean Avery previously claimed his early exit was fueled by personal disagreements with a producer rather than his standing in the competition.

Host and season 19 winner Alfonso Ribeiro pushed back against these assertions when the podcast clip surfaced on social media. Ribeiro dismissed the rigging claims as inaccurate, stating that keeping popular contestants in the competition is basic logic for the show's success. Alexa later clarified that the social media clips of her podcast appearance were taken out of context, noting that her intention was to tell a story about Grier's perspective rather than present a formal accusation.
