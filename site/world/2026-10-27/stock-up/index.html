Shoppers clear shelves of tinned food and torches after government stockpile warning

By Karim Fouad · Published 27 Oct 2026, 11:01 UTC

Supermarkets report massive surges in sales of emergency essentials after the Defence Secretary advised the public to prepare for potential national crises.

Retailers have recorded a 160% jump in radio sales and a 40% surge in tinned food purchases as households rush to assemble emergency kits. Maplin reported that torch sales nearly quadrupled within three days, while major online grocer Ocado saw specific brands of bottled water and soup ranges sell out completely following the government’s "be ready" announcement. Managing director at Maplin, Ollie Marshall, noted that consumers are acting on advice to secure affordable products that maintain household connectivity during power outages.

This trend follows a call from Defence Secretary Wes Streeting for citizens to store three litres of water per person daily, along with baby food and non-perishable items. The initiative aims to improve national resilience against threats such as cyber attacks or extreme weather events, including recent disruption caused by storms. While supermarkets like Iceland maintain healthy supply levels, other chains continue to work with partners to restock daily as demand remains high.

The surge in preparedness activity comes amid reports of vulnerabilities in web architecture and concerns regarding the security of undersea cable networks. Defence Secretary Streeting praised the public for taking practical steps to manage potential disruptions from incidents like burst pipes or flooding. As stores manage intermittent shortages, officials urge the public to continue checking for replenished inventory as they build their three-day survival kits.
