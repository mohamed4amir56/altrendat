Aaron Ashby Sets Historic Postseason Scoreless Record During Brewers World Series Loss

By Karim Fouad · Published 28 Oct 2026, 05:31 UTC

Aaron Ashby established a new MLB record for consecutive scoreless innings by a reliever in a single postseason during Tuesday's 4-3 defeat to the Tampa Bay Rays.

Quick facts

Game Result
Tampa Bay Rays 4, Milwaukee Brewers 3
Record
18 consecutive scoreless innings
Next Game
Wednesday

Aaron Ashby sprinted past Andrew Miller’s record by delivering 2 1/3 perfect innings during Game 4 of the World Series on Tuesday night. By retiring seven consecutive batters after entering in the ninth inning at Tropicana Field, Ashby extended his dominant postseason streak to 18 total frames, according to the Elias Sports Bureau. This feat solidifies his status as the only pitcher in history to record at least five outs in six or more consecutive postseason appearances without allowing a single run.

For the Brewers, Ashby’s performance has been the backbone of their late-season success. Following a career path that once included shoulder surgery and a stint at Triple-A Nashville, the left-hander has transformed into a high-leverage relief ace. This reliability has allowed manager Pat Murphy to utilize him frequently, often calling upon him for multiple innings on consecutive nights to keep the team’s championship hopes alive.

Despite Ashby’s historic performance on Tuesday, which sent the game into extra innings, the Brewers ultimately fell 4-3 to the Rays in 12 frames. While the Brewers led in the 12th after a solo home run by Cooper Pratt, Tampa Bay successfully rallied to win the game. The result forces a Game 5 between the two clubs on Wednesday.

Ashby’s streak now surpasses the 16-inning single-postseason record previously held by Andrew Miller, who set the mark while playing for Cleveland in 2016. Reflecting on the achievement, Miller told 620 WTMJ that he was not concerned about losing the record, instead praising Ashby for his crucial role as the team's "glue guy" during this postseason run.
