Arab Parties Surge to 17 Seats as Election Deadlock Hits Israeli Parliament

By Karim Fouad · Published 28 Oct 2026, 16:01 UTC

Partial results show Netanyahu’s bloc holds 54 seats and the opposition 49, leaving both sides short of the 61-seat majority required to form a government.

Palestinian Israelis represent approximately 20% of the population, and their turnout has proven critical as the Joint List and Ra’am parties combined to secure at least 17 seats in the 120-member Knesset. This record performance signals a significant revival of the Arab vote, forcing major political blocs to confront a reality where they cannot disregard such a large portion of the electorate.

The inability of the main parties to secure a majority highlights the deep ideological divides defining the current political landscape. While a potential coalition between the opposition alliance led by Gadi Eisenkot and the Arab-led parties could reach 66 seats and unseat Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, both leading Jewish blocs have explicitly ruled out such an partnership. Observers note that these rejections are driven by a systemic political veto and a refusal to share power, despite the electoral arithmetic making their support mathematically indispensable for a majority.

According to The Guardian, with 92% of ballots counted, the primary challenge remains the lack of a clear path to the 61 seats needed to govern. Al Jazeera reports that analysts characterize the refusal to collaborate as a choice to prioritize ideological loyalty tests over the practical opportunity to shift the government. The Joint List has already secured at least 11 of the seats won by the Arab-led parties.

As the final ballots are processed, the focus turns to whether the rigidity of these political alliances will force Israel into another round of elections. The General Secretary of Hadash, Amjad Shbita, expressed hope that the opposition might adopt a more flexible approach rather than facing a repeat election cycle, though no formal negotiations between these groups have commenced.
