Hawaii Islands Brace for Flooding as Torrential Rain Swamps Eastern Counties

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Oct 2026, 08:31 UTC

A statewide flood watch remains in effect for all islands except Kauai and Niihau through early Thursday due to persistent heavy rainfall.

Quick facts

Flood Watch Duration
Through early Thursday
Rainfall Rate
0.5 to 2.5 inches per hour
Closed Roads
Piilani Highway, Uakea Road, Keanini Drive
Affected Islands
Oahu, Molokai, Lanai, Maui, Kahoolawe, Hawaii Island

Rainfall rates reaching up to 2.5 inches per hour have forced road closures and triggered emergency measures across Hawaii. In Hana, police shuttered the Piilani Highway at mile marker 51, while local authorities opened a temporary evacuation site at Helene Hall on Keawa Place to provide a safe gathering space for residents. The heavy precipitation prompted the total closure of all schools on Molokai on Wednesday, leaving officials to manage the immediate impact of inundated roads and rising water levels. The current weather pattern is driven by a surface trough carrying an airmass holding double the usual moisture content, which has resulted in persistent, unstable conditions across the state. According to the National Weather Service, Doppler radar identified the most severe downpours hitting windward areas of Oahu and eastern Maui, specifically impacting towns like Kailua and Kaneohe. While some areas experienced only light drizzle, high-capacity moisture levels have maintained a consistent threat of minor flooding in low-lying and windward neighborhoods. Residents should anticipate the flood watch to persist through early Thursday as the trough moves eastward. Meteorologists expect conditions to show gradual improvement throughout Thursday and Friday, with a return of trade winds by the weekend. While the forecast for Halloween trick-or-treating currently suggests mainly light impacts in windward and mountain areas, the First Alert Weather Team continues to monitor the region for any shifts in the trough's path.
