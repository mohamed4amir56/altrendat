Milwaukee Brewers Topple Rays in Game 5 to Clinch First World Series

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Oct 2026, 14:23 UTC

The Milwaukee Brewers secured their first World Series title in the 58-year franchise history by defeating the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 on Wednesday.

Jacob Misiorowski delivered a masterclass for the Milwaukee Brewers, firing 5 2/3 innings of two-hit ball before a right calf cramp forced his exit. While the young ace kept the Tampa Bay Rays silent, rookie Cooper Pratt fueled the offense with two hits and two RBIs, propelling the Brewers to a 4-1 victory in Game 5. The win at Tropicana Field transformed the stadium into a celebration for the visiting Milwaukee faithful, who had spent the series chanting behind the dugout. For a team playing in the majors' smallest market, this championship represents the pinnacle of a 58-year wait, finally overcoming the heartbreak of their previous 1982 World Series appearance.

This title victory acts as a sharp rebuke to the long-standing narrative that small-market franchises cannot compete for baseball's top prize. Despite finishing the 2026 season with a franchise-record 103 regular-season wins and their fourth consecutive National League Central title, the team had often been dismissed by critics as an outlier. By sweeping the Dodgers and dismantling the Rays in five games, the Brewers silenced the skepticism regarding their economic viability and on-field endurance.

Manager Pat Murphy, who took the helm in 2024 and earned back-to-back Manager of the Year honors, credited the collective effort of the 2026 roster for the historic result. The series saw individual brilliance from World Series MVP William Contreras, who hit a key RBI double, and rookie sensation Jackson Chourio, who set a five-game series record with 11 hits. The achievement follows a season where Milwaukee dominated the standings for 149 days, consistently defying the odds.

Fans in Milwaukee can now prepare for a victory parade as the team brings the trophy home. This win officially concludes the 2026 season on the highest note, cementing the legacy of a group that captain Christian Yelich described as the best feeling for a city that has waited a long time for a championship.
