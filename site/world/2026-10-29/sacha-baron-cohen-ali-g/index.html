Sacha Baron Cohen revives Ali G for misguided comedy film Who Iz I

By Karim Fouad · Published 29 Oct 2026, 04:01 UTC

Sacha Baron Cohen brings back his iconic 2000s character Ali G in the new film Ali G: Who Iz I?, which attempts to navigate modern culture through a series of elaborate pranks.

Quick facts

Film title
Ali G: Who Iz I?
Original show
Da Ali G Show
Director
Sacha Baron Cohen

Ali G discovers a fax from HBO offering him a multimillion-dollar deal to return to television, only to arrive in Los Angeles and realize the offer expired 22 years ago. Left without prospects after squandering his previous earnings and moving into his late grandmother's flat, the character attempts to pivot to a career as a TikTok content creator. He films videos featuring mukbangs and outfit hauls alongside local politicians and retired military personnel in an effort to regain his former fame.

The film marks Sacha Baron Cohen’s directorial debut and features a mix of live-ammo hoaxes rather than a traditional narrative structure. By returning to a character that rose to prominence in the early 2000s, the project forces Baron Cohen to confront his own fame, as he is now often recognized by the public he seeks to prank. To manage this challenge, the production utilizes body doubles and covert reshoots to pull off its high-stakes satirical stunts.

According to TheWrap, the movie struggles to find relevance in a culture that has moved past the character’s original style of humor. TheGuardian notes that the project also incorporates performances from previous characters, including the security consultant Erran Morad and liberal activist Dr. Nira Cain-N’Degrocello. One unnamed Republican leader who appeared in the film has already expressed intentions to sue the comedian, labeling the experience an embarrassment.

Ultimately, the production relies on clarifying who is in on the jokes, appearing to address a new generation of viewers who may be unfamiliar with the original series. The film captures a performer attempting to force his legacy into a fragmented, modern media landscape where his original shock tactics face different societal standards.
