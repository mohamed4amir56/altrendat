Prime Minister Andy Burnham admits first Budget fell short of radical promises

By Karim Fouad · Published 30 Oct 2026, 13:01 UTC · Updated 30 Oct 2026, 13:31 UTC

Prime Minister Andy Burnham conceded his first Budget was not radical enough due to limited room for fiscal manoeuvre.

Quick facts

Budget fiscal shortfall
£28bn
National debt milestone
£3tn
Tax raid target
£4.3bn

Prime Minister Andy Burnham admitted that his first Budget lacked the boldness he campaigned on, stating that the "room for manoeuvre wasn't what I would have hoped it to be." This candid assessment comes as the government faces mounting pressure from allies and MPs who describe the fiscal plan as "undistinguished" and "not radical at all."

For the public and government departments, this admission signals significant uncertainty ahead. While the Prime Minister noted that a payment to 20 million households serves as a significant step, the Institute for Fiscal Studies warns that the Chancellor has left a "mountain to climb." By avoiding major decisions in his debut speech, John Healey has set the stage for what experts describe as a "brutal" spending review next year.

According to The Telegraph, the Prime Minister acknowledged that the volatility of current times limited the scope of the Budget, despite his July pledge to reverse decades of neoliberalism. The plan included a £4.3bn tax raid on landlords and savers to address economic pressures. However, The Guardian reports that these measures failed to cover a £28bn shortfall in public finances, potentially necessitating real-terms cuts to public services.

As the government looks toward the next election, the lack of substantial tax-and-spend changes has drawn sharp criticism from the Labour pressure group Mainstream. They argue that the public cannot simply wait for future cycles to see promised change. With national debt recently exceeding £3tn for the first time, the administration must now navigate a narrow fiscal path to avoid a return to austerity measures.
