SSS and DICT partnership targets 1.5 million Filipino freelancers for social coverage

By Karim Fouad · Published 30 Oct 2026, 13:31 UTC

The Social Security System and the Department of Information and Communications Technology signed an agreement on October 21 to bring freelancers and virtual assistants into the national social security fold.

Quick facts

Agreement Date
October 21, 2026
Target Beneficiaries
1.5 million freelancers and virtual assistants
Location
Quezon City

The agreement formalizes a strategic partnership between the Social Security System and the Department of Information and Communications Technology to reach an estimated 1.5 million freelancers and virtual assistants working within the Philippines. By integrating this digital workforce into the system, the agencies aim to guarantee that remote workers receive identical protections and benefits currently afforded to traditional employees. This expansion ensures that individuals have access to support during periods of sickness, maternity leave, disability, retirement, or death, bridging a significant gap in the modern labor market. SSS President and Chief Executive Officer Victor Alfonso A. Limlingan highlighted that these workers represent a vital and growing segment of the national economy, making their inclusion essential for comprehensive social security coverage. The initiative is designed to leave no worker behind, aiming to make social security benefits more responsive to the specific needs of those navigating the digital landscape. According to the SSS, the collaboration will focus on increasing awareness, registration, and compliance with the Social Security Act of 2018 among the freelance community. Through this partnership, the agencies will prioritize capability-building and competency development to support the sustainability of the online freelancing industry. Both agencies confirmed the signing of the Memorandum of Understanding occurred at the DICT office located in Quezon City. This effort reflects a broader commitment to ensuring that all workers, regardless of their employment status or setting, remain protected under the law. Moving forward, the partnership aims to streamline the registration process and improve overall access for this segment of the workforce, ensuring that online workers can secure their financial future through established social security mechanisms.
