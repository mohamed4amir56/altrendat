Torrential Kauai rains surge at two inches hourly forcing Kuhio Highway closure

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Nov 2026, 05:31 UTC · Updated 01 Nov 2026, 06:01 UTC

The National Weather Service has extended a flash flood warning for Kauai until 8:45 p.m. due to persistent heavy rainfall.

Rain is falling across Kauai at a rate of 1 to 2 inches per hour as the entire island remains under a flash flood warning. Officials have shuttered the Kuhio Highway at the Hanalei Bridge, while water levels along the South Fork of the Wailua River continue to rise rapidly. The National Weather Service in Honolulu confirmed that current rainfall patterns have kept stream gauges, including those at the Hanalei River, at elevated levels throughout the day.

Flash flooding remains an imminent danger for residents near streams, rivers, and low-lying areas. According to forecasters, the intense precipitation is expected to continue impacting the region. Authorities have explicitly warned hikers and campers to avoid all creeks and streams, noting that these areas can become hazardous during heavy downpours. In response to the weather, a community-led refuge area has been opened at Hale Halawai in Hanalei to assist those affected.

Separate flood concerns extend to Oahu, where a flood advisory remains active until 10 p.m. due to heavy rain over the Koolau mountains. While current conditions are localized to these islands, forecasters anticipate that a frontal system will move down the island chain from late Sunday through Monday. This change is expected to eventually bring breezy northeasterly trades and drier air across the region later in the week.

Residents are advised to monitor official updates through the Kauai Emergency Management Agency or the National Weather Service website. Officials emphasize that individuals should never attempt to cross fast-flowing or rising water by foot or in a vehicle. For current road conditions, residents may contact the dedicated information line at 808-241-1725.
