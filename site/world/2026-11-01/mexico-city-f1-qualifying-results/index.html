Hamilton Snaps Three-Year Drought to Clinch Ferrari Pole at Mexico City

By Karim Fouad · Published 01 Nov 2026, 06:01 UTC

Lewis Hamilton secured his first pole position for Ferrari at the Mexico City Grand Prix, edging out George Russell by 0.009 seconds.

Lewis Hamilton delivered a 105th career pole position at the Mexico City Grand Prix, stopping the clock just 0.009 seconds faster than George Russell to mark his first pole since July 2023. At 41 years old, the seven-time champion becomes the oldest driver to secure a pole position since Mario Andretti in 1982. The result marks his first time leading the grid since joining Ferrari, a feat he described as special given the team's history.

The qualifying session maintains significant implications for the world championship standings as Kimi Antonelli approaches Sunday's race. Antonelli currently sits in position to claim the title, needing a finish of ninth or higher to secure the championship. Following a one-place grid penalty issued to Max Verstappen for a practice collision, Antonelli will move up to start the race from fourth position.

Ferrari's Charles Leclerc and Red Bull's Max Verstappen finished third and fourth respectively, following errors on their final attempts in Q3. While Lewis Hamilton and George Russell successfully locked out the front row, the session proved difficult for other contenders. Oscar Piastri suffered a surprise exit in Q2, leaving the McLaren driver to start 13th, while Liam Lawson was disqualified from his eighth-place qualifying spot due to a technical breach regarding the MGU-K power requirement.

Lewis Hamilton will lead the field on Sunday as the grid prepares for the race, with Kimi Antonelli's potential title clinching performance serving as the weekend's primary focus.
