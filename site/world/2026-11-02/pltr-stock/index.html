Palantir Routs Earnings Estimates as US Commercial Revenue Surpasses Government Sales

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Nov 2026, 21:01 UTC · Updated 02 Nov 2026, 21:31 UTC

Palantir reported Q3 revenue of $2.313 billion and raised its full-year guidance after commercial sales topped government contracts for the first time.

For the first time in company history, Palantir’s US commercial business has generated more revenue than its government sales. The software firm delivered a significant beat on third-quarter expectations, posting revenue of $2.313 billion—a 96% increase compared to the previous year—and adjusted earnings per share of $0.44. These figures surpassed the Wall Street consensus estimates of $2.17 billion in revenue and $0.40 in earnings per share, according to Yahoo Finance data.

This performance signals a major shift in the company's reliance on its historically dominant US government base. As the market for enterprise AI continues to evolve, Palantir argues that its underlying infrastructure layer provides a distinct advantage over competitors who sell simple model tokens. This pivot toward the commercial sector is now a cornerstone of the company’s growth strategy, reinforcing its presence as a primary provider of data software.

Following these results, the company officially raised its full-year revenue outlook to a range between $8.530 billion and $8.534 billion, clearing analyst expectations of $8.2 billion. The firm also set a new target for US commercial sales at over $3.559 billion. Despite broader volatility in the tech sector earlier this year, analysts remain largely bullish on the company’s trajectory, currently maintaining a consensus price target of $216 per share for the stock.

Palantir’s previous work with the US military, specifically the adoption of its Maven Smart System as an official Pentagon program, helped establish the foundation for this multiyear expansion. The company expects this dual-track growth—balancing government commitments with an accelerating commercial portfolio—to drive its financial performance through the remainder of the fiscal year.
