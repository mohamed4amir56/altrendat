S&P 500 Climbs As Oil Prices Plunge Helping Markets Recover From Sell-off

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Nov 2026, 18:31 UTC · Updated 02 Nov 2026, 19:03 UTC

The S&P 500 rose Monday as U.S. stocks rebounded from a bruising sell-off following a sharp 7.5 per cent drop in oil prices.

The Brent crude oil benchmark fell 7.5 per cent to $104.92 per barrel on Monday, signaling a relief move for investors as global energy uncertainty persists. This price correction provided a much-needed lift for the U.S. stock market, which had struggled through its third consecutive losing week. The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 1 per cent by mid-day, while both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq composite posted gains as markets attempted to find stability after a month of intense volatility.

This recovery remains critical for investors navigating a landscape defined by high-stakes Big Tech earnings and concerns over artificial intelligence investments. Major tech names, including Alphabet and Nvidia, showed signs of stabilization after suffering steep losses late last week. Market sentiment was further bolstered by earnings reports from outside the tech sector, with companies like Marriott International and Idexx Laboratories rallying after posting better-than-expected profits for the summer period.

According to Yahoo Finance, markets were seeking to move past an AI-fueled sell-off that forced the Nasdaq Composite down 5.2 per cent during October. BNN Bloomberg notes that the S&P 500 is now positioned within 2.8 per cent of its all-time high. Despite the Monday gains, analysts remain focused on the potential impact of AI spending on long-term corporate profitability.

Looking ahead, the market enters a period of high activity with midterm elections approaching and a major government jobs report scheduled for release this Friday. Investors are also monitoring diplomatic developments regarding the war in the Middle East, which continues to drive fluctuations in global oil benchmarks.
