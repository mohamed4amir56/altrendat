Stock Futures Surge Following Massive $5 Trillion AI Market Selloff Wipeout

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Nov 2026, 06:03 UTC

U.S. stock futures opened higher Sunday evening as investors prepare for Apple earnings, midterm elections, and fluctuating oil prices.

Since October 6, the technology and chip sectors have suffered a staggering $5 trillion loss in market value, according to Yahoo Finance calculations. While the broader market demonstrated resilience—with 9 of the 11 S&P 500 sectors rising by at least 1% last week—this specific AI-driven downturn represents one of the most concentrated selloffs since 1999. Wall Street now pivots to a high-stakes week characterized by major corporate earnings and significant political developments.

This week is crucial for market sentiment as Apple, the world’s most valuable company, reports its earnings after the bell on Monday. Investors are closely monitoring iPhone 18 sales and the performance of Palantir, which also releases results Monday. With the S&P 500 having slipped 0.6% last week, the market is bracing for volatility triggered by the midterm elections on Tuesday and the release of the October jobs report on Friday.

Market participants are also weighing the implications of a volatile energy sector. West Texas Intermediate crude settled at $104 per barrel on Friday, its highest level since mid-September. Meanwhile, OPEC+ confirmed it will maintain its current output targets through December. Historical data offers a glimmer of optimism for traders; since 1950, the S&P 500 has risen every time in the period spanning from the end of a midterm October through the following June.

As the tech sector attempts to stabilize, analysts remain divided on whether the meltdown will deepen or if the broader market's recent climb indicates a turning point. Investors will closely scrutinize upcoming data to determine if the Federal Reserve will adjust its policy stance in response to the latest economic indicators.
