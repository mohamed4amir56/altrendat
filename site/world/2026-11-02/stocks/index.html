Stock Futures Surge Following $5 Trillion AI Rout Ahead of Monster Week

By Karim Fouad · Published 02 Nov 2026, 06:01 UTC

S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow futures opened higher Sunday as investors brace for Apple earnings, midterm elections, and $100 oil.

The $5 trillion market value wipeout in AI and chip stocks since October 6 marks a brutal close to the month, yet the broader market tells a different story. While tech suffered, the remaining 625 large U.S. stocks tracked by Yahoo Finance added approximately $1.3 trillion in value during the same period. This trend continued last week, as the S&P 500 slipped 0.6% even though 9 of its 11 sectors rose by 1% or more.

This discrepancy matters because it challenges the severity of the tech meltdown. According to Yahoo Finance, there has not been a down week for the index with such widespread sector gains since at least 1999. Investors are now watching to see if the market's historical "sweetest stretch" holds, as the S&P 500 has risen every time from the end of a midterm October through the following June since 1950.

Financial markets are entering a high-stakes week. Apple is set to report earnings Monday afternoon, remaining the world's most valuable company at $5.03 trillion, slightly ahead of Nvidia at $4.93 trillion. Meanwhile, energy markets remain volatile, with West Texas Intermediate crude settling at $104 a barrel on Friday. OPEC+ confirmed it is holding December output targets steady, and the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed in the ninth month of the Iran war.

Looking ahead, Tuesday features the midterm elections, while Friday brings the October jobs report. These events will force Wall Street to determine if the Federal Reserve is ready to intervene as market participants weigh conflicting signals between sinking tech giants and the rest of the climbing market.
