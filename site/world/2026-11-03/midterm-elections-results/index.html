Americans head to polls in referendum on Trump's second term agenda

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Nov 2026, 14:01 UTC

Voters across the United States are casting ballots today to determine which party controls Congress for the remainder of Donald Trump's presidency.

Record numbers of Americans have already cast early votes before polling stations opened nationwide this Tuesday, 3 November. These midterm elections serve as a direct referendum on the current administration's direction, with the political landscape shaped by an ongoing war alongside Israel against Iran that has fueled a global energy crisis and regional conflict. While the president does not appear on the ballot himself, the outcomes in key Senate and House races will dictate his ability to push his legislative agenda over the final two years of his term.

The results of today's voting will prove critical for the balance of power in Washington. Democrats are currently pushing for victories in historically Republican-leaning districts that have remained uncompetitive for decades. For voters, the stakes remain high as the election determines the trajectory of national policy in the face of widespread unpopularity regarding current administrative choices and the high costs associated with ongoing military engagements.

Polls began closing in the first states, Indiana and Kentucky, at 6 p.m. ET (3 p.m. PT), marking the beginning of the reporting process. According to the Associated Press, additional results will continue to roll in throughout the evening as more states finish voting. The final polls will close in Alaska and Hawaii at midnight ET (9 p.m. PT). Official tallies of elected members of Congress will be updated as races are called by the Associated Press, with data reflected in live tracking charts.
