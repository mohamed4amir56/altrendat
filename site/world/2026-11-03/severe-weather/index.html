Dangerous storms threaten Queensland and New South Wales with giant hail tornadoes

By Karim Fouad · Published 03 Nov 2026, 06:01 UTC · Updated 03 Nov 2026, 06:32 UTC

Severe thunderstorms carrying the risk of tornadoes and hail larger than golf balls are targeting parts of Queensland and northern New South Wales today.

Quick facts

Wind Speed
Up to 125km/h
Event Date
November 3, 2026
Primary Risks
Giant hail, tornadoes, destructive winds, flash flooding

Destructive winds reaching up to 125km/h are expected to accompany the storms as they develop through this afternoon and evening. The Bureau of Meteorology has placed multiple regions in the firing line for potentially volatile conditions, with residents in saturated areas of south-east Queensland facing an increased risk of flash flooding due to the forecasted heavy rainfall.

These warnings are critical for communities as the atmospheric conditions shift rapidly. Meteorologist Jonathon How indicated that the weather event poses significant dangers to the listed forecast districts, ranging from the Illawarra and Hunter regions down to the Northern Tablelands and beyond. For those in the path of the storm system, the threat of giant hail larger than golf balls and possible tornadoes highlights the severity of this weather pattern.

According to Nine.com.au, the areas under notice include the Mid North Coast, Central Tablelands, Southern Tablelands, and the North West Slopes and Plains. Queensland regions such as the Central Highlands and Coalfields, Maranoa and Warrego, and the Darling Downs and Granite Belt are also included in the warnings. Brisbane residents have already experienced muggy conditions with temperatures climbing into the high 20s.

The weather system is not expected to remain stationary. Meteorologists anticipate the storms will migrate north throughout Queensland and track across northern Australia tomorrow, continuing the period of instability. Authorities advise staying updated as the storms progress across these regions.
