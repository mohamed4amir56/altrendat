Semiconductor Rout Deepens as Nasdaq Plunges 3.8% Amid Fading Midterm Rally

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Nov 2026, 15:31 UTC

The S&P 500 fell 2.1% and the Nasdaq dropped 3.8% on Thursday as a sell-off in chip stocks countered the gains from the post-election surge.

The PHLX Semiconductor index suffered a heavy blow on Thursday, sinking over 9% as major players Nvidia and Micron saw shares plummet 7% and 12% respectively. This sharp decline in the technology sector pushed the Nasdaq down by 3.8% and the S&P 500 by 2.1%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average retreated 1% from its recent highs. This pullback arrived immediately following a historic rally, which had previously secured the Dow its best single-day performance since April 2025.

Investors are now evaluating the economic consequences of the Democratic midterm victory and the resulting shift in congressional power. The uncertainty surrounding the political landscape has replaced the initial relief rally, creating a more cautious environment across Wall Street. Market participants are particularly focused on potential volatility tied to upcoming debt limit negotiations and the ongoing impacts of the war in Iran.

Oil prices have displayed conflicting trends according to financial reports. While some benchmarks saw a recent settlement lower by 8%, Yahoo Finance reports that Brent crude traded near $97 per barrel and WTI crude reached $90 per barrel on Thursday. Concerns remain that the administration may struggle to resolve the conflict in the Middle East, a factor that both market analysts and political figures have cited as influential in the recent electoral outcome.

Looking ahead, markets are bracing for additional volatility as they await the release of Friday’s key employment report. Companies are currently shifting their focus toward employee retention, with layoff plans in October reaching their lowest level since 2023, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas data.
