Catastrophic Flash Flood Warning Forces McCaysville Shutdown as Waters Rise Across Georgia

By Karim Fouad · Published 05 Nov 2026, 11:31 UTC

The National Weather Service issued a catastrophic flash flood warning for north Georgia, triggering a state of emergency and bridge closures in McCaysville.

Fannin, Gilmer, Murray, Union
Emergency Status
City closed to unauthorized personnel

A catastrophic flash flood warning remains active for Fannin, Gilmer, Murray, and Union counties following heavy rainfall that submerged roads and businesses. McCaysville officials officially declared a state of emergency at 5:20 a.m. on Thursday as rising floodwaters forced the closure of multiple access points, including areas near Blue Ridge Drive and the Steel Bridge. Police Chief and Public Safety Director Michael O. Earley warned that downtown shops have already been impacted by the deluge, prompting evacuations and restricting city access to emergency personnel only.

Residents in the region are under strict orders to avoid driving or walking through any flooded areas. The closure of key transit bridges and roads presents a significant danger to the public, and authorities are prioritizing the safety of citizens and emergency responders as they work to evacuate families displaced from their homes. The situation remains fluid, with local officials emphasizing that these mandates are necessary to prevent further casualties during the ongoing severe weather event.

While parts of the region grapple with these emergency conditions, other areas expect a front to move through Thursday morning, bringing scattered showers. According to WDBJ7, this front will likely bring a final batch of rain to eastern areas before moving out on Friday. Residents are cautioned that environmental conditions can change rapidly and are advised to follow all local emergency directives until warnings are lifted.
