Bomb threat email triggers massive evacuations across Irish and European media outlets

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 14:31 UTC

RTÉ and other media organizations were evacuated this morning following a coordinated bomb threat sent via email.

Quick facts

Threat received
8.33am
Garda response
Searches conducted by sniffer dogs
Locations affected
Ireland, Britain, and across Europe

Director General Kevin Bakhurst received the threatening correspondence at 8.40am, just seven minutes after it was sent to RTÉ and multiple media organizations across Ireland and Britain. The security alert forced staff from the Donnybrook campus in Dublin to clear their offices, while other major outlets like Mediahaus and Independent News Media also vacated their Talbot Street headquarters for safety checks. Garda sniffer dogs were deployed to sweep the buildings, which have since been declared safe.

The widespread disruption rippled beyond Ireland as officials identified similar threats targeting European flights, including at least eight incoming and outgoing operations at Prague airport. For media workers, the event meant an immediate shift in workflows, with RTÉ switching to pre-recorded programming as a precaution. While some outlets like Virgin Media Television maintained their broadcast schedule after assessing the situation, the impact of the email was significant enough to trigger emergency protocols across the continent.

Kevin Bakhurst described the situation as a disgraceful attack on free media that caused considerable operational chaos. The broadcaster relied on recently reviewed contingency plans to manage the evacuation. Authorities in Ireland confirmed they are actively investigating the incident, which appears to be part of a larger, coordinated campaign of disruption affecting various European platforms. Gardaí remain in contact with affected organizations as they continue to monitor the broader security threat. The investigation into the source of the emails is ongoing, with officials liaising with security partners across Europe to trace the origins of these coordinated warnings.
