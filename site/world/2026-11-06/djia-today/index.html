Dow Jones tumbles 1,800 points as AI sell-off wipes out election gains

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 16:31 UTC

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 3.4% on Thursday as tech and chip stocks plummeted, erasing post-election market rallies.

More than 1,800 points vanished from the Dow Jones Industrial Average in a single Thursday session as the market retreat shifted into high gear. The selling pressure hit the technology sector hardest, with the Nasdaq-100 index shedding $2.3 trillion in total market value during the sharp decline.

This broad sell-off carries significant implications for investors who had bet on a sustained post-election rally. By shedding the year's top artificial intelligence performers, the market signaled a major repricing of large-cap tech stocks, which recorded their worst day of losses since the pandemic-era sell-off. The movement highlights how quickly investor sentiment can shift when concerns over sector valuations overtake the relief that followed the resolution of election uncertainties.

According to Yahoo Finance, the Dow Jones Industrial Average tumbled 3.4%, while the S&P 500 fell 4.3%. The Nasdaq Composite suffered a 5.5% drop, officially entering correction territory. Within the tech sector, the PHLX Semiconductor index plummeted 12%, driven by steep declines in companies such as Nvidia and Micron. While investors navigated the equity sell-off, commodity markets reacted to geopolitical developments; Brent crude oil prices reached $98 per barrel amid uncertainty regarding future foreign policy engagements in the Middle East.

Looking ahead, market participants are turning their focus toward upcoming labor data. A report from Challenger, Gray & Christmas indicated that October layoff plans hit their lowest level since 2023, providing a critical baseline for the government employment report due this Friday. Additionally, corporate earnings remain a focus, with Duke Energy, Airbnb, and Lumentum scheduled to release their latest results.
