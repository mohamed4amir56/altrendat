FTSE 100 claws back heavy losses after AI tech sector sell-off

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 18:01 UTC · Updated 06 Nov 2026, 18:32 UTC

The London blue-chip index fell 2.35% before closing 1% lower on Friday following a global market rout triggered by US political shifts.

The FTSE 100 faced a volatile session on Friday, at one point dropping by 254 points, or 2.35%, to 10,528 points. This sharp decline mirrored a broader global market sell-off initiated by a massive slump in US tech shares, which saw $3.2tn (£2.39tn) wiped from Wall Street values on Thursday. While the London index managed to recover some ground by the closing bell, finishing down 1%, the turbulence highlighted deep investor anxiety regarding the future of artificial intelligence investments.

This market instability follows the Democratic Party's decisive victory in the US midterm elections. Investors are now bracing for a potential legislative crackdown on data center development, a sharp policy pivot from the previous Republican-led focus on AI promotion. The uncertainty has directly impacted high-profile ventures, with reports suggesting that major investors are lowering their valuation expectations for the upcoming Anthropic stock-market float from $1.5tn to approximately $1tn.

Individual stocks in London felt the brunt of the shifting sentiment. BT emerged as the day's worst performer, sliding more than 6% on fears that its cost-saving initiatives could be compromised. Pharmaceutical giants like AstraZeneca and GSK also saw declines as optimism cooled regarding AI’s role in accelerating drug discovery. Meanwhile, the contagion spread globally, with Japan’s Nikkei 225 index suffering a 6% plunge and Taiwanese regulators taking the extraordinary step of restricting short-selling to prevent further collapses in the Taiex market.

Despite the sell-off, some analysts noted minor signs of stabilization as US tech stocks attempted a partial recovery on Friday afternoon. Market participants remain focused on the potential legislative agenda in Washington, where new rules targeting AI infrastructure are reportedly in preparation.
