S&P 500 futures edge up 0.3% following brutal $2.3 trillion market sell-off

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 10:54 UTC

S&P 500 futures attempted to stabilize on Friday as investors await the October jobs report following a 4.4% index decline on Thursday.

The VIX Volatility Index surged 70% to reach 28 after Wednesday's massive sell-off erased $2.3 trillion in total market value. This sharp downturn marked the worst single-day performance for the S&P 500 since Liberation Day in April 2025, with the benchmark index closing down 4.4%. Market tremors were triggered by an artificial intelligence trade meltdown, specifically affecting chip stocks including Nvidia, Micron, AMD, and Sandisk, due to mounting concerns regarding slowing data center construction and AI spending. While the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 fell into a correction on Thursday, futures for that index were up 1% on Friday morning. As the major indexes head toward substantial weekly losses, the Dow Jones Industrial Average futures fell 0.7%, reflecting ongoing volatility from a week that saw the Democrats retake both the House and the Senate. Energy traders are also monitoring the geopolitical climate, with both Brent crude and WTI crude futures falling below $100 per barrel amid assessments of potential reescalation in the Middle East. With markets attempting to find stability, all eyes are turned toward the Labor Department for the monthly jobs report due at 8:30 a.m. ET. Economists anticipate the release will show the U.S. added 65,000 jobs in October, providing a definitive update on the current health of the labor market. Market participants are also looking toward further potential catalysts as they track upcoming earnings reports from Moderna and Oklo to gauge the broader economic trajectory following the week's sharp decline.
