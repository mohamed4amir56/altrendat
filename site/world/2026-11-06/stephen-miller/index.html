Trump Promotes Stephen Miller to White House Chief of Staff After Midterms

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 19:31 UTC

President Trump announced on Friday that Stephen Miller will replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff starting this Monday.

Quick facts

Incoming Chief of Staff
Stephen Miller
Outgoing Chief of Staff
Susie Wiles
Start Date

The appointment of Stephen Miller follows a period of political turmoil, coming just three days after Democrats secured a landslide victory in the midterm elections. This shift in personnel marks a significant change in the president's inner circle as the administration adjusts to the loss of Republican majorities in both the House and Senate.

For the administration, this leadership change signifies a move toward a different strategic approach. Miller, known as an architect of mass-deportation efforts, takes the reins while the White House prepares for potential congressional investigations by Democrats. The decision to elevate him is viewed by many as a tightening of the president's circle following the election results.

According to CNBC, President Trump announced the transition on Truth Social, where he described Miller as one of his most trusted advisors. Miller, currently the White House deputy chief of staff, will officially take over the position on Monday. Susie Wiles, who served as the first woman in this top role, will transition to work within the Trump Political Operation. The New York Times reports that Wiles, who helped steer the president's 2024 election victory, leaves her post after playing a central role in the administration's policy decisions, including the appointments of Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

The administration remains under pressure as it faces the economic and political effects of the ongoing conflict with Iran. Officials are bracing for further turnover as the White House navigates the aftermath of the midterm wipeout and upcoming congressional scrutiny.
