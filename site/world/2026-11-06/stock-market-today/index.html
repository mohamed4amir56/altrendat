Wall Street Plunges as AI Sell-off Erases $3.2 Trillion in Market Value

By Karim Fouad · Published 06 Nov 2026, 17:01 UTC

The NASDAQ plummeted 6.3 per cent as investor confidence in AI infrastructure collapsed following the US midterms.

The massive sell-off triggered an unrelenting decline across all three major US indices, with automated trading software accelerating the rout. According to Tapas Strickland of Moomoo, the algorithms went into overdrive, selling into a market devoid of buyers and causing prices to gap down rapidly as the euphoria from the post-election rally vanished. This downturn marked the worst single-session drop for the S&P 500 since April 2025, when tariffs previously shook global markets.

For millions of Australians, this volatility carries significant implications as roughly 20 per cent of the $4.5 trillion invested in superannuation funds is exposed to Wall Street. The downturn was specifically driven by a fundamental shift in how investors view the AI infrastructure build-out. While investors initially hoped for a Democrat-controlled Congress to introduce rationality to the sector, voters' rejections of data centre developments in local ballots—such as those in Texas—have signaled a potential halt to the growth that previously underpinned the bull market.

While technology stocks faced the steepest declines, the selling was widespread. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 3.4 per cent, losing more than 1800 points. Meanwhile, in Australia, the S&P/ASX200 fell 44.3 points to 9020.8 as mining heavyweights like BHP and Rio Tinto tracked the US tech slump. Despite the Friday decline, Australian shares finished the week 1.2 per cent higher, marking a second consecutive week of gains despite the Reserve Bank raising the official cash rate to 4.85 per cent.

As the market digests these developments, uncertainty remains regarding whether this sell-off represents a temporary correction or the beginning of a broader bear market.
