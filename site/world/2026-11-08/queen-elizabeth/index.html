King Charles Leads Royal Family Commemorating Remembrance Sunday at London's Cenotaph

By Karim Fouad · Published 08 Nov 2026, 11:31 UTC

King Charles III and senior royals gathered at London's Royal Albert Hall and the Cenotaph to pay tribute to military veterans and service personnel.

Some 10,000 veterans prepared to march past in the Royal British Legion’s annual event, honoring those who served across more than 300 different armed forces and civilian organizations. The gathering at London's Royal Albert Hall featured performances by musicians like Alfie Boe and Mica Paris, while marking significant milestones including the 35th anniversary of the end of the First Gulf War and 15 years since the conclusion of British operations in Iraq. For the Princess of Wales, the occasion held a deeply personal tribute; she wore a three-poppy pin representing her great-grandmother’s brothers—Maurice, Lionel, and Francis—who died in action during World War I between 1915 and 1917. She also chose to wear Princess Diana’s Collingwood pearl earrings and the HMS Glasgow Brooch to the service at the Cenotaph. King Charles led the nation in two minutes of silence on Sunday to commemorate those who died in the two world wars and other conflicts. He was joined by senior royals and political leaders, including Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, to lay wreaths at the war memorial in central London. The Remembrance Sunday observances, held on the closest Sunday to Armistice Day, serve as a national opportunity to reflect on the selflessness of those who committed their lives to protecting others. Following these events, the Prince of Wales is scheduled to travel to India next week for the annual Earthshot Prize. Kensington Palace has not yet confirmed whether the Princess of Wales will accompany him on the trip, which would mark her second overseas journey since finishing her recent cancer treatment.
