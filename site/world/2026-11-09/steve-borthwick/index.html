England storms to record 45-22 victory over Australia in Nations Championship

By Karim Fouad · Published 09 Nov 2026, 14:01 UTC

Steve Borthwick's side delivered a clinical seven-try performance against Australia at Allianz Stadium to secure their highest-ever points tally against the Wallabies.

Quick facts

Final Score
England 45, Australia 22
Venue
Allianz Stadium
Tries Scored
7
Date
November 2026

England recorded a staggering 45-22 win over Australia on Sunday, marking their highest points tally ever against the Wallabies. The performance defied pre-match concerns regarding discipline and internal unity, with England missing only two tackles throughout the opening 40 minutes of the match. The team demonstrated significant growth, moving past earlier struggles by maintaining intensity, accuracy, and tactical cohesion across the full 80 minutes.

This victory is a crucial turning point for head coach Steve Borthwick, following recent speculation regarding player morale and a summer tour marked by reported unrest. By silencing critics through a dominant display, the team proved their commitment to the current project. The win provides a strong start to the autumn series and demonstrates that the squad has successfully addressed the defensive weaknesses that plagued their earlier campaign in the Six Nations.

According to BBC Sport, the team's ability to remain focused while ignoring outside criticism helped galvanize the group, resulting in an abundance of the discipline that was allegedly lacking over the previous nine months. The Telegraph notes that the defensive improvement reflects well on senior assistant coach Richard Wigglesworth and the players, who executed a game plan that left Australia struggling to respond despite their own dangerous individual talent.

While this performance is being hailed as a major step forward, observers highlight that it remains one game in a broader autumn series. England will look to carry this momentum into their remaining fixtures, having already demonstrated that their attacking cohesion and defensive work are on a clear upward trajectory.
