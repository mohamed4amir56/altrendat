Public Sector Strike Looms As Fifty Thousand Workers Prepare For Nationwide Rallies

By Karim Fouad · Published 10 Nov 2026, 23:31 UTC

Irish public sector unions are weighing a revised pay offer while maintaining strike plans for Thursday as negotiations continue at the Workplace Relations Commission.

While 50,000 workers prepare for nationwide rallies this Thursday, the Government and unions remain locked in high-stakes negotiations to avert a second all-out public sector strike in as many months. More than 20,000 people are expected to participate in the Dublin demonstration alone, according to the Irish Examiner. Union sources emphasize that plans for the industrial action remain in place, with Fórsa deputy general secretary Katie Morgan confirming that preparations must continue until an agreement is formally established.

Resolving the pay dispute is critical for the Government, as the planned stoppage coincides with the European Political Community summit in Dublin, an event expected to host approximately 50 European leaders. A senior government source acknowledged that while a narrow window remains to reach a deal, the situation is urgent, noting that failing to secure an agreement by Wednesday morning could make it too late to prevent the strike.

According to RTÉ, the current proposal under consideration by unions involves a 10.75% pay increase over two years and nine months, extending through March 2029. The Irish Examiner reports that an alternative revised offer includes a 10.5% increase, with lower-paid workers potentially receiving up to 17.8% in improvements, alongside an additional backdated payment for 2026 of €1,200 or 2%, whichever is greater.

Taoiseach Micheál Martin has stated he believes the basis for strike action no longer exists, citing a constructive atmosphere in current talks. Meanwhile, Fórsa General Secretary Kevin Callinan has expressed a commitment to reaching a deal, though he noted that success depends entirely on the speed and outcome of the ongoing mediation process at the Workplace Relations Commission.
