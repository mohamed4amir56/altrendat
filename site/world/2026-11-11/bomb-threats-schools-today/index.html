Over 7,000 Schools Across Europe Hit by Wave of Bomb Hoaxes

By Karim Fouad · Published 11 Nov 2026, 17:03 UTC

More than 6,300 Italian schools and over 700 across Scotland and Northern Ireland received bomb threats that authorities have labeled as not credible.

A single email addressed to over 6,300 Italian state schools was reported on Wednesday, creating a massive disruption that extended to hundreds of institutions in Scotland and Northern Ireland. Despite the scale of the electronic warnings, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni confirmed that checks have yielded no evidence of any concrete danger, describing the reports as a calculated strategy intended to cause widespread alarm.

For parents, students, and staff, the news brings significant concern, yet education officials and police remain firm in their assessment. The widespread nature of these emails, which have also previously targeted airports, supermarkets, and hospitals, underscores a disturbing trend of digital intimidation that threatens the stability of daily academic life across multiple nations.

According to the BBC, the threats in Italy originated from an email address already flagged as spam, while Scotland's education secretary Mairi McAllan stated that specialist cyber-crime officers are working to identify the source. Gardaí in Ireland reported that schools in Dublin, Cork, and Wicklow were targeted, advising staff to contact emergency services while noting that the threats are not credible. Police Scotland emphasized that there is no requirement to keep children away from school based on these messages. In Northern Ireland, the PSNI reported that 300 schools received the threat, and the Education Authority confirmed it is aware of the situation.

While authorities have largely advised that there is no need for evacuation, RTÉ News reported that several schools chose to evacuate pupils or perform precautionary searches anyway. Investigations into the origin of these messages remain ongoing as governments continue to monitor the situation and coordinate with educational authorities to ensure school security.
