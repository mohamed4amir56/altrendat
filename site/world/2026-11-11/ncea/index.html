System crash forces 40,000 students to abandon digital NCEA English exam

By Karim Fouad · Published 12 Nov 2026, 00:31 UTC

A major technical outage hit the NCEA Level 2 English exam across New Zealand this morning, forcing students to switch to paper or accept derived grades.

Quick facts

Affected exam
NCEA Level 2 English
Number of students
Up to 40,000
Outage start time
10am
Resolution time
10.35am

One student was just 20 seconds from completing a final essay when the NZQA digital platform shut down, forcing a sudden and panicked transition to paper assessments for thousands of teenagers. Parents reported children in states of distress after losing progress on critical work, with many students unaccustomed to handwriting their responses after preparing specifically for a digital testing environment. The disruption triggered immediate concern regarding the fairness of the assessment process, as these results serve as vital markers for university entrance and scholarship applications.

The system failure, which began around 10am, left schools scrambling to distribute physical papers as the platform struggled to transfer student responses to the marking system. Teachers reported that some students were unable to log in at all, while others faced the frustration of having their work deleted mid-session. NZQA confirmed that the technical platform issues were resolved by 10.35am, though the impact had already rippled across schools nationwide.

To mitigate the damage, officials confirmed that affected students were granted additional time to complete their assessments. For those unable to finish their work digitally or whose performance was compromised by the technical malfunction, the authority will provide derived grades at scale. Education Minister Erica Stanford labeled the event an isolated incident and confirmed that NZQA has identified the cause of the failure and is implementing measures to prevent a recurrence.

The Minister is scheduled to meet with NZQA representatives this afternoon to review the incident and ensure that no student is unfairly disadvantaged by the outage. While the Level 2 English exam suffered significant disruptions, NZQA stated that the Chemistry scholarship exam held earlier in the morning was unaffected by the technical errors.
