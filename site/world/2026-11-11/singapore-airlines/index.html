Singapore Airlines defends controversial business class seats after viral dental chair comparisons

By Karim Fouad · Published 11 Nov 2026, 20:01 UTC

Singapore Airlines addressed public criticism regarding its new business class cabin color palette following the November 6 unveiling of a $1.6 billion fleet revamp.

Quick facts

Investment value
$1.6 billion
Announcement date

Official response date
November 11

Critics and travel writers have drawn direct comparisons between the new grey-and-yellow business class seat design and dental chairs, sparking a wave of online discourse following the recent product reveal. The design, which originated from a collaboration between Singapore Airlines, seat manufacturer Recaro, and the design firm Designworks, features a specific palette that has faced scrutiny from the moment of its debut.

For passengers, the design aesthetic is just one component of a significant long-haul cabin overhaul that aims to modernize the flying experience. Beyond the visual choices, these seats incorporate full-grain premium leather upholstery, sliding privacy doors, and enhanced ergonomic features. According to the airline, these updates are intended to provide a more seamless, private, and functional environment for long-distance travel, supported by adjustable LED lighting to manage cabin ambiance throughout the journey.

Singapore Airlines stated on Wednesday that the colors, materials, and finishes were selected to establish a fresh and contemporary atmosphere. While acknowledging the mixed reactions, the carrier noted that frequent flyers who tested the seats during the launch event praised the spaciousness and the integration of larger display screens, improved charging ports, and a companion-bed configuration. The company emphasized that its priority remains the functional comfort of its passengers, including the utility of the full-flat bed and a larger footwell.

Moving forward, the airline intends to continue collecting data from operational insights and passenger feedback to refine future elements of the customer journey. This product update is part of a broader $1.6 billion investment that also includes the introduction of what the airline describes as the largest double bed available in the sky as part of its new first-class suite offerings.
