Springboks Regain World Number One Ranking After Wales Stuns All Blacks

By Karim Fouad · Published 14 Nov 2026, 17:31 UTC

South Africa climbed back to the top of the World Rugby rankings on Saturday following a 35-28 loss for New Zealand against Wales in Cardiff.

Wales secured their first victory over New Zealand since 1953, a result that proved pivotal in shifting the global hierarchy of international rugby. The 35-28 scoreline in Cardiff cost the All Blacks 2.00 ranking points, effectively ending their brief tenure at the summit and allowing South Africa to reclaim the top spot with 91.53 points. This shuffle occurred immediately after the Springboks suffered a 21-19 loss to France in Paris on Friday night, a result that had initially caused the South Africans to surrender their lead. The movement in the official rankings highlights the volatility of the standings, where a single match result can trigger a significant points swing. The 2.00-point deduction for the All Blacks highlights the high stakes of international competition, as they now sit in second place with 90.65 points. The ripple effect of these matches also allowed France to climb to third place with 89.05 points, pushing Ireland down to fourth. Data from SA Rugby magazine details the current state of the top 15 nations, with Wales rising from 10th to 9th position following their historic win. The rankings remain sensitive to point differentials; for instance, France gained 1.56 points from their win over the Springboks, while Italy dropped 0.47 points. Argentina also saw a positive shift, gaining 0.47 points, while Portugal faced a decline of 2.28 points in the latest update. The shifting landscape reflects the intense competition at the professional level, as teams continue to vie for positioning in the Nations Championship. While the Springboks now occupy the top position, the rankings demonstrate that the margin between the elite sides remains narrow as the international season progresses.
