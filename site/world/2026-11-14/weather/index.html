Orange Level 5 warning triggers flood alert across three South African provinces

By Karim Fouad · Published 14 Nov 2026, 05:01 UTC

The South African Weather Service issued an Orange Level 5 warning for severe thunderstorms, flooding, and damaging winds affecting multiple provinces this Saturday and Sunday.

Quick facts

Warning Level
Orange Level 5
Affected Provinces
7 provinces
Peak Probability
80%
Date
November 14-15, 2026

Rainfall probabilities are forecast to climb to 80% in parts of KwaZulu-Natal and Mpumalanga as a wave of unsettled weather hits the nation. Motorists face difficult navigation and increased travel times throughout the weekend as storms threaten to submerge roads and bridges.

The South African Weather Service (SAWS) cautions that the severe weather poses a significant threat to infrastructure. Heavy downpours are expected to disrupt communication networks and electricity supplies, while localized flooding could impact settlements and low-lying areas. This alert requires immediate vigilance from those living or traveling within the affected regions.

The Orange Level 5 warning specifically covers the far eastern parts of the Free State, western KwaZulu-Natal, and the Mpumalanga Highveld. Additionally, the SAWS has issued a Yellow Level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms across Gauteng, eastern North West, the eastern Free State, southern Limpopo, and north-eastern Mpumalanga. A separate Yellow Level 2 warning for disruptive rainfall applies to eastern KwaZulu-Natal, while the Western Cape faces a Yellow Level 1 warning for damaging winds along the south-west coast and the City of Cape Town district.

While the worst of the storms are forecast for Saturday, the unsettled conditions will persist into Sunday, November 15. The weather service expects widespread rainfall to continue across the eastern parts of the country throughout the day. In contrast, the western regions of the nation are set to experience hotter and generally drier conditions as the weekend progresses.
