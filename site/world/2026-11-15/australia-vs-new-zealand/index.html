Liam Martin storms home in 84 seconds as Australia leads World Cup

By Karim Fouad · Published 15 Nov 2026, 09:01 UTC

Australia established an early six-point lead against New Zealand in the 2026 Men's Rugby League World Cup final in Brisbane.

Quick facts

Event
2026 Men's Rugby League World Cup Final
Venue
Suncorp Stadium, Brisbane
Opening Score
6-0

Liam Martin surged through the opposition defense to score the opening try just 84 seconds after kick-off at Suncorp Stadium. Cameron Munster successfully converted the play to secure a six-point advantage for the holders, forcing New Zealand to chase the game from the opening moments. This lightning-fast start provides the Kangaroos with a significant psychological edge in their quest to retain the world title in Brisbane.

Following this early breakthrough, the final remains a high-stakes encounter under official NRL rules, featuring six-player interchange benches. The match carries immense weight for both nations, as the Kangaroos look to build on the momentum of their 2022 World Cup success. For spectators and fans, the intensity of these opening exchanges signals a battle between two highly disciplined squads, with New Zealand needing to overcome an early deficit to remain in contention for the trophy.

Team rosters underwent last-minute adjustments prior to the match. Australia made a tactical shift by moving Keaon Koloamatangi into the starting side and shifting Pat Carrigan to the bench, while James Tedesco joined the interchange following Latrell Mitchell’s withdrawal due to illness. The Australian lineup maintains continuity, with eight players returning from the 2022 final victory against Samoa. Meanwhile, New Zealand coach Stacey Jones confirmed that prop Naufahu Whyte and playmaker Kodi Nikorima were cleared to compete despite injury concerns earlier in the week. The Kiwis also welcomed back winger Ronaldo Mulitalo, who returned from a one-match suspension following his high shot against Fiji.

The tournament finale at Suncorp Stadium concludes a grueling schedule for both teams, with the match officials enforcing strict adherence to NRL protocols regarding player usage on the interchange bench.
