3.4 Magnitude Earthquake Shakes Northern Utah Residents in Weber and Davis Counties

By Karim Fouad · Published 15 Nov 2026, 14:01 UTC

A 3.4 magnitude earthquake struck near Huntsville on Saturday at 6:40 p.m., prompting hundreds of reports of light shaking.

More than 310 people reported feeling the tremors by 9:15 p.m., as the ground moved in Weber and Davis counties. While an earthquake of this magnitude often goes unnoticed by those nearby, residents across a wide area felt the impact of the late-evening event. The Utah Division of Emergency Management confirmed that individuals in Ogden, east Layton, Syracuse, and as far south as Fruit Heights reached out to share their experiences of the shaking.

Understanding the frequency of such seismic events helps local communities remain prepared for geological shifts in the region. The University of Utah Seismograph Stations monitors these occurrences to refine epicenter data and maintain accurate historical records, which are essential for long-term safety awareness in the Beehive State.

According to the University of Utah Seismograph Stations, the quake occurred at 6:40 p.m. on Saturday. The U.S. Geological Survey reported the depth at approximately 3.4 miles. While sources provide differing details regarding the exact location—citing spots 7 miles southeast or northwest of Huntsville—the data confirms the minor nature of the quake. Records show that since 1981, scientists have tracked 73 earthquakes of magnitude 2 or greater within 15 miles of this epicenter. The largest recorded in that timeframe was a magnitude 3.7 event on Aug. 30, 2023.

The Utah Division of Emergency Management continues to monitor reports, though no further alerts or immediate follow-up actions have been issued following the conclusion of the seismic activity. Residents are encouraged to stay updated through official seismological channels.
