Wales Ends 73-Year Drought With 35-28 Victory Over New Zealand

By Karim Fouad · Published 15 Nov 2026, 14:31 UTC

Wales defeated New Zealand for the first time in 73 years during a match in Cardiff on 14 November 2026.

Quick facts

Match Date
14 November 2026
Final Score
Wales 35-28 New Zealand
Venue
Principality Stadium, Cardiff
Previous Drought
73 years

The 14 November 2026 result at the Principality Stadium broke a 73-year wait for a win against the All Blacks, marking the first time Wales has bested the side since 19 December 1953. Prior to this, Wales had endured 34 consecutive defeats against New Zealand, and the team had recently suffered through a record-breaking streak of 18 straight Test losses over 16 months, leaving them ranked 14th in the world before this match. Captain Dewi Lake and player-of-the-match James Botham led the effort, with Wales centre Eddie James describing the result as the best day of his life.

This victory offers a potential path to stability for a national team that has struggled with financial disputes and empty stadiums since the 2023 World Cup. Coach Steve Tandy noted that while he urges caution regarding the team's long-term trajectory, the group is showing increased confidence and growth. Many of the players involved, including Lake, Jac Morgan, and Tomos Williams, have been gaining experience playing in high-level club competitions, contributing to a more mature and cohesive unit.

According to the BBC, the match saw Wales enter as the 10th-ranked team against a top-ranked New Zealand side. The Guardian reports that this performance represents a shift for a team that had previously struggled to score against top-tier opponents, including a heavy 73-0 loss to South Africa in the same stadium a year prior. Steve Tandy emphasized that the team's development over the last 12 months, specifically providing younger players the freedom to play, has been a key factor in this transformation.
