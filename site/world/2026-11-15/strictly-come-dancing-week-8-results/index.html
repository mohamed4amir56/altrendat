Cach and Maddie Charge Ahead With Series-Best Charleston During Week 8 Strictly

By Karim Fouad · Published 15 Nov 2026, 07:31 UTC

Cach and Maddie earned praise for the dance of the series while Dani and Nikita faced judge criticism over illegal lifts.

Week 8 competition event
Strictly Come Dancing

Cach and Maddie secured their comeback status as Motsi and Shirley rose to their feet to applaud a high-energy Charleston that Motsi hailed as the dance of the series. The pair, who survived the dance-off the previous week, moved with enough momentum that they appeared capable of dancing at Duracell levels of energy by the time the routine concluded, according to The Telegraph.

This week’s performances carry heavy weight as the competition approaches Blackpool. For the couples, these scores are crucial for maintaining a path to the final. However, the week was marked by significant disruption: Delta Goodrem, who holds an average score of 32/40, was forced to perform with substitute professional Aljaž Škorjanec after her partner Kai Widdrington fell ill. Goodrem, who has been competing with a fractured rib, successfully performed an Argentine tango under these changing circumstances.

Controversy also emerged regarding choreography and rules. Dani and Nikita performed an electric paso doble, but judge Craig Revel Horwood penalized the pair for performing four illegal lifts, pointedly remarking that the couple was taking liberties with the competition rules. Meanwhile, Vito Coppola faced scrutiny from the judges, including Anton Du Beke and Craig, who argued that his choreography for his partner lacked sufficient Foxtrot content, suggesting it would have been better suited for an American Smooth or a freestyle performance. Vito defended his routine, insisting that the dance contained the required amount of Foxtrot elements.

As the contestants look toward the next round, the pressure remains high following these varied performances. The dancers are now focusing on their upcoming routines as they aim to solidify their standing before the next live show.
