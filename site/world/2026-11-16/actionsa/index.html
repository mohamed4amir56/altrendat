Herman Mashaba Rejects Bullying Claims as Johannesburg Coalition Negotiations Heat Up

By Karim Fouad · Published 16 Nov 2026, 08:01 UTC

ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba asserts his party’s independence amid ongoing coalition talks as Johannesburg’s electrical grid faces critical failures.

More than 20,000 residents are currently reeling from serial substation fires that have left significant portions of Johannesburg without power. As City Power’s infrastructure continues to buckle under the strain of 574 reported outages in October alone, the city’s political leadership is locked in intense coalition negotiations expected to conclude within a two-week window.

This climate of instability has prompted ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba to issue a stern rebuke to critics who characterize his organization as a mere extension of other political parties. Mashaba declared that he will no longer tolerate attempts to dictate his leadership or coalition strategy, emphasizing that his party answers only to its principles and the citizens who voted for them. He underscored that ActionSA operates independently of the Democratic Alliance, the ANC, or the EFF, and will not be bullied into compromising its autonomy for the sake of political maneuvering.

The urgency of these negotiations is compounded by the severe state of the city's power infrastructure. Recent fires at the Mayfair/Brixton substation destroyed seven critical cables and a transformer, forcing major facilities like the Garden City Clinic and the SABC to rely on alternative energy sources. While political leaders bargain over governance, the Joburg Crisis Alliance, representing 57 residents' associations, has demanded that the incoming administration prioritize the electorate's choices over the trading of positions for power.

As the city navigates this crisis, the Joburg Crisis Alliance insists that any coalition agreement must reflect proportional voter support rather than granting minor parties undue influence. With the city's grid described as being in a death spiral, the outcome of these talks will dictate the administration's ability to address the frequent blackouts currently plaguing neighborhoods like Alexandra.
