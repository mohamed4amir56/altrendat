United Airlines resolves technical meltdown after 100-minute system outage delays flights

By Karim Fouad · Published 17 Nov 2026, 07:02 UTC

A software issue affecting weight and balance calculations forced United Airlines to delay departing flights for one hour and 40 minutes on Monday.

Quick facts

Duration of outage
1 hour and 40 minutes
Affected flights
Approximately 600 delayed
System impact
Aircraft weight and balance calculations

Pilots reported the system failure to air traffic controllers by requesting permission to remain at airport gates while software issues prevented them from finalizing critical pre-flight weight and balance data. A recording from the ATC app captured one pilot in Houston choosing to wait until the connectivity was restored rather than attempting a departure without the required performance software calculations. The outage lasted for one hour and 40 minutes before technical teams resolved the error, according to statements from the airline. Flights already in the air and those operating under the United Express regional network remained unaffected throughout the event.

For passengers, the incident meant widespread travel disruptions, with FlightAware data reporting approximately 600 delayed United flights by Monday evening. The ability for pilots to calculate a plane's final weight—accounting for passengers, cargo, baggage, and fuel against runway and weather conditions—is a mandatory safety requirement before every departure. Because these calculations are tied to internal performance software, the inability to access that data effectively grounded departures until the system was back online.

United confirmed that while the issue caused significant delays, there is no evidence that the event was related to cybersecurity threats. The airline has not specified if the glitch was linked to the Unimatic system, which caused a similar outage in August 2025 that impacted more than 1,000 flights. While the primary issue was resolved on Monday, the airline has not provided further details regarding the specific software failure. The disruption followed a separate nationwide ground stop caused by a Federal Aviation Administration network malfunction just days earlier, as well as unrelated technical issues at several major U.S. banks reported the same day.
