England Surges to 2/113 as Australia Battles Injury Setbacks in Series Decider

By Karim Fouad · Published 18 Nov 2026, 05:01 UTC

England elected to bat first in the ODI series decider at Bellerive Oval today after leveling the series with a 135-run win in Adelaide.

Quick facts

Venue
Bellerive Oval, Hobart
Match Type
ODI Series Decider
Start Time
2.30pm AEDT
England Score
2/113 after 15 overs

England reached 2/113 after 15 overs in the series decider at Hobart’s Bellerive Oval, with batsman Ben Duckett anchoring the innings at the half-century mark. Skipper Harry Brook chose to bat first on a pitch he deemed favorable, keeping the same squad that secured a decisive 135-run victory in Adelaide to force this final showdown. Former captain Michael Vaughan labeled England the favorites for the match, citing the absence of Australia’s top seam bowlers as a critical advantage for the visitors.

This series decider carries high stakes for both nations, effectively acting as a final to determine the overall winner. For Australia, the match represents a test of depth as they navigate a challenging injury list and look to contain a confident English lineup that has momentum from their previous performance. The outcome hinges on whether Australia's adjusted bowling attack can disrupt England’s momentum in this pressure-filled encounter.

Australia’s team selection has been forced into flux due to late medical withdrawals. Leg-spinner Adam Zampa was ruled out just before the match due to back spasms, leading to the return of young quick Mahli Beardman. Alex Carey also joins the side for the Hobart clash. Beardman enters the match following a breakout debut performance where he took 6-24, a feat that drew comparisons to Pat Cummins and followed private mentorship sessions with fast-bowling legend Dennis Lillee.

While Vaughan remains confident in England’s position, Australian cricket figure Mark Waugh noted the inconsistency displayed by both sides throughout the series, leaving the ultimate result unpredictable. England will look to capitalize on their early surge to set a challenging target, while Australia relies on its new-look attack to keep the tourists in check.
