NHS staff caught snooping on patient medical records over 3,000 times

By Karim Fouad · Published 18 Nov 2026, 08:31 UTC

Health workers in England accessed sensitive patient information without authorization in 3,029 incidents recorded between 2021 and 2026.

Quick facts

3,029
Reporting period
2021-22 to 2025-26
Reporting deadline
72 hours

Records of a three-year-old boy, victims of the 2024 Southport attack, and victims of the 2023 Nottingham attack were among the files inappropriately accessed by hospital personnel. These instances of unauthorized snooping, documented in a five-year period, reflect a recurring issue where staff inspect information belonging to people they know or satisfy personal curiosity.

Data breaches of this nature damage the relationship between the health service and the public. Patient advocates emphasize that these actions are distressing for families and fundamentally erode the trust required when individuals provide the NHS with their most sensitive personal information. A reported lack of transparency from some organizations following these discoveries has further compounded the negative impact on patients.

According to the Press Association, 3,029 incidents occurred across England between 2021-22 and 2025-26. This figure is likely an underestimate of the true scale, as only 79 of 143 acute hospital and ambulance trusts provided precise data, while others failed to report exact numbers. The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) has identified over 300 reports this year alone regarding staff looking at records without a legitimate reason or sharing information with third parties.

Regulators are now demanding immediate improvements to data security. Paul Arnold, chief executive of the ICO, has written to health leaders across the UK to call for a definitive end to these breaches. Organizations are expected to implement more rigorous access controls, enhance monitoring systems, increase staff training, and ensure consistent disciplinary actions are taken when violations occur. Trusts are also reminded of their legal obligation to report any breaches that pose a risk to patient rights to the ICO within 72 hours.
