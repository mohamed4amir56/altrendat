Toyota Unveils Corolla Deck Pickup Truck Set for 2027 Production Launch

By Karim Fouad · Published 19 Nov 2026, 10:02 UTC

Toyota officially revealed the Corolla Deck, a new Corolla Cross-based unibody pickup truck slated for production in Brazil by early 2027.

Quick facts

Production start
First half of 2027
Production location
Sorocaba, São Paulo, Brazil
Powertrain
1.8-litre flex-fuel hybrid (HEV)
Target markets
Latin America and Caribbean

The Corolla Deck features a rear bed designed for cargo and a redesigned chassis, replacing the standard torsion beam with an independent multi-link rear suspension. Confirming its utilitarian capabilities, the tailgate prominently displays an HEV badge, signaling the inclusion of a flex-fuel hybrid powertrain that utilizes a 1.8-litre engine paired with an electric motor and a 1.3 kWh battery. According to Carmag South Africa, this system produces a combined 90 kW of power, delivered to the front wheels via an e-CVT, while AutoIndustriya notes the setup mirrors the 122 PS output of the existing Corolla Cross.

This new model represents a strategic expansion of the Corolla family, moving the nameplate into the compact double-cab bakkie segment. Positioned below the rugged, body-on-frame Hilux, the unibody Corolla Deck is designed to compete against upcoming rivals like the Volkswagen Tukan, Renault Niagara, and BYD Mako. For prospective buyers, the unibody architecture suggests a vehicle focused on versatile load-carrying rather than the heavy-duty towing typical of traditional ladder-frame pickups.

Toyota revealed the pickup during the opening of its new manufacturing facility in Sorocaba, São Paulo. The automaker confirmed that the production line at this site will start operations in the first half of 2027. While reports from Quatro Rodas suggest future iterations could incorporate more powerful 2.5-litre hybrid or plug-in hybrid powertrains with all-wheel drive, these specifications are not yet finalized.

Currently, the Corolla Deck is configured exclusively as a left-hand-drive vehicle. Both Carmag South Africa and AutoIndustriya report that distribution will be limited to Latin American and Caribbean markets, making a release in regions like South Africa or other right-hand-drive territories unlikely for the foreseeable future.
