Panic Buying Surges at Costco as Tech Outages Fuel Emergency Prep

By Karim Fouad · Published 20 Nov 2026, 17:31 UTC

Costco U.S. comparable sales climbed 11% to 12% in late October as shoppers rushed to purchase emergency supplies following widespread technology failures.

As technology outages hit sectors from banking to aviation, shoppers are emptying shelves of high-ticket items and emergency goods. In the final week of October, Costco saw U.S. comparable sales jump to between 11% and 12%, significantly higher than the 6% to 7% growth recorded during the first three weeks of the month. By Sunday night, 21 out of 26 high-ticket inventory items tracked on the retailer’s website were completely sold out, according to research from Mizuho.

This trend toward so-called "doomsday prepping" has moved from fringe behavior into the mainstream. Analyst David Bellinger of Mizuho noted that consumer anxiety, stoked by Microsoft Outlook outages, federal aviation ground stops, and viral social media posts, is directly driving these sales. While officials insist there is no cause for alarm, the fear of potential supply disruptions is pushing shoppers to stock up on staples like generators, canned goods, and water purification supplies.

Data from Circana reflects this behavioral shift, with sales of water germicidal tablets skyrocketing by 154% for the four weeks ending November 1 compared to the previous year. Similarly, disinfecting wipe sales rose by 72%. While retailers like Walmart are also reporting increased demand for ready-to-eat foods, analysts emphasize that these patterns are currently occurring on the margins and have not yet significantly impacted broader corporate financial results.

Looking ahead, Mizuho has raised its price target for Costco shares from $1,100 to $1,150, projecting that U.S. comparable sales growth could exceed 10% through November. The market will receive a clearer picture of whether this surge translates into long-term membership growth when Costco reports its first-quarter fiscal 2027 results on December 10.
