Magnitude 5.0 earthquake strikes near Soledad rattling California's Central Coast

By Karim Fouad · Published 20 Nov 2026, 07:01 UTC · Updated 20 Nov 2026, 07:31 UTC

A magnitude 5.0 earthquake hit 13 miles northeast of Soledad late Thursday night.

Quick facts

Magnitude
5.0
Time
10:49 p.m. Thursday
Epicenter
13 miles northeast of Soledad
Depth
4.2 miles

Residents as far north as Stockton reported feeling the rumbling after the earthquake struck at 10:49 p.m. Thursday. The U.S. Geological Survey initially reported the temblor as a magnitude 5.4 before revising the measurement down to 5.0. It occurred at a depth of approximately 4.2 miles just north of Pinnacles National Park.

This event follows a series of smaller seismic activities in the same area throughout the evening. Authorities currently report no damage or injuries. The tremors serving as a reminder of the region's active seismic nature prompted concern across Monterey County.

According to the U.S. Geological Survey, the shaking was preceded by a magnitude 3.1 quake at 9:53 p.m. and a magnitude 3.3 quake at 10:12 p.m. A subsequent magnitude 3.0 earthquake was also recorded in the same vicinity at 10:52 p.m. Following the initial shake, emergency services and officials continue to monitor the area for any reports of impact.

The U.S. Geological Survey maintains logs of all seismic activity in the region. Residents are advised to stay informed through official updates as geological teams analyze the series of quakes. While there were no immediate reports of structural failures or harm to the public, the frequency of the events on Thursday evening signifies a notable period of seismic movement near San Benito County.
