S&P 500 and Dow Jones plunge into official 10% market correction

By Karim Fouad · Published 21 Nov 2026, 16:01 UTC

U.S. indices hit correction territory on Friday as a tech-driven sell-off wiped out Nasdaq gains for 2026.

Nvidia shares plummeted 14% on Friday alone, bringing the chipmaker's total loss for the month of November to nearly 40%. The selling pressure intensified following reports of a Securities and Exchange Commission probe into the company’s accounting, while the recent resignation of CEO Jensen Huang amid an affair with an alleged Chinese spy further rattled investor confidence. This decline marks a significant shift for a stock that until recently served as the primary engine for the artificial intelligence bull market.

The broader market correction, defined as a decline of at least 10% from record highs, reflects deepening anxiety regarding the sustainability of the AI trade. Analysts compare the current wave of fear to the volatility observed during the Covid-19 pandemic. The Nasdaq index alone suffered its worst performance in a single week since March 2020, dropping 10% as investors questioned the high valuations and massive capital expenditures of major AI-focused companies.

Adding to the market turbulence was the high-profile IPO of Anthropic. After debuting Thursday at $83 and surging above $115, the stock immediately reversed course and closed Friday at $88.91. This sharp swing reinforced investor fears that recent large-scale public offerings represent a collective cash-out by early players in the AI complex. While tech stocks led the downturn, the sell-off extended to broader indices, with the S&P 500 falling 3.7% and the Dow Jones industrial average dropping 1.93% during Friday’s trading session.

The pressure was not limited to U.S. exchanges, as the S&P/TSX composite index in Canada also slipped into correction territory after a 2.47% single-day decline. With the Nasdaq having erased all of its gains accumulated throughout 2026, market participants are now bracing for continued volatility as the reality of the AI-led rally faces its most severe test to date.
