England Clinches Record-Breaking 37-12 Victory Over All Blacks At Twickenham

By Karim Fouad · Published 22 Nov 2026, 08:31 UTC

England dominated New Zealand in a 37-12 Nations Championship win at Twickenham on Saturday, securing a record victory margin over the All Blacks.

England produced a commanding performance at Twickenham, delivering a record victory margin against New Zealand. The hosts established control early, building a 19-0 lead in the first half through tries from Henry Slade, Jack van Poortvliet, and Ben Earl. Although Ethan de Groot scored for the All Blacks just before the break, England extended their advantage after the restart with a try from Ollie Chessum. Despite Beauden Barrett marking his 150th cap with a try for New Zealand, Seb Atkinson effectively sealed the 37-12 win shortly after the hour mark.

This outcome is significant for England, as it places them at the top of the Northern Hemisphere Nations Championship table with 25 points, providing them a chance to reach next weekend's final. For supporters, the victory offers a promising shift in tactics; the team demonstrated a more fluid approach, favoring passing over the kicking game that defined their previous win over New Zealand twelve months ago.

According to BBC reports, the match featured a completely different starting backline compared to the previous year, highlighting a shift in personnel under Steve Borthwick. Statistical analysis shows a dramatic change in playstyle: England recorded 176 passes and 29 kicks, a stark contrast to the 104 passes and 35 kicks seen in the 2025 match. This more expansive strategy was supported by key contributors like Fin Smith, Henry Slade, and George Furbank, with Seb Atkinson playing a pivotal role in the midfield.

The victory marks a notable development in England's recent trajectory, which has historically been characterized by cycles of wins and losses. With this result, the team moves forward with the goal of securing a spot in the upcoming Nations Championship final.
