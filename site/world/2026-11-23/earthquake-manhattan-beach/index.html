3.3-Magnitude Earthquake Strikes Off Manhattan Beach Coast Near Los Angeles

By Karim Fouad · Published 23 Nov 2026, 06:01 UTC · Updated 23 Nov 2026, 06:32 UTC

A 3.3-magnitude earthquake hit the ocean off L.A. County’s South Bay at 9:17 p.m. Sunday with no damage expected.

The shaking traveled as far as Thousand Oaks to the northwest and Long Beach to the southeast, marking the fourth earthquake to hit the region in just four days. According to the U.S. Geological Survey, the tremor registered a 3 on the Modified Mercalli Intensity Scale, described as weak but noticeably felt by those indoors. Residents in Venice reported a short but significant single jolt, while those in Sawtelle experienced enough vibration to rattle light fixtures. The quake’s epicenter was situated approximately two miles from El Segundo and three miles from Manhattan Beach at a depth of 6.2 miles. This seismic event follows a series of recent activity in the area, including a magnitude 3.9 earthquake on Friday and a magnitude 3.3 tremor near San Pedro on Saturday morning. Experts at the USGS have categorized the intensity as weak, noting that no damage is expected from this particular event. While Saturday's activity occurred near the Palos Verdes fault zone, which stretches 70 miles along the coast, the current incident remains under observation. The region has seen multiple seismic movements recently, such as a magnitude 5 quake recorded Thursday night near the San Andreas fault in San Benito County. Updates will continue as further data becomes available regarding the stability of the local fault lines and any subsequent seismic reports.
