Tape found in bin sparked suspicion during 2018 sandpaper ball tampering scandal

By Karim Fouad · Published 24 Nov 2026, 20:01 UTC

Evidence of tape containing super glue and grit led to suspicions of ball tampering by David Warner during the 2018 Cape Town series.

Quick facts

Event
2018 Cricket Australia sandpaper scandal
Key Reporter
Gerard Whateley

An ICC representative attempted to test discarded finger and thumb strapping on David Warner’s digits to see if it fit, in a scene described as worthy of the OJ trial. According to reports from SEN’s Gerard Whateley, the strapping was recovered from the field by grounds staff and handed to officials, with an ICC source later claiming it contained a mixture of super glue and grit. Warner was the primary focus of South African networks at the time, as Cameron Bancroft later noted in a 4,497-word column that Warner was the brains behind the tampering plot but felt he could not personally carry it out due to camera scrutiny.

The revelations highlight the intense friction within the Australian squad as the scandal unfolded. At the team hotel, a heated confrontation occurred between Warner and Mitch Marsh. Sources described the scene as one where Cameron Bancroft attempted to take responsibility, Steve Smith sought to mediate, and Warner remained largely detached while walking the corridors. The incident was part of a broader atmosphere of denial and finger-pointing that divided Cricket Australia’s players and staff.

Even after the events in Cape Town, the habit of ball manipulation proved difficult to abandon. Gerard Whateley revealed that during the 2018 series against Pakistan, a player asked, “Who’s got the mints?” while searching for ways to take wickets on a flat Dubai pitch. The comment was met with a sharp rebuke from a teammate who insisted the squad would not repeat their previous mistakes. Captain Tim Paine and coach Justin Langer ultimately intervened to stop a substitute fielder from bringing items onto the field, maintaining a strict team policy against having lollies on the pitch to avoid any negative perception.
