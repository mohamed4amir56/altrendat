Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro reunite for Focker In-Law cinema release

By Karim Fouad · Published 25 Nov 2026, 06:31 UTC

Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro return for the fourth installment of the Meet the Parents franchise, opening in theaters this Wednesday.

Quick facts

Release date
Wednesday, November 25, 2026
Key stars
Ben Stiller, Robert De Niro, Ariana Grande, Owen Wilson
Director
John Hamburg

Sixteen years after their last collaboration in the franchise, Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro are returning to the big screen for the third sequel to the 2000 film Meet the Parents. The new project, Focker In-Law, brings the original stars back alongside a fresh ensemble cast featuring Ariana Grande, Skyler Gisondo, Beanie Feldstein, and Eduardo Franco. Ariana Grande joins the fold as Olivia, a former FBI agent whose introduction to the family includes an embarrassing meeting with Stiller’s character on a plane, while the "little Fockers" from previous films now appear as adults.

For audiences who have followed the series since its inception over a quarter-century ago, the film serves as a return to a well-known comedic world. Critics remain divided on the outcome; while some see the film as a welcome franchise course correction that mirrors the tone of the original, others describe the experience as a repetitive rehash of familiar premises. The production is characterized by some reviewers as a final example of 90s-style grossout cringe comedy, featuring a version of Stiller who is now the same age as his onscreen father-in-law was during their first meeting.

Writing for The Guardian, reviewers noted that while Ariana Grande shows clear signs of mastering the material, she is limited by the script. Ben Stiller, portraying a lean and glassy-eyed version of his character, navigates the tension of a family gathering scheduled for Jack Byrnes’s 80th birthday. The movie was written and directed by John Hamburg, who previously worked on the franchise and Zoolander. The film officially hits theaters this Wednesday, marking the latest iteration of what industry observers call one of Hollywood’s most consistently lucrative, if moderate, comedy franchises.
