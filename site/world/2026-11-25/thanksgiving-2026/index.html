Powerful East Coast Storm Slams Northeast With Heavy Rain and 50mph Gusts

By Karim Fouad · Published 25 Nov 2026, 07:01 UTC

A potent storm system moves up the East Coast, bringing flooding risks and travel delays from Florida through New England on Thanksgiving and Black Friday.

Quick facts

Max wind gusts
50 mph
New England arrival time
3:00 p.m. - 4:00 p.m. Thanksgiving Day
Projected rainfall in Southern New England
Over one inch
Primary travel impacts
I-95, I-75, I-26 and I-20

The incoming weather system carries the potential to become one of the most severe Thanksgiving Day storms for the I-95 corridor, combining heavy rainfall with damaging winds. Forecasters at the FOX Forecast Center warn that the storm is expected to strengthen off the Southeast coast by Wednesday afternoon before hitting the Northeast on the holiday itself. While many regions will remain dry during morning festivities like football games and turkey trots, the situation shifts drastically by the afternoon.

For travelers and holiday shoppers, the storm poses significant hazards to both road and air transit. Coastal flooding and power outages are major concerns as the system tracks north. Onshore wind gusts are expected to reach up to 50 mph, which could exacerbate existing tidal flooding and make navigation hazardous. The precipitation will be widespread, with Southern New England projected to see over an inch of rain by Friday morning.

According to the FOX Forecast Center, the impact begins in Florida, where a Flood Watch remains in effect from Titusville to Miami through Wednesday. Heavy rain will spread into Georgia and the Carolinas, with a Level 2 out of 4 flash flood risk stretching from Charleston, South Carolina, to North Carolina's Outer Banks.

By Thursday, the storm moves toward New England. The Boston Globe reports that the first raindrops should arrive in Southern New England between 3 p.m. and 4 p.m. on Thanksgiving, with heavier downpours intensifying after 8 p.m. The rain is expected to persist through Friday morning, creating wet conditions for early Black Friday shoppers, before tapering off by the early afternoon as the system pulls away from the region.
