Storms and flooding set to drench Australia during wild weekend weather

By Karim Fouad · Published 27 Nov 2026, 11:31 UTC

Severe weather warnings for heavy rain and flash flooding impact Tasmania as storms hit the Australian east and south-east coast this weekend.

Quick facts

Expected rainfall
40 to 70mm
Potential risk
Flash flooding
Affected areas
Northern Tasmania and east/south-east coast

Six-hourly rainfall totals are predicted to reach between 40mm and 70mm, bringing a significant risk of flash flooding to parts of northern Tasmania by early Saturday morning. This drenching rain is expected to push into the state from the north throughout Friday evening, spreading across the entire region overnight. The Bureau of Meteorology has confirmed that the weather system, fed by tropical moisture, creates an evolving risk for residents. Flood watches are currently in place for several key waterways, including the Forth, Mersey, Meander, North Esk, and Tamar rivers, as well as various north coastal rivers where sudden rises are deemed likely. The weekend forecast across the broader Australian landscape presents a sharp contrast in extremes. While parts of the east coast brace for these intense storms and potential floods, other regions are battling heatwaves and dangerous bushfires. On Flinders Island, an out-of-control fire has already destroyed or damaged at least eight buildings. Tasmania fire service officials noted that while some rain is moving toward the island late Friday, the heaviest falls are expected to bypass the fire-ravaged areas, providing little relief for those contending with the blaze. Authorities remain on high alert as the combination of wind, rain, and fire creates a volatile situation for the weekend. Residents are urged to monitor local warnings and official reports from the Bureau of Meteorology closely as the weather patterns shift across the country throughout the coming days.
