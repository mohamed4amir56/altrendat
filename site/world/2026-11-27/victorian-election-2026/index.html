Labor’s 12-year Victorian reign nears end as polls close on Saturday

By Karim Fouad · Published 27 Nov 2026, 18:31 UTC

Victorian voters head to the polls on Saturday, November 28, to decide the state government as Labor's long-term leadership faces an expected defeat.

Quick facts

Election Day
Saturday, November 28, 2026
Poll closing time
6pm
Sewerage lease proposal value
$9.2 billion

Opposition Leader Jess Wilson is facing fierce opposition from One Nation’s Warren Pickering over a proposed $9.2 billion lease of Melbourne Water’s sewerage treatment plants, pipes, and pumping stations. This 90-year lease proposal represents a major point of contention heading into the final hours of the election campaign, with One Nation pledging to vote against the legislation required to enact such a deal. One Nation leader Pauline Hanson has been actively touring Melbourne’s northern and western suburbs to support her party's candidates as they seek to secure the balance of power in the upper house. Meanwhile, Premier Ben Carroll has cautioned against the influence of the populist party, suggesting the Opposition Leader will regret aligning with their political agenda. This dispute highlights the shifting political landscape as the state transitions away from the long-term Labor administration.

For voters, the election represents a critical choice between the outgoing government and a Coalition platform that includes plans to slash net debt and cut annual interest repayments. The decision to privatize critical infrastructure remains a flashpoint, reflecting deeper concerns about budget management and the state's fiscal future. Labor's 12-year period in power, led successively by Daniel Andrews, Jacinta Allan, and Ben Carroll, is widely expected by pollsters to conclude once the final votes are tallied.

Both major parties have traded barbs over fiscal policy and transparency. Wilson has criticized One Nation for failing to submit their policies to the Department of Treasury and Finance or the Parliamentary Budget Office for independent scrutiny. One Nation, conversely, claims it will rebuild the state budget from scratch while opposing the Coalition’s infrastructure leasing strategy. Voting centers are scheduled to close at 6pm on Saturday, November 28, marking the conclusion of the campaign cycle and the start of the transition to a new parliament.
